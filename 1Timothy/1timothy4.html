1 Timothy 4

1. Immediate Context

The Spirit expressly says that in later times some will fall away from the faith, attending to seducing spirits and doctrines of demons. Timothy is to instruct the brothers in these things, refuse profane fables, exercise himself toward godliness, and not neglect the gift given through prophecy with the laying on of hands.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 But the Spirit says expressly that in later times some will fall away from the faith, paying attention to seducing spirits and doctrines of demons, 2 through the hypocrisy of men who speak lies, branded in their own conscience as with a hot iron, 3 forbidding marriage and commanding to abstain from foods which God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and know the truth. 4 For every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving. 5 For it is sanctified through the word of God and prayer.

6 If you instruct the brothers of these things, you will be a good servant of Christ Jesus, nourished in the words of the faith and of the good doctrine which you have followed. 7 But refuse profane and old wives’ fables. Exercise yourself toward godliness. 8 For bodily exercise has some value, but godliness has value in all things, having the promise of the life which is now and of that which is to come. 9 This saying is faithful and worthy of all acceptance. 10 For to this end we both labor and suffer reproach, because we have set our trust in the living God, who is the Savior of all men, especially of those who believe. 11 Command and teach these things.

12 Let no man despise your youth; but be an example to those who believe, in word, in your way of life, in love, in spirit, in faith, and in purity. 13 Until I come, pay attention to reading, to exhortation, and to teaching. 14 Don’t neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you by prophecy with the laying on of the hands of the elders. 15 Be diligent in these things. Give yourself wholly to them, that your progress may be revealed to all. 16 Pay attention to yourself and to your teaching. Continue in these things, for in doing this you will save both yourself and those who hear you.

Source: ebible.org

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • In later times (v. 1): The later times of the old covenant age — already present for Timothy.
  • Forbidding marriage and foods (v. 3): Ascetic restrictions that deny the goodness of what God created to be received with thanksgiving.
  • Promise of the life which is now and of that which is to come (v. 8): Godliness carries the promise of present and coming life.
  • Savior of all men, especially of those who believe (v. 10): The scope of salvation is universal in offer, particular in effect.

4. Annotations

  • Branded in their own conscience (v. 2): The false teachers’ consciences are seared.
  • Every creature of God is good (v. 4): Creation is not to be rejected; it is sanctified by the word and prayer.
  • Don’t neglect the gift (v. 14): The gift is already present through prophecy and the laying on of hands.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

Later Times and False Restrictions (v. 1–5)

The Spirit has already spoken expressly: in later times some will fall away, attending to deceiving spirits and doctrines of demons. Through the hypocrisy of liars with seared consciences they forbid marriage and command abstinence from foods. Yet every creature of God is good and is to be received with thanksgiving; it is sanctified through the word of God and prayer.

Exercise Toward Godliness (v. 6–11)

Timothy will be a good servant if he instructs the brothers in these things. He is to refuse profane fables and exercise himself toward godliness. Bodily exercise has limited value; godliness has value for all things, holding promise for the present life and the life to come. The living God is the Savior of all, especially of believers.

Example · Gift · Progress (v. 12–16)

Timothy is not to let anyone despise his youth but is to be an example in word, conduct, love, faith, and purity. He is to attend to public reading, exhortation, and teaching, and not neglect the gift already given. Diligence in these things will make his progress evident. Attention to himself and to the teaching will save both himself and his hearers.

8. New Creation Reality

The later times of the old order brought ascetic restrictions and seared consciences. Creation remains good. Godliness holds promise for the life that is now. We exercise ourselves toward the godliness that matches the finished work, without neglecting the gift already given.

9. Reflection

Where are you tempted to treat as forbidden what God has created to be received with thanksgiving, and how does the call to exercise toward godliness reorient that?
