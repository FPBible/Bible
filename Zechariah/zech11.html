Case File: Divine Council Proceedings // The Covenant Breach

Zechariah 11

The Severance of Contract and the Statutory Receipt

1. Judicial Context: The Mishpat of Severance

Zechariah 11 details the formal **Breach of Contract** between the Divine Council and the apostate nation. The prophet is ordered to execute a sign-act representing the Good Shepherd taking charge of a "flock of slaughter." The core of the chapter is a devastating legal transaction: the Old Covenant leadership evaluates the worth of God's administration at exactly thirty pieces of silver—the statutory price of a dead slave. In response, the Council orders this "receipt" to be thrown into the House (the Temple), legally closing the account. The two staffs, representing the Council's protective jurisdiction ("Favor") and the tribal brotherhood ("Union"), are officially broken, handing the apostate nation over to a "worthless shepherd" and the AD 70 destruction.

2. Scripture (WEB Technical)

4 Thus says Yahweh my God: "Shepherd the flock of slaughter. 5 Their buyers slaughter them, and go unpunished. Those who sell them say, 'Blessed be Yahweh, for I am rich;' and their own shepherds don’t pity them. 6 For I will no more pity the inhabitants of the land," says Yahweh; "but, behold, I will deliver the men everyone into his neighbor’s hand, and into the hand of his king. They will strike the land, and **I will not deliver them out of their hand**."
7 So I shepherded the flock of slaughter, especially the oppressed of the flock. I took for myself two staffs. The one I called "**Favor**," and the other I called "**Union**," and I fed the flock. 8 I cut off the three shepherds in one month; for my soul was weary of them, and their soul also loathed me. 9 Then I said, "I will not feed you. That which dies, let it die; and that which is to be cut off, let it be cut off; and let those who are left eat each other’s flesh."
10 I took my staff "**Favor**," and cut it apart, that I might **break my covenant** which I had made with all the peoples. 11 It was broken in that day; and thus the oppressed of the flock who saw me knew that it was the word of Yahweh.
12 I said to them, "If you think it best, give me my wages; and if not, keep them." So they weighed for my wages **thirty pieces of silver**. 13 Yahweh said to me, "**Throw it to the potter**, the handsome price that I was valued at by them!" I took the thirty pieces of silver, and threw them to the potter, in the house of Yahweh. 14 Then I cut apart my other staff, "**Union**," that I might **break the brotherhood** between Judah and Israel.
15 Yahweh said to me, "Take for yourself yet again the instruments of a foolish shepherd. 16 For, behold, I will raise up a shepherd in the land... 17 Woe to the worthless shepherd who leaves the flock! The sword will be on his arm, and on his right eye."

3. Fulfillment Notes: The Covenantal Receipt

  • Thirty Pieces of Silver (v. 12): The statutory fine in the Mosaic Law for a slave gored by an ox (Exodus 21:32). By paying this exact amount to Judas (Matt 26:15), the Sanhedrin legally declared the Messiah to be nothing more than a dead slave.
  • Thrown into the House (v. 13): The money was cast directly into the Temple by Judas, forcing the corrupt administration to use it to buy the Potter’s Field (the graveyard). This legally bound the Temple’s treasury to the price of innocent blood, sealing its AD 70 doom.
  • Breaking of Favor (v. 10): The withdrawal of the Divine Council's supernatural protection over the physical nation, leaving them vulnerable to the Roman armies.
  • Breaking of Union (v. 14): The legal dissolution of ethnic Israel's tribal brotherhood. This manifested literally in the brutal Jewish civil wars inside Jerusalem during the Roman siege (AD 66-70).

4. Annotations: Courtroom Terminology

Favor (No'am)

The legal grace/immunity granted by the Divine Council that previously prevented the surrounding empires from consuming Israel.

Union (Chobelim)

The binding contractual tie that held the tribes together as a single covenantal entity. Its breaking marks the end of genealogical Judaism's relevance in the Divine Council.

Worthless Shepherd (Ro'eh Ha-elil)

A "shepherd of nothingness." The judicial consequence of rejecting the Good Shepherd. The Council hands the nation over to tyrant leaders (Zealots/Herodians) who consume the flock from within.

5. Courtroom Commentary: The Final Settlement

Zechariah 11 is the formal termination of the Old Covenant contract. When the leaders of Israel weighed out exactly thirty pieces of silver, they were not just committing a betrayal; they were issuing a **legal receipt**. They appraised the administration of the Divine Council at the lowest possible statutory value under their own Law. By commanding the prophet to throw this blood money back into the "House of Yahweh," the Council officially rejected the payment and closed the account. The breaking of the two staffs represents the total withdrawal of heavenly jurisdiction over the physical nation. Left without "Favor" (protection) and "Union" (internal cohesion), the nation became a literal "flock of slaughter," perfectly fulfilling Jesus' Olivet Discourse regarding the catastrophic end of the age in AD 70.

8. New Creation Reality (Spiritual Instruction)

Standing: The old contract has been permanently severed. In the New Creation, your relationship with God is not mediated by a system whose "Union" and "Favor" were broken and discarded. The Old Covenant account was closed with the receipt of blood money. Application: Do not try to splice the broken staffs back together. The genealogical and ritual ties of the old world have no jurisdiction in the New Jerusalem. You belong to the Good Shepherd who paid the ultimate price to redeem you from the "flock of slaughter." Because you are under the New Covenant, the Divine Council's "Favor" toward you is secured eternally in Christ, and the "Union" you share is of the Spirit, unable to be broken by human failure.
