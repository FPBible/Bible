Connected

Why we’re linked to everyone and still feel alone

1. The gap you already know

You carry the whole world in your pocket. You can message almost anyone, almost anywhere, in seconds. And many of us still feel more alone than generations who had far less.

You also know the version of you that gets posted isn’t the whole one. There’s a gap between the person you want to be and the one who shows up at 2 a.m., or when someone cuts you off, or when a comment lands wrong.

You’re not unusual. Two thousand years ago a man wrote: “I don’t practice what I would like to do, but what I hate, that I do.”

Go deeper
Romans 7:15–24

2. Why trying harder doesn’t fix it

We’ve tried more information, better systems, therapy, wellness, discipline. Some of it helps. None of it reaches the root.

An old letter describes what we still see online and at home: “If you bite and devour one another, watch out that you aren’t consumed by one another.” It isn’t an insult. It’s a description, and it’s older than the internet.

Go deeper
Galatians 5:15, 19–21; Jeremiah 17:9; John 6:63

3. An old explanation

The oldest story we have says people were made from dust and given breath. Life wasn’t something we generated. It flowed from God, in connection. And we were made in his image — you were never worthless.

Then a voice suggested God couldn’t be trusted, and life would go better on our own terms. The connection broke. Since then, every one of us is born running on our own: capable, brilliant, and cut off.

Dust, running without breath.

Go deeper
Genesis 1:27; 2:7; 3:1–6; Ephesians 4:18; Romans 5:12; John 3:6

4. What Jesus did

Jesus didn’t bring a better rulebook. He said, “I came that they may have life, and may have it abundantly.”

He took that cut-off, self-run nature into his own death. “Our old man was crucified with him.” Then he rose, early on the first day of the week — a new beginning for humanity. That evening he breathed on his friends and said, “Receive the Holy Spirit.” It’s the same breath as the beginning. The connection was restored.

Go deeper
John 10:10; Romans 6:6; 8:3; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8, 45; John 20:19–22; 2 Corinthians 5:17.
For the Eighth Day: John 20:1, 26.

5. Available now, to anyone

You don’t have to earn this, and you don’t have to wait. “Now is the day of salvation.” “Whoever wishes, let him take the water of life freely.” There’s no résumé and no performance.

What grows in a connected life is the opposite of biting and devouring: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness. It isn’t forced. It’s fruit. And the point of it all is a relationship: knowing the Father, and being able to call him “Abba.”

Go deeper
2 Corinthians 6:2; Revelation 22:17; Romans 10:12; 8:2, 15–16; Galatians 5:22–23; John 17:3

An invitation

You don’t need special words. It might sound like this:

Father, I’ve tried to run life on my own, and I’m tired of the gap. I believe Jesus took that into death and rose. I receive your Life and your Spirit. Make your home in me.

Next step: read the Gospel of John, and talk to someone who knows the way.
