Is the “Second Coming” a Biblical Phrase?

A unified commentary for the Fulfilled Prophecy Bible

If you open a concordance and search for the exact phrase “Second Coming,” you will not find it. The New Testament never uses those words. What it does use is more precise, more covenantal, and more first-century than the later label suggests.

The Silence of the Phrase

The Holy Spirit never inspired the expression “Second Coming.” The nearest statement appears in Hebrews 9:28: Christ “will appear a second time.” That single verse has been stretched into an entire system of expectation that pictures Christ as currently absent, waiting to return in a biological body at some indefinite future point.

The New Testament writers were not primarily concerned with a distant “return.” They were concerned with a near manifestation of presence, authority, and covenantal change.

The Actual Vocabulary of the Apostles

Three Greek words dominate the discussion of Christ’s climax in the first century. None of them means “coming back after a long absence.”

Parousia — Presence

Parousia literally means “being alongside” or “presence.” In the first-century world it often described the official arrival of a king or high official to a city — the moment his authority became publicly operative. It is a word of presence and royal visitation, not of long-distance travel.

Apocalypsis — Unveiling

Apocalypsis means the pulling back of a curtain. It assumes something is already there but not yet fully seen. The term fits the idea of Christ being revealed as the true High Priest and King once the Old Covenant system that obscured him was removed.

Epiphaneia — Manifestation

Epiphaneia means a shining forth or appearing. It is the moment light is turned on so that what was already true becomes visible.

The High Priest Logic of Hebrews 9:28

Hebrews 9:28 is the only place in Scripture that speaks of Christ appearing “a second time.” The language is deliberately liturgical. It belongs to the Day of Atonement pattern:

  • The High Priest appears before the people to take the sacrifice.
  • He disappears behind the veil into the Holy of Holies to present the blood.
  • He appears a second time to the waiting people, showing that the sacrifice has been accepted.

Hebrews applies this pattern to Christ. His first appearance was to offer himself. His second appearance was the public confirmation that the sacrifice had been accepted and the old order was finished. That public confirmation took place when the physical sanctuary — the last remaining sign of the old system — was removed in AD 70.

“…so Christ also, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, without sin, to those who are eagerly waiting for him for salvation.”

The “eagerly waiting” generation was the transitional generation that lived between the cross and the fall of the Temple. Their waiting was not for a distant future event; it was for the completion of the atonement process already underway.

Shadow and Substance

As long as the Temple stood, the old system still cast its shadow. The way into the true Holy Place was not yet fully opened in public view (Hebrews 9:8). When the physical sanctuary fell, the High Priest’s second appearance was finalized in history. The first was taken away so that the second might be established (Hebrews 10:9).

The parousia is therefore not primarily about a physical body descending to a geographic location. It is the covenantal takeover — the public end of the old order and the full establishment of the new.

Why the Phrase Matters

When the church continues to speak of a future “Second Coming,” it unintentionally treats the atonement as unfinished and the High Priest as still behind the veil. The New Testament presents the opposite picture: the High Priest has appeared the second time, the old world has passed, and the unshakable kingdom has been received (Hebrews 12:28).

The language of Scripture points to presence already given, not to prolonged absence. The shift from “He is coming” to “He is here” is the shift from shadow to substance, from waiting to living in the finished work.

Conclusion

The phrase “Second Coming” is a later construct. Scripture speaks of presence, unveiling, and a second appearing that fits the High Priest pattern of the Day of Atonement. That appearing was realized in the first-century horizon when the old covenant order was brought to its appointed end. The result is not a church still waiting for its High Priest to emerge, but a people already living in the open presence of the New Creation.
