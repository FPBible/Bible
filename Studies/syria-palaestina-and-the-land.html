Syria Palaestina Is Nearly 2,000 Years Old: The Old Covenant Ended, There Is No Jew or Gentile, and the Land Was Never a Bloodline Prize

The name Syria Palaestina has marked this region for almost 1,900 years, since the Emperor Hadrian imposed it after crushing the Bar Kokhba revolt around AD 135. That is documented imperial history, not a modern invention. But the decisive event came sixty-five years earlier, and it was not a Roman one. It was the close of an age.

1. A covenant with sanctions

Israel did not receive the land as an unconditional deed. It received it under a covenant, and covenants in the ancient world came with terms, witnesses, and sanctions. Deuteronomy 28 is the sanctions section. Obey, and there is blessing: rain, harvest, fertility, safety from enemies. Break the covenant, and every blessing reverses into curse: failed crops, disease, defeat, siege, and finally exile (Deut 28:15-68).

  • The curse section is about six times longer than the blessing section. Moses was not being decorative. He was warning.
  • The curse is tied to the land. The worst outcome was not poverty but removal: “You shall be plucked off the land” and “the Lord will scatter you among all peoples” (Deut 28:63-64).
  • The land itself was never Israel’s to own outright. “The land is mine. For you are strangers and sojourners with me” (Lev 25:23).

So the land came as a covenant gift, held on covenant conditions. The Babylonian exile was the curse landing once already. When the exiles returned, Nehemiah’s prayer shows they did not feel the curse had lifted: “Behold, we are slaves this day... in the land that you gave to our fathers” (Neh 9:36). Israel remained under the covenant’s shadow, awaiting its resolution.
