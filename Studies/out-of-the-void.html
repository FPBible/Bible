Out of the Void

The Human Spirit and the New Birth in the Post-AD 70 New Creation

One of the most significant shifts in Christian theology occurs when Scripture is read through the lens of audience relevance and covenantal fulfillment. When we recognize that the destruction of Jerusalem and the temple in AD 70 marked the decisive end of the Old Covenant age, our understanding of the present human condition changes in fundamental ways.

A common challenge when embracing fulfilled eschatology is re-examining the mechanics of salvation. If the cross and the events of AD 70 together terminated the old system of condemnation, what is the default spiritual state of a person born today? Are we still born “dead in Adam” in the same legal and covenantal sense? And if the old framework has been removed, why do human beings still possess an inner, non-physical reality—a spirit—that hungers for meaning, morality, and relationship?

The answer lies in moving from a primarily biological or ontological framework to a consistently covenantal one. In the post-AD 70 New Creation, the New Birth is not best understood as a rescue operation out of an active prison of death. It is an act of creation out of nothing—a transition out of a state lacking covenantal union and into eternal life.

1. The Legal Demolition of AD 70

In Pauline theology, sin and death were explicitly tied to a legal and covenantal framework. As Paul writes, “the strength of sin is the law” (1 Corinthians 15:56). The Mosaic Law did not merely reveal sin; it actively ministered condemnation and amplified transgression.

When the temple fell in AD 70, it was not only a historical catastrophe for the Jewish nation. It represented the cosmic termination of the old covenantal system of condemnation itself. The entire legal mechanism that had bound humanity to the corporate sentence of Adamic death was permanently dissolved. As the writer of Hebrews noted, that which was growing old and aging was “ready to vanish away” (Hebrews 8:13). With the destruction of the temple system, the outer framework administering that corporate death sentence was removed, proving that the ministration of death engraved on stones had finally given way to the permanent glory of the Spirit (2 Corinthians 3:7-11). The courtroom of the old order has been adjourned; the judge has left the bench; the chains of that former administration have been broken.

2. The Baseline Condition: A Covenantal Void

Because the active ministry of condemnation has ended, a person born in the present era does not enter the world bearing a covenantal identity of death. Instead, they enter with no covenantal identity at all. Apart from faith, a natural human being is not an active enemy under an ancient legal sentence; they are a creature of the dust existing in a spiritual and relational zero—a covenantal void.

This does not mean humans are morally neutral or without personal sin. It means they possess no covenantal standing or relational life with God by birth. The old legal system that once defined humanity’s position before God has been removed, yet separation from divine life remains until that relationship is established.

At the same time, every human being is born with a God-given human spirit. Scripture states that the Lord “forms the spirit of man within him” (Zechariah 12:1). This spirit is the seat of consciousness, conscience, intellect, and the capacity for meaning and relationship. It explains the universal human longing for transcendence, morality, and purpose.

However, possessing a natural human spirit is not the same as possessing covenantal life. They possess the capacity for God. This capacity is not itself spiritual life; it is the God-given faculty—the human spirit—through which the Holy Spirit awakens a person and brings them into living covenantal union with Christ. In the post-AD 70 world, an unbeliever has a functional human spirit that remains unawakened to God. They have the capacity for God (the spirit of man) but lack vital connection to God (the Spirit of Christ). They possess a lamp, yet it sits unlit in a covenantal vacuum.

3. New Birth as Creation Ex Nihilo

When the natural state is understood as a relational and covenantal void, the New Birth emerges as a profound act of creation out of nothing (ex nihilo). This is precisely the imagery Paul employs:

“For it is the God who commanded light to shine out of darkness, who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” (2 Corinthians 4:6)

In Genesis 1, the primordial darkness and void were not actively opposing God; they simply lacked light and form. God did not repair the darkness—He spoke light into existence where none had been. Likewise, the moment of faith is the moment God speaks light into a covenantal vacuum. The New Birth does not repair an old, broken relationship with Adam; it brings into being an entirely new life. As Proverbs 20:27 affirms, “The spirit of man is the lamp of the Lord.” The New Birth is the moment the divine Light enters and illuminates that natural lamp.

Admittedly, because the New Testament documents were written during the transition period before AD 70, Scripture does not explicitly detail the mechanics of human spiritual texturing *after* the close of the apostolic age. However, this conclusion is the necessary theological inference of a finished redemption. If the corporate "Body of Death" tied to the Old Covenant structure was dissolved and cast into the lake of fire at the close of the age (Revelation 20:14), then Adamic condemnation no longer operates as a reigning corporate legal position. Humanity today is not born into a doomed covenantal house that no longer exists; rather, they are born outside the gates of the City. They are not born condemned by a defunct covenant, but they remain alienated from life until they heed the invitation to enter the open gates of the New Jerusalem (Revelation 22:17).

4. The Mechanics of Adoption: Bypassing the Past

This covenantal framework illuminates the New Testament’s strong emphasis on adoption. In the Roman world, adoption was a radical legal act. An individual outside a wealthy household—whether an orphan or a member of another family—possessed no legal rights, no inheritance, and no standing within that household. To the paterfamilias, such a person possessed no legal standing or claim to inheritance within that household.

At the moment the adoption decree was finalized:

  • The individual’s prior identity (or lack of identity) was completely erased.
  • Previous debts and legal liabilities were extinguished.
  • A new name, a new lineage, and immediate access to the father’s inheritance were granted.

Because the old house of Moses and the corporate Adamic order were terminated in the first century, the believer is not moving from “slave in Moses’ house” to “son in Christ’s house.” They are moving from stranger to son. The past is not improved; it is bypassed entirely.

Conclusion: The Open Gates

The glory of the post-AD 70 reality is that access to the New Adam has been cleared of every obstruction. The fiery sword that once guarded the tree of life has been removed. The gates of the New Jerusalem stand open, and the invitation sounds across a world living in covenantal void: “Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely” (Revelation 22:17).

We no longer look back to Adam as our federal head, nor do we answer to Moses as our covenant administrator. By faith, the Holy Spirit applies the finished work of Christ to the individual, uniting with their human spirit so that they become “one spirit with Him” (1 Corinthians 6:17). The believer steps out of the wilderness of covenantal alienation and directly into the vibrant, eternal reality of the New Creation.
