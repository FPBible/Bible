The Serpent Nature: Carnal Sin Nature in Man

Locked theology for the Fulfilled Prophecy Bible

The serpent is the carnal nature — the adversarial principle of the flesh within man.

Genesis does not introduce a separate personal cosmic being. It introduces the serpent as “more crafty than any other beast of the field that the LORD God had made” (Gen 3:1). The dialogue is the internal questioning of God’s word: “Did God really say?” followed by the desire of the eyes, the desire of the flesh, and the pride of life (Gen 3:6; cf. 1 John 2:16). This is the origin point of the carnal mind.

The New Testament Identifies the Same Adversary

The New Testament identifies the same adversary:

  • “The carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be” (Rom 8:7).
  • “The flesh lusts against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh” (Gal 5:17).
  • Temptation arises from within: “Each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own desires and enticed. Then, when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin” (James 1:14-15).
  • Evil proceeds from the heart (Mark 7:21-23).

Revelation later names this ancient adversarial principle: “that serpent of old, called the Devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world” (Rev 12:9; 20:2). The identification is canonical, but the substance is the same: the deceiver that operates through the flesh and the old Adamic order.

The Curse and the Seed

The serpent is cursed to go on its belly and eat dust (Gen 3:14) — earthbound, feeding on mortality and the dust of the flesh. Enmity is placed between the serpent’s seed and the woman’s seed (Gen 3:15). The seed of the serpent is those who walk according to the flesh; the seed of the woman is Christ and those in Him. The head is crushed at the cross (Heb 2:14; Col 2:15). The heel strike is the suffering of the cross; the decisive blow is the destruction of the adversarial power.

In the New Creation

The old man was crucified with Christ (Rom 6:6). The law of sin and death that operated in the members (Rom 7) was dealt with in the body of Christ (Rom 8:3). The accuser’s standing ended when the Old Covenant system that magnified sin and provided the ground of accusation was judged and removed (the transition completed in 70 AD). In the Eighth Day reality there is one new man in Christ. The serpent nature no longer has dominion over those who are in Him (Rom 6:14). We walk by the Spirit and do not fulfill the lust of the flesh (Gal 5:16).

The adversary is not an external personal agent still roaming and accusing. It is the carnal sin nature — the flesh — that was judged at the cross and whose power ended with the Old Covenant. What remains is the New Creation.

Contrast with Gnostic Inversions

Certain second- and third-century Gnostic systems (Ophites, Naassenes, and related texts in the Nag Hammadi library) inverted the narrative. They treated the God of Genesis as a jealous Demiurge and the serpent as a liberator who brought saving gnosis. Texts such as the Hypostasis of the Archons and the Testimony of Truth present the serpent as an instructor or even identify it with Christ. This is a systematic reversal of the biblical account. Scripture presents the serpent principle as the source of deception and hostility to God, not as the bringer of life. The head is crushed, not enthroned. The New Creation stands free of that principle.

Conclusion

The serpent nature is the carnal sin nature in man. It is the adversarial principle of the flesh, judged at the cross, whose covenantal vehicle ended in AD 70. Those in Christ walk free of its dominion in the New Creation.
