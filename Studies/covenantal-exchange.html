Covenantal Shift

The Great Covenantal Exchange

We do not strive to become holy or righteous; we already ARE these things because we abide in Christ.

In 1 Corinthians 1:30, the Apostle Paul gives us a profound summary of our identity in the New Creation. He tells us that Christ became for us Wisdom, Righteousness, Sanctification, and Redemption. Within the fulfilled reality of the Eighth Day, these aren't just abstract religious concepts—they are the legal and spiritual realities of the New Covenant age. When the Old Covenant shadows passed away in AD 70, Jesus didn’t just give us these things; He replaced the entire old system with Himself.

To try to earn these states through human effort is to look for life in an obsolete house. The Good News is that the system of external management has been completely exchanged for a structural, finished reality in Christ.

The Fourfold Exchange

1. Wisdom

Under the Old Covenant, wisdom was found in tracking the external letter of the Law, following external laws, and managing behavior via rules. In the New Covenant, the arrangement is altered entirely. We no longer look to carved stones to find the mind of God. Christ is the logic and the master plan of the New Creation, offering us the internal mind and direct direction of the Spirit.

2. Righteousness

The old world was defined by an ongoing sacrificial system and a constant payment of debt that never settled the ledger. Today, we are no longer burdened by debt-management. Because the old covenantal ledger was completely removed in the fires of AD 70, our legal standing is perfect. We have perfect standing over debt. We are as righteous as Christ is because He is our head.

3. Sanctification

The religious mind believes holiness is tied to spaces, specific rituals, physical buildings, or titles. But the structural reality of the New Creation changes the architecture: we aren’t made holy by external rituals or by standing in a physical Temple. You are it indwelling; you are the living temple, set apart and permanently filled by His Spirit.

4. Redemption

The Old Covenant house was a prison where a ransom was always waiting to be paid. The first-century saints eagerly waited for the final deliverance from that covenantal death-state, and at the consummation of the age, that rescue mission was completed. The ransom is a fully paid rescue mission. The prison doors are gone, and we are free citizens of the New Jerusalem.

Responsibility deepens when it is rooted in identity freely received. It becomes a shallow performance when it is rooted in trying to build a ladder that Christ already demolished.

The Eighth Day Reality

We don't strive to become wise, righteous, holy, or redeemed. We simply abide in the fact that we already are all of these things by virtue of our union with Him. The courtroom of the Old Covenant is permanently closed, and the King is on His throne!

For the person caught on the performance treadmill, this realization is total release. You are not on probation, you are not being measured against a fading shadow, and you are not awaiting a future validation. The exchange is complete.

A Question to Carry Into Your Week

How does resting in this "finished work" change the way you walk through your week? If you are already fully righteous, holy, and redeemed in Him right now, what happens to the anxiety of religious performance?
