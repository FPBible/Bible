The Manifesto

1. Fulfillment, Not Expectation

We affirm that the “end of the age” spoken of by Jesus and the prophets was not the end of the physical universe, but the completion of Israel’s covenant age. That age reached its appointed conclusion in the first century, climaxing in the judgment of Jerusalem and the passing of the temple system.

This was not a failed expectation or a postponed promise. It was fulfillment. The old covenant world has ended. A new covenant world has begun.

2. The Presence of Christ

The New Testament uses parousia for presence — an abiding, royal nearness. Christ has taken His throne. Christian life is lived in awareness of His nearness, not in anticipation of a delayed arrival.

3. Life in the New Heavens and New Earth

The passing of the “former heaven and earth” is the removal of the old covenant order — temple, priesthood, ethnic boundary. We live in the New Heavens and New Earth: access without place, ritual, or lineage. The veil is gone.

4. The New Jerusalem

The New Jerusalem is the living community of God’s people — a spiritual city, founded in heaven, expressed on earth. Its gates are open. To belong to Christ is to belong to this city now.

5. Resurrection and Life

The gospel proclaims victory over Death — the covenantal enemy that reigned from Adam. That Death was never biology. It was exile from the Garden, condemnation under the Law, Sheol as the holding of the old world. Grave and Hades were emptied and cast into the lake of fire.

Jesus said to Martha, “Whoever lives and believes in me will never die.” Resurrection life is the present inheritance of those who live in Him. Those in Christ do not wait in Sheol. Eternal life is present participation in the Last Adam — the celestial order, the image of the Man from heaven.

Full study: The Covenantal Resurrection (Corporate Body View) · 1 Corinthians 15 · Covenant Death

6. God All in All

With Israel’s covenant mission fulfilled, God’s nearness is no longer confined to a nation or a geography. Worship is not bound to temples. Holiness is not confined to rituals. God is all in all.

A Living Analogy

The lantern was for the night. The prophets spoke of a sunrise that would make it unnecessary. That sunrise has come. We are learning how to live in the day.

Closing Statement

The New Creation is not an idea to defend. It is a reality to inhabit.

We do not wait for Christ. We walk with Him.
