2 Timothy 2

1. Immediate Context

Paul continues his charge to Timothy: be strengthened in the grace that is in Christ Jesus, entrust the teaching to faithful men, endure hardship as a good soldier, and remember Jesus Christ risen from the dead. Some have erred concerning the truth, saying the resurrection is already past. Yet God’s firm foundation stands.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 You therefore, my child, be strengthened in the grace that is in Christ Jesus. 2 The things which you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit the same things to faithful men who will be able to teach others also. 3 You therefore must endure hardship as a good soldier of Christ Jesus. 4 No soldier on duty entangles himself in the affairs of life, that he may please him who enrolled him as a soldier. 5 Also, if anyone competes in athletics, he isn’t crowned unless he has competed by the rules. 6 The farmer who labors must be the first to get a share of the crops. 7 Consider what I say, and may the Lord give you understanding in all things.

8 Remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, of the offspring of David, according to my Good News, 9 in which I suffer hardship to the point of chains as a criminal. But God’s word isn’t chained. 10 Therefore I endure all things for the chosen ones’ sake, that they also may obtain the salvation which is in Christ Jesus with eternal glory. 11 This saying is trustworthy: “For if we died with him, we will also live with him. 12 If we endure, we will also reign with him. If we deny him, he also will deny us. 13 If we are faithless, he remains faithful; for he can’t deny himself.”

14 Remind them of these things, charging them in the sight of the Lord that they don’t argue about words to no profit, to the subverting of those who hear. 15 Give diligence to present yourself approved by God, a workman who doesn’t need to be ashamed, properly handling the Word of Truth. 16 But shun empty chatter, for it will go further in ungodliness, 17 and those words will consume like gangrene, of whom is Hymenaeus and Philetus: 18 men who have erred concerning the truth, saying that the resurrection is already past, and overthrowing the faith of some. 19 However, God’s firm foundation stands, having this seal: “The Lord knows those who are his,” and, “Let every one who names the name of the Lord depart from unrighteousness.”

20 Now in a large house there are not only vessels of gold and of silver, but also of wood and of clay. Some are for honor and some for dishonor. 21 If anyone therefore purges himself from these, he will be a vessel for honor, sanctified, and suitable for the master’s use, prepared for every good work.

22 Flee from youthful lusts; but pursue righteousness, faith, love, and peace with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart. 23 But refuse foolish and ignorant questionings, knowing that they generate strife. 24 The Lord’s servant must not quarrel, but be gentle toward all, able to teach, patient, 25 in gentleness correcting those who oppose him. Perhaps God may give them repentance leading to a full knowledge of the truth, 26 and they may recover themselves out of the devil’s snare, having been taken captive by him to do his will.

Source: ebible.org

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Grace that is in Christ Jesus (v. 1): Strengthening is in the grace already present in Christ.
  • Jesus Christ, risen from the dead (v. 8): The resurrection is the settled centre of the Good News Paul preaches.
  • Saying the resurrection is already past (v. 18): A present error that overthrows faith; the true resurrection hope remains tied to the appearing.
  • God’s firm foundation stands (v. 19): Even amid error and departure, the foundation with its seal remains unmoved.

4. Annotations

  • Good soldier (v. 3–4): Entanglement in civilian affairs hinders the one who aims to please the one who enlisted him.
  • Hymenaeus and Philetus (v. 17–18): Named examples of those whose empty talk spreads like gangrene and overthrows faith.
  • Vessel for honor (v. 20–21): Purging oneself from dishonorable associations prepares one for the Master’s use.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

Strengthened in Grace · Entrust to Faithful Men (v. 1–7)

Timothy is to be strengthened in the grace already in Christ Jesus. The teaching received is to be committed to faithful men who will teach others. Endurance as a soldier, athlete, and farmer illustrates the focused, rule-keeping, laboring life required. The Lord gives understanding.

Remember Jesus Christ Risen (v. 8–13)

The centre is Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, of David’s line, according to the Good News. Paul’s chains do not chain the word. He endures for the sake of the chosen ones. The trustworthy saying holds both promise and warning: dying with him means living with him; enduring means reigning; denying brings denial; yet he remains faithful even if we are faithless, for he cannot deny himself.

The Firm Foundation Amid Error (v. 14–19)

Empty arguments subvert hearers. Timothy is to handle the Word of Truth accurately and shun empty chatter that spreads like gangrene. Hymenaeus and Philetus have erred by saying the resurrection is already past. Yet God’s firm foundation stands with its double seal: the Lord knows those who are his, and those who name the Lord must depart from unrighteousness.

Vessels and the Lord’s Servant (v. 20–26)

In a large house there are vessels for honor and dishonor. Purging oneself prepares a vessel for the Master’s use. The Lord’s servant flees youthful lusts, pursues righteousness, faith, love, and peace, refuses foolish questions, and corrects opponents with gentleness, hoping for their repentance and recovery from the snare.
