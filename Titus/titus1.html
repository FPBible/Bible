Titus 1

1. Immediate Context

Paul writes to Titus, left in Crete to complete the unfinished work of establishing the churches. The island is troubled by many unruly talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision. Titus must appoint elders who can both exhort in sound doctrine and convict those who contradict it. The letter opens with the settled foundation: the faith of God’s chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth that accords with godliness.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 Paul, a servant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the faith of God’s chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth which is according to godliness, 2 in hope of eternal life, which God, who can’t lie, promised before time began; 3 but in his own time revealed his word in the message with which I was entrusted according to the commandment of God our Savior, 4 to Titus, my true child according to a common faith: Grace, mercy, and peace from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ our Savior.

5 I left you in Crete for this reason, that you would set in order the things that were lacking and appoint elders in every city, as I directed you— 6 if anyone is blameless, the husband of one wife, having children who believe, who are not accused of loose or unruly behavior. 7 For the overseer must be blameless, as God’s steward, not self-pleasing, not easily angered, not given to wine, not violent, not greedy for dishonest gain; 8 but given to hospitality, a lover of good, sober minded, fair, holy, self-controlled, 9 holding to the faithful word which is according to the teaching, that he may be able to exhort in the sound doctrine, and to convict those who contradict him.

10 For there are also many unruly men, vain talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision, 11 whose mouths must be stopped: men who overthrow whole houses, teaching things which they ought not, for dishonest gain’s sake. 12 One of them, a prophet of their own, said, “Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, and idle gluttons.” 13 This testimony is true. For this cause, reprove them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith, 14 not paying attention to Jewish fables and commandments of men who turn away from the truth. 15 To the pure, all things are pure, but to those who are defiled and unbelieving, nothing is pure; but both their mind and their conscience are defiled. 16 They profess that they know God, but by their deeds they deny him, being abominable, disobedient, and unfit for any good work.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Promised before time began (v. 2): Eternal life was already promised; its revelation has come in the message entrusted to the apostles.
  • In his own time revealed (v. 3): The word has been manifested in the present age through the apostolic proclamation.
  • Those of the circumcision (v. 10): The primary source of disruption is the same Judaizing pressure that appears throughout the transition generation.
  • Jewish fables and commandments of men (v. 14): Turning from the truth to human traditions and myths is the characteristic danger of the closing period of the old order.

4. Annotations

  • True child according to a common faith (v. 4): Titus shares the same faith that unites Jew and Gentile in the one new man.
  • God’s steward (v. 7): The overseer manages what belongs to God, not his own agenda.
  • Mouths must be stopped (v. 11): The elders are not merely positive teachers; they must actively silence those who overturn households.
  • Profess that they know God (v. 16): Verbal profession without corresponding deeds is empty and destructive.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

The Settled Foundation (v. 1–4)

Paul begins with the faith of God’s chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth that accords with godliness. Eternal life was promised before time began and has now been revealed in the message entrusted to the apostles. Titus stands inside that revealed reality. Grace, mercy, and peace flow from the Father and the Son who is already Savior.

Qualified Elders (v. 5–9)

The practical task is clear: appoint elders in every city. The qualifications are character-first — blameless, hospitable, self-controlled, holding the faithful word. These men must be able both to exhort in sound doctrine and to convict those who contradict it. The churches cannot be left unprotected while deceivers are active.

The Deceivers of the Circumcision (v. 10–16)

The primary threat is identified: unruly men, vain talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision. They overthrow whole houses for dishonest gain. They turn people to Jewish fables and the commandments of men. Their profession of knowing God is contradicted by their deeds. Titus is to reprove them sharply so that the people may be sound in the faith. The old order’s pressure is still active; the churches must be guarded against it.

8. New Creation Reality

The truth has been revealed. The faith is common. Elders who hold the faithful word protect the household of God. We do not wait for a future purification of teaching; we live inside the sound doctrine already given and refuse the fables that once bound the old order.

9. Reflection
