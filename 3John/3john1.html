3 John 1

1. Immediate Context

The elder writes a warm personal letter to Gaius, a man already walking in truth and showing practical love to travelling brothers. At the same time he names the opposing spirit of Diotrephes, who loves to be first, rejects the elder’s authority, refuses the brothers, and casts out those who would receive them. The letter is a clear call to imitate what is good and to become fellow workers for the truth.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 The elder to Gaius the beloved, whom I love in truth.

2 Beloved, I pray that you may prosper in all things and be healthy, even as your soul prospers. 3 For I rejoiced greatly when brothers came and testified about your truth, even as you walk in truth. 4 I have no greater joy than this: to hear about my children walking in truth.

5 Beloved, you do a faithful work in whatever you accomplish for those who are brothers and strangers. 6 They have testified about your love before the assembly. You will do well to send them forward on their journey in a way worthy of God, 7 because for the sake of the Name they went out, taking nothing from the Gentiles. 8 We therefore ought to receive such, that we may be fellow workers for the truth.

9 I wrote to the assembly, but Diotrephes, who loves to be first amongst them, doesn’t accept what we say. 10 Therefore, if I come, I will call attention to his deeds which he does, unjustly accusing us with wicked words. Not content with this, he doesn’t receive the brothers himself, and those who would, he forbids and throws out of the assembly.

11 Beloved, don’t imitate that which is evil, but that which is good. He who does good is of God. He who does evil hasn’t seen God. 12 Demetrius has the testimony of all, and of the truth itself; yes, we also testify, and you know that our testimony is true.

13 I had many things to write to you, but I am unwilling to write to you with ink and pen; 14 but I hope to see you soon. Then we will speak face to face. Peace be to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends by name.

Source: ebible.org

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Walking in truth (v. 3–4): The elder’s greatest joy is hearing that his children walk in the truth already given.
  • Fellow workers for the truth (v. 8): Supporting those who go out for the Name makes the supporters partners in the work of the truth.
  • Loves to be first (v. 9): Self-promotion that rejects the brothers stands in direct opposition to the life of the New Covenant community.
  • Imitate what is good (v. 11): The one who does good is of God; the one who does evil has not seen God.

4. Annotations

  • Gaius (v. 1): A beloved brother already known for walking in truth and practical hospitality.
  • For the sake of the Name (v. 7): The missionaries went out depending on the Name, taking nothing from the Gentiles.
  • Diotrephes (v. 9–10): A local leader whose love of preeminence leads him to reject the elder, refuse the brothers, and expel those who would receive them.
  • Demetrius (v. 12): A brother with good testimony from all and from the truth itself.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

Joy in Walking Truth (v. 1–4)

The elder’s greatest joy is not new revelation or expanding influence. It is hearing that his children walk in the truth. Truth is already present. The life that brings joy is the life that continues in what has been received. Prosperity of soul is the measure; the prayer is that outward life may match the inward reality already at work.

Fellow Workers for the Truth (v. 5–8)

Gaius has been faithful in supporting brothers and strangers who go out for the sake of the Name. They take nothing from the Gentiles. Those who receive and send them forward become fellow workers for the truth. In the New Covenant the work is shared. Hospitality and support are not optional extras; they are participation in the truth itself.

The Opposite Spirit (v. 9–10)

Diotrephes loves to be first. He rejects the elder’s word, refuses the brothers, and throws out of the assembly those who would receive them. This is the spirit of self-promotion that cannot coexist with the life of the one new man. The elder will call attention to these deeds if he comes. The contrast is deliberate and sharp.

Imitate What Is Good (v. 11–12)

The simple command is given: do not imitate what is evil, but what is good. The one who does good is of God. The one who does evil has not seen God. Demetrius stands as a living example with the testimony of all and of the truth itself. The elder adds his own testimony. The path is clear.

Face to Face (v. 13–14)

As in 2 John, the elder prefers presence over paper. He hopes to speak face to face. The letter closes with ordinary greetings among friends. Even while confronting serious opposition, the ordinary life of mutual greeting and peace continues.

8. New Creation Reality

Walking in the truth already given, supporting those who go out for the Name, and refusing self-seeking control are the ordinary marks of life in the finished work of Christ. We do not wait for a future community free of these tensions. We live now as fellow workers for the truth.

9. Reflection

Where is the Spirit calling you to move from spectator to fellow worker — and where might self-promotion or control still resist that call?
