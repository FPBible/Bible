John 12 — The Hour Has Come

Immediate Context

Six days before Passover. Mary anoints. Judas objects. Crowds come because of Lazarus. Greeks seek Jesus through Philip. The hour that has been approaching now arrives. A grain of wheat must fall. The ruler of this world is judged and cast out. The light is among them a little while longer. Many of the rulers believe but will not confess for fear of the synagogue. Isaiah’s blindness oracle is recalled. The chapter closes the public ministry and turns toward the upper room.

Scripture (WEB)

23Jesus answered them, “The time has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.” 24“Most certainly I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains by itself alone. But if it dies, it bears much fruit.” 31“Now is the judgment of this world. Now the prince of this world will be cast out.” 32“And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself.” 35Jesus therefore said to them, “Yet a little while the light is with you. Walk while you have the light, that darkness doesn’t overtake you.” 46“I have come as a light into the world, that whoever believes in me may not remain in the darkness.”

Fulfillment Notes

1) The hour is not postponed; it arrives within the narrative timeline of that generation.

2) The grain of wheat must die to bear fruit — the cross is the path to the much fruit.

3) “Now” the ruler of this world is judged and cast out — present tense judgment language.

4) Lifted up draws all peoples; the Greeks’ request signals the widening scope.

5) Light is temporary among them; walking in it is urgent, not optional delay.

Annotations

the hour: the appointed time of glorification through the cross.
grain of wheat: death as the necessary condition for multiplication.
prince of this world: the accusing power tied to the old order, judged now.
lifted up: cross and exaltation in one movement.
a little while: urgency of the remaining light before the decisive transition.

Fulfillment Commentary

1) The Hour Arrives

Throughout the Gospel the hour has been “not yet.” The request of the Greeks becomes the trigger. The scope is no longer only the lost sheep of the house of Israel; the nations are already seeking. The hour of glorification is the hour of the cross.

2) Death That Bears Fruit

A grain that refuses to fall remains alone. The Son’s death is not defeat but the condition for the much fruit of the New Covenant people. Self-preservation would leave the mission sterile. The path through death is the path to life for many.

3) Now the Ruler Is Cast Out

The language is present and decisive. The prince of this world — the accusing power that held the old order in its grip — is judged and expelled in the lifting up of the Son. The judgment of the world is not postponed to a remote future; it is enacted in the cross and its aftermath within that generation.

4) Light While It Remains

The light is among them a little while longer. Walking in it is the only way darkness does not overtake. Belief that remains secret for fear of the synagogue is exposed as incomplete. Isaiah’s oracle of blindness is fulfilled in those who will not see the glory that has come.

New Creation Reality

The hour has come. The grain has fallen. The ruler has been judged. You are not waiting for a future casting-out of the accusing power; it has already been accomplished in the lifting up of the Son. Walk in the light that remains.

Reflection

Where are you still treating the decisive hour as future when the narrative presents it as arrived?
