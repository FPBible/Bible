John 12 — The Hour Has Come

Immediate Context

Six days before the Passover, Jesus returns to Bethany where He raised Lazarus, receiving a royal anointing from Mary that foreshadows His burial. As Jesus enters Jerusalem riding a donkey's colt, the crowds hail Him as the King of Israel, prompting the Pharisees to lament that "the world has gone after him." The arrival of Gentile Greeks seeking an audience with Jesus signals that the appointed hour for Messiah's passion and global glorification has officially arrived. Concluding His public ministry to first-century Israel, Jesus proclaims the impending judgment of the old world order, the casting out of its ruling accuser, and the inevitable judicial blindness coming upon those who refuse the light before the temple's destruction in AD 70.

Scripture (WEB)

1Then six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was, who had been dead, whom he raised from the dead. 2So they made him a supper there. Martha served, but Lazarus was one of those who sat at the table with him. 3Mary, therefore, took a pound of ointment of pure nard, very precious, and anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped his feet with her hair. The house was filled with the fragrance of the ointment. 4Then Judas Iscariot, Simon’s son, one of his disciples, who would betray him, said, 5“Why wasn’t this ointment sold for three hundred denarii, and given to the poor?”

6Now he said this, not because he cared for the poor, but because he was a thief, and having the money box, used to steal what was put into it. 7But Jesus said, “Leave her alone. She has kept this for the day of my burial. 8For you always have the poor with you, but you don’t always have me.” 9A large crowd therefore of the Jews learned that he was there, and they came, not for Jesus’ sake only, but that they might see Lazarus also, whom he had raised from the dead. 10But the chief priests conspired to put Lazarus to death also,

11because on account of him many of the Jews went away and believed in Jesus. 12On the next day a great multitude had come to the feast. When they heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem, 13they took the branches of the palm trees, and went out to meet him, and cried out, “Hosanna! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, the King of Israel!” 14Jesus, having found a young donkey, sat on it. As it is written, 15“Don’t be afraid, daughter of Zion. Behold, your King comes, sitting on a donkey’s colt.”

16His disciples didn’t understand these things at first, but when Jesus was glorified, then they remembered that these things were written about him, and that they had done these things to him. 17The multitude therefore that was with him when he called Lazarus out of the tomb, and raised him from the dead, was testifying about it. 18For this cause also the multitude went and met him, because they heard that he had done this sign. 19The Pharisees therefore said among themselves, “See how you accomplish nothing. Behold, the world has gone after him.” 20Now there were certain Greeks among those that went up to worship at the feast.

21These, therefore, came to Philip, who was from Bethsaida of Galilee, and asked him, saying, “Sir, we want to see Jesus.” 22Philip came and told Andrew, and in turn, Andrew came with Philip, and they told Jesus. 23Jesus answered them, “The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified. 24Most certainly I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains by itself alone. But if it dies, it bears much fruit. 25He who loves his life will lose it. He who hates his life in this world will keep it to eternal life.

26If anyone serves me, let him follow me. Where I am, there will my servant also be. If anyone serves me, the Father will honor him. 27“Now my soul is troubled. What shall I say? ‘Father, save me from this time?’ But for this cause I came to this time. 28Father, glorify your name!” Then there came a voice out of the sky, saying, “I have both glorified it, and will glorify it again.” 29The multitude therefore, who stood by and heard it, said that it had thundered. Others said, “An angel has spoken to him.” 30Jesus answered, “This voice hasn’t come for my sake, but for your sakes.

31Now is the judgment of this world. Now the prince of this world will be cast out. 32And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself.” 33But he said this, signifying by what kind of death he should die. 34The multitude answered him, “We have heard out of the law that the Christ remains forever. How do you say, ‘The Son of Man must be lifted up?’ Who is this Son of Man?” 35Jesus therefore said to them, “Yet a little while the light is with you. Walk while you have the light, that darkness doesn’t overtake you. He who walks in the darkness doesn’t know where he is going.

36While you have the light, believe in the light, that you may become children of light.” Jesus said these things, and he departed and hid himself from them. 37But though he had done so many signs before them, yet they didn’t believe in him, 38that the word of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled, which he spoke, “Lord, who has believed our report? To whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?” 39For this cause they couldn’t believe, for Isaiah said again, 40“He has blinded their eyes and he hardened their heart, lest they should see with their eyes, and perceive with their heart, and would turn, and I would heal them.”

41Isaiah said these things when he saw his glory, and spoke of him. 42Nevertheless even of the rulers many believed in him, but because of the Pharisees they didn’t confess it, so that they wouldn’t be put out of the synagogue, 43for they loved men’s praise more than God’s praise. 44Jesus cried out and said, “Whoever believes in me, believes not in me, but in him who sent me. 45He who sees me sees him who sent me.

46I have come as a light into the world, that whoever believes in me may not remain in the darkness. 47If anyone listens to my sayings, and doesn’t believe, I don’t judge him. For I came not to judge the world, but to save the world. 48He who rejects me, and doesn’t receive my sayings, has one who judges him. The word that I spoke, the same will judge him in the last day. 49For I spoke not from myself, but the Father who sent me, he gave me a commandment, what I should say, and what I should speak. 50I know that his commandment is eternal life. The things therefore which I speak, even as the Father has said to me, so I speak.”

Fulfillment Notes

  • The arrival of the Greeks marks the fulfillment of the appointed hour for Messiah's passion and international glorification.
  • The parable of the grain of wheat demonstrates that Christ's sacrificial death is the necessary catalyst for New Covenant harvest.
  • "Now is the judgment of this world" establishes that the expulsion of the Old Covenant accuser occurred at the cross in that generation.
  • Being "lifted up" joins Christ's crucifixion with His exaltation, drawing both Jew and Gentile into one New Creation family.
  • The invocation of Isaiah 6 confirms the judicial hardening of the Jerusalem establishment prior to its destruction in AD 70.

Annotations

The hour has come: The long-anticipated, first-century climax of redemptive history where Messiah enters His passion to establish the Kingdom.
grain of wheat: Agricultural metaphor for Christ's substitutionary death producing a multiplied harvest of New Creation believers.
prince of this world: The covenantal Accuser (*satan*) and legalistic system of Law-condemnation, decisively dethroned and cast out at the cross.
lifted up: Double-entendre referring simultaneously to Christ's physical crucifixion on the cross and His divine exaltation to authority.
a little while: Bounded temporal indicator emphasizing the imminent conclusion of Jesus' public ministry before the AD 70 judgment.

Fulfillment Commentary

1) The Arrival of the Gentiles and the Appointed Hour

Throughout the earlier chapters of John’s Gospel, Jesus repeatedly declared that His "hour had not yet come" (John 2:4; 7:30; 8:20). However, when certain God-fearing Greeks approach Philip asking to see Jesus, Messiah immediately announces a monumental shift: "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified." In Old Testament prophecy, the gathering of the Gentile nations to Zion was the definitive sign of the messianic age (Isaiah 2:2–4; Zechariah 8:22–23). The request of these Greeks signals that Christ’s public ministry to Israel has reached its climax. The hour of glorification in John’s Gospel is not an earthly political enthronement, nor is it a event deferred to a distant future age; it is the immediate redemptive event of the cross, resurrection, and ascension, through which the local barrier separating Jew from Gentile was dismantled forever.

2) The Seed, the Cross, and the Universal Harvest

Jesus explains the necessity of His upcoming death using powerful agricultural imagery: "Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains by itself alone. But if it dies, it bears much fruit." Had Jesus avoided the cross to establish a physical kingdom in first-century Jerusalem, He would have remained isolated as an earthly King over a single nation. By surrendering His life as the substitutionary grain of wheat, Messiah released the life of God into the world, bearing a global harvest of New Covenant sons and daughters. This principle extended to His first-century followers, who were called to "hate their life in this world"—meaning they were to relinquish their status, securities, and ties to the expiring Old Covenant order to participate in the indestructible life of the Kingdom.

3) The Decisive Casting Out of the Accuser

In verse 31, Jesus makes a profound statement regarding the temporal immediacy of redemptive judgment: "Now is the judgment of this world. Now the prince of this world will be cast out." In the covenantal framework, the "prince of this world" represents the accuser (*satan*) operating through the legalistic condemnation of the Law and the persecuting power of the apostate Jerusalem authorities. Jesus does not place this judgment in an indeterminate end-of-world scenario. By using the repeated Greek temporal adverb *nyn* ("now"), He asserts that His imminent death on the cross stripped the old system of its judicial power. When Christ was "lifted up" on the cross, He bore the full curse of the Law, disarming the spiritual accusation that bound humanity in fear and drawing people of all nations into His reconciled New Creation.

4) Judicial Hardening and the Close of Public Ministry

John concludes Jesus' public ministry by addressing the tragic paradox of Israel's widespread unbelief despite witnessing undeniable miraculous signs. He explains this rejection as the fulfillment of Isaiah 6:10, where God judicially blinded the eyes and hardened the hearts of an apostate generation. Many of the Judean rulers actually believed in Jesus in their hearts, but out of fear of the Pharisees, they refused to confess Him openly so as not to be "put out of the synagogue." This cowardice exposed the ultimate idolatry of the Old Covenant elite, who "loved men's praise more than God's praise." Their judicial blindness sealed the fate of the earthly Jerusalem, leading directly to the covenantal catastrophe of AD 70 when the temple was burned, the old order was abolished, and the light of Christ fully displaced the shadows of Sinai.

New Creation Reality

In Christ, you live on this side of the decisive hour. The grain of wheat has fallen and risen, yielding an eternal harvest of which you are a full partaker. The accusing power of legalism and spiritual condemnation has been cast out forever by the cross. You do not walk in darkness or under the fear of divine rejection; you are a child of light, permanently united to the Son who has drawn you to Himself and honored by the Father in His everlasting Kingdom.
