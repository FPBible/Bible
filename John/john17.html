John 17 — The High Priestly Prayer

Immediate Context

On the night of His betrayal, following the upper-room discourse, Jesus lifts His eyes to heaven in the presence of His disciples to utter His High Priestly prayer. Standing on the threshold of His sacrifice at Calvary, Messiah intercedes for His immediate apostolic core and all who would believe through their word during the AD 30–70 covenant transition. Jesus frames His imminent death, resurrection, and ascension not as a tragic defeat, but as the long-awaited hour of His glorification, transferring divine glory and covenant unity from the expiring temple-centric system of Judea into the indwelling, global New Creation body.

Scripture (WEB)

1Jesus said these things, and lifting up his eyes to heaven, he said, “Father, the time has come. Glorify your Son, that your Son may also glorify you; 2even as you gave him authority over all flesh, so he will give eternal life to all whom you have given him. 3This is eternal life, that they should know you, the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ. 4I glorified you on the earth. I have accomplished the work which you have given me to do. 5Now, Father, glorify me with your own self with the glory which I had with you before the world existed.

6I made known your name to the people whom you have given me out of the world. They were yours, and you have given them to me. They have kept your word. 7Now they have known that all things whatever you have given me are from you, 8for the words which you have given me I have given to them, and they received them, and knew for sure that I came from you, and they have believed that you sent me. 9I pray for them. I don’t pray for the world, but for those whom you have given me, for they are yours. 10All things that are mine are yours, and yours are mine, and I am glorified in them.

11I am no more in the world, but these are in the world, and I am coming to you. Holy Father, keep them through your name which you have given me, that they may be one, even as we are. 12While I was with them in the world, I kept them in your name. Those whom you have given me I have kept. None of them is lost, except the son of destruction, that the Scripture might be fulfilled. 13But now I come to you, and I say these things in the world, that they may have my joy made full in themselves. 14I have given them your word. The world hated them, because they are not of the world, even as I am not of the world. 15I pray not that you would take them from the world, but that you would keep them from the evil one.

16They are not of the world even as I am not of the world. 17Sanctify them in your truth. Your word is truth. 18As you sent me into the world, even so I have sent them into the world. 19For their sakes I sanctify myself, that they themselves also may be sanctified in truth. 20Not for these only do I pray, but for those also who will believe in me through their word,

21that they may all be one; even as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they also may be one in us; that the world may believe that you sent me. 22The glory which you have given me, I have given to them; that they may be one, even as we are one; 23I in them, and you in me, that they may be perfected into one; that the world may know that you sent me, and loved them, even as you loved me. 24Father, I desire that they also whom you have given me be with me where I am, that they may see my glory, which you have given me, for you loved me before the foundation of the world. 25Righteous Father, the world hasn’t known you, but I knew you; and these knew that you sent me.

26I made known to them your name, and will make it known; that the love with which you loved me may be in them, and I in them.”

Fulfillment Notes

  • Eternal life is redefined from endless duration to direct covenantal knowledge of the Father through the Son.
  • Christ's prayer for unity marks the transition from ethnic Israel to one new man indwelt by divine glory.
  • The "son of destruction" fulfills Old Testament prophecy through Judas' apostasy, clearing the way for apostolic witness.
  • Believers are kept within the world during the AD 30–70 transition to manifest the Kingdom before Jerusalem's fall.
  • The shared glory of the Father and Son is permanently imparted to the New Creation body, superseding temple worship.

Annotations

eternal life: Experiential, covenantal communion with the only true God through Messiah, replacing Mosaic legal standing.
made known your name: Fully revealing Yahweh's character, fatherhood, and redemptive nature in the person of Messiah.
son of destruction: Judas Iscariot as the first-century covenant apostate whose betrayal fulfilled Old Testament scripture (Psalm 41:9).
sanctify them: Consecrating believers into divine truth and setting them apart from the expiring Old Covenant world order.

Fulfillment Commentary

1) The Covenantal Redefinition of Eternal Life and Glory

In John 17:1–5, Messiah opens His High Priestly prayer by declaring, "Father, the time has come. Glorify your Son, that your Son may also glorify you." Under the Old Covenant, divine glory (*kavod*) was localized behind the veil of the Jerusalem temple, restricted to physical architecture, levitical ritual, and ethnic Israel. Jesus announces the decisive covenantal shift: authority over all flesh has been granted to the Son so that He may bestow eternal life (*zōē aiōnios*). Crucially, Messiah redefines eternal life not as static post-mortem duration in a distant heaven, but as present-tense covenantal intimacy: "This is eternal life, that they should know you, the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ." Having accomplished the redemptive work assigned to Him on earth, Jesus prays to be restored to the pre-existent glory He shared with the Father before the Old Covenant world (*kosmos*) existed. This request signals the immediate inauguration of the New Creation, where divine presence moves from stone structures into human hearts.

2) Apostolic Protection and the Son of Destruction

Moving to His intercession for the eleven apostles (John 17:6–19), Jesus highlights their divine election out of the hostile Judean world order. He testifies that He has guarded them in the Father's name, noting that "none of them is lost, except the son of destruction, that the Scripture might be fulfilled." The title "son of destruction" (*ho uios tēs apōleias*) refers directly to Judas Iscariot, whose betrayal fulfilled Old Testament prophetic types (Psalm 41:9; 109:8). Judas embodied the spirit of apostate Old Covenant Judaism, which rejected the Messiah to preserve its earthly temple standing. Jesus specifically requests that the Father keep His followers in the world rather than removing them out of history, sanctifying them in the truth of His word. During the thirty-forty year transition period (AD 30–70), the apostles were preserved amidst intense synagogue persecution to serve as the eyewitness foundation of the New Covenant temple.

3) Trinitarian Unity Imparted to the Global Body

In John 17:20–23, Jesus expands His intercession beyond the upper room: "Not for these only do I pray, but for those also who will believe in me through their word, that they may all be one." This prayer for unity (*hen*) is not a call for organizational ecumenism or institutional uniformity; it is the ontological sharing of divine life. Just as the Father is in the Son and the Son in the Father, believers are incorporated into the internal communion of the Godhead. Messiah bestows upon the church the very glory given to Him by the Father. This shared glory serves as the ultimate prophetic sign to the world that Jesus was sent by God and that believers are loved with the identical love wherewith the Father loves the Son. By collapsing the distinction between Jewish and Gentile believers, this divine oneness dismantled the wall of partition (Ephesians 2:14), manifesting the perfected New Creation family before the collapse of the earthly temple system.

4) Historical Consummation and the Eternal Dwelling of God

Jesus closes His prayer with the confident declaration: "Father, I desire that they also whom you have given me be with me where I am, that they may see my glory" (John 17:24–26). Inaugurated through His cross, resurrection, and ascension, this vision of glory reached its historical consummation in AD 70. When the earthly Jerusalem temple was dismantled and its levitical shadow ended forever, the veil was completely removed, revealing that God's true dwelling place is His people. The love of the Father and the indwelling presence of Messiah abide permanently in the New Covenant community. Believers do not look backward to Sinai or forward to an earthly millennial reign; they abide in the fulfilled reality of Messiah's High Priestly prayer, living as perfected sons and daughters in the unshakeable Kingdom of God.

New Creation Reality

In Christ, you are the direct answer to Jesus' High Priestly prayer. You possess eternal life right now as a present reality—not a distant promise—because you live in unbroken, personal communion with the Father through the Son. The very glory, protection, and love that the Father bestowed upon Messiah has been poured into your heart. You are permanently unified with God, secure in His name, and completely free from legalistic condemnation or the threat of rejection.

Reflection
