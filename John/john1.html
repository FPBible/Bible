John 1 — The Word Tabernacled Among Us

Immediate Context

John opens not with a human birth register but with a lawsuit against Israel's covenant establishment. Genesis initiated a physical world with a divine Word; this Gospel announces the close of Israel’s old covenant era with that same Word standing in flesh among "his own." Priests and Levites travel from Jerusalem to interrogate John the Baptist with formal temple-court questions regarding Christ, Elijah, and the Prophet. The answers they receive relocate divine glory, covenant lineage, and sacred access away from physical bloodlines and the Jerusalem temple, establishing them permanently in the Son during that first-century generation.

Scripture (WEB)

1In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. 2The same was in the beginning with God. 3All things were made through him. Without him, nothing was made that has been made. 4In him was life, and the life was the light of men. 5The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness hasn’t overcome it.

6There came a man sent from God, whose name was John. 7The same came as a witness, that he might testify about the light, that all might believe through him. 8He was not the light, but was sent that he might testify about the light. 9The true light that enlightens everyone was coming into the world.

10He was in the world, and the world was made through him, and the world didn’t recognize him. 11He came to his own, and those who were his own didn’t receive him. 12But as many as received him, to them he gave the right to become God’s children, to those who believe in his name: 13who were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God.

14The Word became flesh and lived among us. We saw his glory, such glory as of the only born Son of the Father, full of grace and truth. 15John testified about him. He cried out, saying, “This was he of whom I said, ‘He who comes after me has surpassed me, for he was before me.’” 16From his fullness we all received grace upon grace. 17For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth were realized through Jesus Christ. 18No one has seen God at any time. The only born Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, has declared him.

19This is John’s testimony, when the Jews sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to ask him, “Who are you?” 20He declared, and didn’t deny, but he declared, “I am not the Christ.” 21They asked him, “What then? Are you Elijah?” He said, “I am not.” “Are you the prophet?” He answered, “No.” 22They said therefore to him, “Who are you? Give us an answer to take back to those who sent us. What do you say about yourself?” 23He said, “I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, ‘Make straight the way of the Lord,’ as Isaiah the prophet said.”

24The ones who had been sent were from the Pharisees. 25They asked him, “Why then do you baptize if you are not the Christ, nor Elijah, nor the prophet?” 26John answered them, “I baptize in water, but among you stands one whom you don’t know. 27He is the one who comes after me, who is preferred before me, whose sandal strap I’m not worthy to loosen.” 28These things were done in Bethany beyond the Jordan, where John was baptizing.

29The next day, he saw Jesus coming to him, and said, “Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!” 30This is he of whom I said, “After me comes a man who is preferred before me, for he was before me.” 31I didn’t know him, but for this reason I came baptizing in water, that he would be revealed to Israel. 32John testified, saying, “I have seen the Spirit descending like a dove out of heaven, and it remained on him. 33I didn’t recognize him, but he who sent me to baptize in water said to me, ‘On whomever you will see the Spirit descending and remaining on him is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit.’ 34I have seen and have testified that this is the Son of God.”

35Again, the next day, John was standing with two of his disciples, 36and he looked at Jesus as he walked, and said, “Behold, the Lamb of God!” 37The two disciples heard him speak, and they followed Jesus. 38Jesus turned and saw them following, and said to them, “What are you looking for?” They said to him, “Rabbi” (which is to say, being interpreted, Teacher), “where are you staying?” 39He said to them, “Come and see.” They came and saw where he was staying, and they stayed with him that day. It was about the tenth hour. 40One of the two who heard John and followed him was Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother. 41He first found his own brother, Simon, and said to him, “We have found the Messiah!” (which is, being interpreted, Christ). 42He brought him to Jesus. Jesus looked at him and said, “You are Simon the son of Jonah. You shall be called Cephas” (which is by interpretation, Peter).

43On the next day, he was determined to go out into Galilee, and he found Philip. Jesus said to him, “Follow me.” 44Now Philip was from Bethsaida, the city of Andrew and Peter. 45Philip found Nathanael, and said to him, “We have found him of whom Moses in the law and also the prophets, wrote: Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph.” 46Nathanael said to him, “Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?” Philip said to him, “Come and see.” 47Jesus saw Nathanael coming to him, and said about him, “Behold, an Israelite indeed, in whom is no deceit!” 48Nathanael said to him, “How do you know me?” Jesus answered him, “Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you.” 49Nathanael answered him, “Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are King of Israel!” 50Jesus answered him, “Because I told you, ‘I saw you underneath the fig tree,’ do you believe? You will see greater things than these!” 51He said to him, “Most certainly, I tell you all, hereafter you will see heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending on the Son of Man.”

Fulfillment Notes

  • Genesis creation language ("In the beginning") signals the dawn of the New Creation and the final closing of Israel’s old covenant world.
  • "Lived among us" (tabernacled) marks divine glory relocating from temple stone onto a Person prior to AD 70.
  • Covenant lineage is redefined: royal standing is granted through birth of God rather than physical Israelite genealogy.
  • The Law given through Moses reaches its administrative endpoint, superseded by the reality of grace and truth in Jesus Christ.
  • "Heaven opened" on the Son of Man fulfills Bethel's ladder, making Christ the sole access between heaven and earth.

Annotations

In the beginning: An explicit echo of Genesis 1:1, framing Christ's advent as the origin-word of the New Creation world order.
the Word (Logos): God's ultimate self-expression and covenant voice embodied in a Person rather than written stone tablets.
world (kosmos): Refers to the ordered covenant system of Israel that failed to recognize its own Creator and Lord.
his own: First-century Israel under the mosaic administration, who held the promises yet officially rejected the Messiah.
born... of God: Re-specification of divine family entry, explicitly bypassing physical descent, circumcision, and human lineage.
lived among us: Greek eskēnōsen ("pitched tent"), indicating that God's true tabernacle and glory are now embodied in Christ.
glory: The visible Shekinah presence of God that previously filled Moses' tent and Solomon's temple, now revealed in the Son.
law... Moses / grace... Jesus Christ: Contrast between the provisional Old Covenant administration and its permanent fulfillment in Christ.
voice... in the wilderness: Isaiah 40:3 herald announcing Jehovah’s immediate covenant arrival to inspect and transform Israel.
Lamb of God / sin of the world: Fulfills the Old Covenant sacrificial system, taking away the covenant curse and guilt of that old world.
heaven opened... Son of Man: Fulfills Jacob's ladder (Genesis 28) and Daniel 7:13, establishing Christ as the single meeting-place of God and man.

Fulfillment Commentary

1) Genesis Language and the Close of the Old Order

Matthew opens his Gospel with legal paperwork because the Old Covenant order operated on documented physical lineage. John bypasses genealogies entirely, opening with Genesis 1:1 language to announce that the old world has reached its divine culmination. A covenant world possesses a start-word, light, darkness, and an appointed people designed to reflect their Maker. John asserts that the cosmos of Israel was constructed through the Word, yet when that Word arrived in human history, the temple authorities failed to recognize Him. This framing is not an abstract lesson in physical cosmology; it is a formal covenant lawsuit. The leadership managing Moses, the temple altars, and ancestral records are informed that their foundational history is being reconstituted around the Son. John declares that Israel’s public world has reached the climax where its first word and final fulfillment are the identical Word standing in flesh.

2) The Tabernacle Relocates Divine Glory

The declaration that the Word became flesh and "lived among us" utilizes explicit Exodus terminology. In the Hebrew Scriptures, God manifested His Shekinah glory within a physical tent and later inside a stone house built upon Mount Zion. Both structures were provisional, heavily guarded by levitical priests, and defined by strict physical boundaries. John employs the tabernacle verb (eskēnōsen) to reveal that God has pitched His permanent tent in a human body. The apostles witnessed His authentic glory—not as a fleeting manifestation over an earthly ark, but as the only born Son full of grace and truth. This historical reality passed sentence upon Jerusalem's temple decades before its physical destruction. With divine glory permanently localized in Christ, the stone building became obsolete long before Roman armies dismantled it in AD 70.

3) Redefining the Family of God Beyond Blood

John 1:12–13 establishes the foundational transition regarding covenant sonship. Under the Sinai administration, membership in the divine family was tied directly to physical descent from Abraham, marked by physical circumcision, and maintained through bloodlines. John dismantles this earthly framework by declaring that true divine sonship is granted exclusively to those who receive the Son through faith. Genuine spiritual birth originates directly from God, standing in contrast to ancestral blood, biological impulses, or human patriarchal choice. This structural shift explains why legalistic teachers in Jerusalem remained in spiritual darkness while Gentiles and Samaritans entered the Kingdom. Covenant identity was stripped of physical lineage, establishing a spiritual house that survived intact when the old Jerusalem fell.

4) Mosaic Administration Yields to Ultimate Reality

The assertion that the Law was given through Moses while grace and truth were realized through Jesus Christ defines historic covenant succession. The Sinai Law served as a temporary custodian, introducing shadows, animal sacrifices, and a restricted priesthood. It mediated distance between a holy God and a sinful nation. Christ did not arrive to patch the Mosaic system but to realize everything to which the Law pointed. The priests and Levites sent from Jerusalem were actively managing an administrative apparatus that had served its purpose. When John the Baptist identified himself as the wilderness voice of Isaiah 40, he signaled that the Lord had arrived to conduct covenant inspection. The old sacrificial order was yielding to the Son, who fully reveals the Father without veil, animal blood, or intermediate human priesthood.

5) The Open Heaven and the Human Ladder

Jesus concludes the chapter by reframing Nathanael’s traditional messianic titles. When Nathanael hails Him as the Son of God and King of Israel, Jesus points to a far greater covenant reality: open heavens with God's angels ascending and descending upon the Son of Man. This imagery directly fulfills Jacob’s vision at Bethel in Genesis 28. Jacob identified a geographical location as the house of God and the gate of heaven; Jesus reveals that He Himself is the true ladder connecting heaven and earth. Access to God is no longer tied to geographical sites, stone altars, or earthly sanctuaries. The Son of Man is the permanent meeting-place between God and humanity. The first-century disciples witnessed this reality unfolded in their lifetime through the inauguration of the New Covenant kingdom.

New Creation Reality

Believers no longer approach God through physical temples, earthly shadow-altars, or human genealogical qualifications. In Christ, the Word has tabernacled permanently within His people, establishing an unbroken union that can never be severed by legalistic condemnation. Your standing as a child of God rests entirely on divine birth and the finished work of the Son, free from performance-driven anxiety or Old Covenant bondage. You live under the full outflow of grace and truth, residing in an open heaven where Christ Himself is your eternal access and life.
