John 3 — Born From Above

Immediate Context

A ruler of the Jews comes after dark, still measuring Jesus by signs. Chapter 2 just relocated the temple to a body. Chapter 3 tells Israel’s teacher that seeing the kingdom will take a birth he cannot manage from flesh. The conversation is not a later altar-call script. It is Ezekiel 36–37 spoken to a man who should have recognized it, then Numbers 21 aimed at a cross inside that generation.

Scripture (WEB)

1Now there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews. 2He came to Jesus by night and said to him, “Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs that you do, unless God is with him.” 3Jesus answered him, “Most certainly I tell you, unless one is born anew, he can’t see God’s Kingdom.”

4Nicodemus said to him, “How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother’s womb and be born?” 5Jesus answered, “Most certainly I tell you, unless one is born of water and Spirit, he can’t enter into God’s Kingdom. 6That which is born of the flesh is flesh. That which is born of the Spirit is spirit. 7Don’t marvel that I said to you, ‘You must all be born anew.’ 8The wind blows where it wants to, and you hear its sound, but don’t know where it comes from and where it is going. So is everyone who is born of the Spirit.”

9Nicodemus answered him, “How can these things be?” 10Jesus answered him, “Are you the teacher of Israel, and don’t understand these things? 11Most certainly I tell you, we speak that which we know and testify of that which we have seen, and you don’t receive our witness. 12If I told you earthly things and you don’t believe, how will you believe if I tell you heavenly things? 13No one has ascended into heaven but he who descended out of heaven, the Son of Man, who is in heaven. 14As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of Man be lifted up, 15that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life.

16For God so loved the world, that he gave his only born Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life. 17For God didn’t send his Son into the world to judge the world, but that the world should be saved through him. 18He who believes in him is not judged. He who doesn’t believe has been judged already, because he has not believed in the name of the only born Son of God. 19This is the judgment, that the light has come into the world, and men loved the darkness rather than the light, for their works were evil. 20For everyone who does evil hates the light and doesn’t come to the light, lest his works would be exposed. 21But he who does the truth comes to the light, that his works may be revealed, that they have been done in God.”

22After these things, Jesus came with his disciples into the land of Judea. He stayed there with them and baptized. 23John also was baptizing in Enon near Salim, because there was much water there. They came and were baptized; 24for John was not yet thrown into prison. 25Therefore a dispute arose on the part of John’s disciples with some Jews about purification. 26They came to John and said to him, “Rabbi, he who was with you beyond the Jordan, to whom you have testified, behold, he baptizes, and everyone is coming to him.”

27John answered, “A man can receive nothing unless it has been given him from heaven. 28You yourselves testify that I said, ‘I am not the Christ,’ but, ‘I have been sent before him.’ 29He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly because of the bridegroom’s voice. Therefore my joy is made full. 30He must increase, but I must decrease. 31He who comes from above is above all. He who is from the earth belongs to the earth and speaks of the earth. He who comes from heaven is above all. 32What he has seen and heard, of that he testifies; and no one receives his witness. 33He who has received his witness has set his seal to this, that God is true. 34For he whom God has sent speaks the words of God; for God gives the Spirit without measure. 35The Father loves the Son, and has given all things into his hand. 36One who believes in the Son has eternal life, but one who disobeys the Son won’t see life, but the wrath of God remains on him.”

Fulfillment Notes

1) Nicodemus is not a random seeker. He is old-covenant Israel’s faculty in one man.

2) Born from above / water and Spirit is Ezekiel’s promised national washing and new heart, arriving in the Son.

3) “You” in “you must be born” is plural. The teacher of Israel is being told about Israel.

4) The lifted serpent is the lifted Son — look and live, in the wilderness of that age.

5) Judgment in this chapter is already underway: light has come, and refusal is the verdict.

6) The Baptist decreases because the bridegroom has the bride. The forerunner’s job ends when the voice has done its work.

Annotations

by night: Darkness is this Gospel’s climate for unbelief. A ruler still walks in it.
born anew / from above: Greek anōthen — again and from above. Not a second womb. A different origin.
water and Spirit: Ezekiel 36:25–27 — sprinkle, cleanse, new heart, Spirit within.
teacher of Israel: If Israel’s instructor misses Ezekiel, the problem is not information. It is covenant blindness.
lifted up: Wilderness pole and cross. Healing by looking at God’s provision, not by climbing back under Moses.
world: The ordered world that needed saving — first Israel’s public order under sentence.
has been judged already: Refusal is not waiting for a later courtroom. Light has already exposed it.
bridegroom: Cana’s wedding continues. John is friend, not rival.

Fulfillment Commentary

1) Why a Teacher of Israel Comes at Night

Nicodemus grants that God is with Jesus because of signs. That is chapter 2’s crowd-faith wearing better clothes. Jesus does not compliment the concession. He denies that flesh can see the kingdom. The old world produced teachers, rulers, and night visits. It could not produce the birth Ezekiel promised. If you read this as only a private conversion chat, you miss why Jesus asks whether Israel’s teacher understands. The man is standing in for a people whose credentials are about to expire.

2) Water, Spirit, and Bones That Need Breath

Ezekiel heard two promises that belong together: clean water sprinkled, a new spirit given, and a valley of bones told to live. Jesus puts both on Nicodemus. Born of water and Spirit is not “baptism plus sincerity.” It is the announced regeneration of a people who were covenant-dead. Flesh births flesh. Lineage can staff a Sanhedrin. It cannot open the kingdom that is arriving in the Son. The wind-word is the same as Spirit: you hear it, you do not manage it. That is why a ruler who manages Israel cannot schedule this birth.

3) The Serpent on the Pole Is the Clock

Moses lifted a bronze serpent in a camp that was dying under judgment. Looking was life. Jesus says the Son of Man must be lifted the same way. That is not a symbol parked for a later planet. It is the means by which that wilderness generation — and everyone who would believe — lives. Eternal life in John is the life of the coming age arriving now, not a postponement of breath. Perishing here is covenant death under the old order, the thing the lifted Son deals with before that order’s house falls.

4) Love, World, and a Judgment Already Running

“God so loved the world” is not a slogan that empties the lawsuit of chapters 1–2. The world in view is the world light has entered. Sending the Son is rescue. Refusing the Son is already judgment, because the light is not future. Men loved darkness. That is the verdict on a leadership that can study signs at night and still not come to the light. AD 70 will make the verdict public. It does not start it.

5) The Friend Steps Aside

The purification argument in the Judean countryside is the old jars still talking. John’s disciples worry about market share. John hears a wedding. The bride belongs to the bridegroom. Increase and decrease is not humility advice. It is administration change. The forerunner’s water yields to the One who gives the Spirit without measure. Wrath remaining on the one who disobeys the Son is the old world’s weather — not a threat that the New Creation people still live under.

Key Idioms

  • Born from above — Spirit-origin against flesh-origin.
  • Water and Spirit — Ezekiel’s cleansing and breath, not a second physical birth.
  • Lifted up — wilderness pole and cross in one motion.
  • Eternal life — the age-to-come life possessed now in the Son.
  • Already judged — light present; refusal is the sentence.

New Creation Reality

You are not waiting to be born from a later program. The Spirit-birth Ezekiel named has arrived in the Son. Physical death still returns a body to dust. What ended is covenant death — the darkness that could not see the kingdom. Life is the Son, given, not earned.

Reflection

Where are you still coming at night with sign-talk, instead of coming to the light as one already told to look at the lifted Son and live?
