John 3 — Born From Above

Immediate Context

Nicodemus, a prominent Pharisee and member of the Sanhedrin, comes to Jesus under cover of night during the early period of Christ's public ministry in Jerusalem. Following Jesus' cleansing of the temple and performance of signs during Passover week, this conversation represents Old Covenant Israel's theological establishment confronting the inaugural presence of the Messiah. Jesus addresses Israel's leading teacher directly, explaining that seeing and entering the Kingdom of God cannot be achieved through physical lineage, ancestral covenant privilege, or legal compliance, but requires the prophetic regeneration promised in Ezekiel 36 and Numbers 21 for that first-century generation.

Scripture (WEB)

1Now there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews. 2He came to Jesus by night and said to him, “Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs that you do, unless God is with him.” 3Jesus answered him, “Most certainly I tell you, unless one is born anew, he can’t see God’s Kingdom.”

4Nicodemus said to him, “How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother’s womb and be born?” 5Jesus answered, “Most certainly I tell you, unless one is born of water and Spirit, he can’t enter into God’s Kingdom. 6That which is born of the flesh is flesh. That which is born of the Spirit is spirit. 7Don’t marvel that I said to you, ‘You must all be born anew.’ 8The wind blows where it wants to, and you hear its sound, but don’t know where it comes from and where it is going. So is everyone who is born of the Spirit.”

9Nicodemus answered him, “How can these things be?” 10Jesus answered him, “Are you the teacher of Israel, and don’t understand these things? 11Most certainly I tell you, we speak that which we know and testify of that which we have seen, and you don’t receive our witness. 12If I told you earthly things and you don’t believe, how will you believe if I tell you heavenly things? 13No one has ascended into heaven but he who descended out of heaven, the Son of Man, who is in heaven. 14As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of Man be lifted up, 15that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life.

16For God so loved the world, that he gave his only born Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life. 17For God didn’t send his Son into the world to judge the world, but that the world should be saved through him. 18He who believes in him is not judged. He who doesn’t believe has been judged already, because he has not believed in the name of the only born Son of God. 19This is the judgment, that the light has come into the world, and men loved the darkness rather than the light, for their works were evil. 20For everyone who does evil hates the light and doesn’t come to the light, lest his works would be exposed. 21But he who does the truth comes to the light, that his works may be revealed, that they have been done in God.”

22After these things, Jesus came with his disciples into the land of Judea. He stayed there with them and baptized. 23John also was baptizing in Enon near Salim, because there was much water there. They came and were baptized; 24for John was not yet thrown into prison. 25Therefore a dispute arose on the part of John’s disciples with some Jews about purification. 26They came to John and said to him, “Rabbi, he who was with you beyond the Jordan, to whom you have testified, behold, he baptizes, and everyone is coming to him.”

27John answered, “A man can receive nothing unless it has been given him from heaven. 28You yourselves testify that I said, ‘I am not the Christ,’ but, ‘I have been sent before him.’ 29He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly because of the bridegroom’s voice. Therefore my joy is made full. 30He must increase, but I must decrease. 31He who comes from above is above all. He who is from the earth belongs to the earth and speaks of the earth. He who comes from heaven is above all. 32What he has seen and heard, of that he testifies; and no one receives his witness. 33He who has received his witness has set his seal to this, that God is true. 34For he whom God has sent speaks the words of God; for God gives the Spirit without measure. 35The Father loves the Son, and has given all things into his hand. 36One who believes in the Son has eternal life, but one who disobeys the Son won’t see life, but the wrath of God remains on him.”

Fulfillment Notes

  • Nicodemus represents Old Covenant Israel's ruling establishment confronting the transition to Messiah's Kingdom.
  • "Born of water and Spirit" fulfills Ezekiel 36:25–27 as the promised national cleansing and divine spirit-bestowal.
  • The lifting up of the Son of Man fulfills Numbers 21, providing instant life over Old Covenant curse-death.
  • Divine judgment in John 3 is already active in the first century, exposing those who reject the light.
  • John the Baptist's decrease marks the official administrative transition from forerunner shadow to Messiah's reign.

Annotations

Nicodemus / ruler of the Jews: A member of the Sanhedrin representing the pinnacle of Old Covenant legal authority and ancestry.
by night: Symbolizes the spiritual darkness and blindness surrounding Israel’s religious establishment.
born anew / from above: Greek anōthen ("again" or "from above"), signaling divine Spirit-origin rather than biological descent.
water and Spirit: Direct reference to Ezekiel 36:25–27, promising spiritual cleansing and heart-transformation for New Covenant entry.
teacher of Israel: Title highlighting Nicodemus' responsibility to understand prophetic promises of Israel's national regeneration.
lifted up the serpent / Son of Man be lifted up: Typological link to Numbers 21:8–9; Christ’s cross and glorification bring deliverance from covenant curse.
eternal life: Greek zōēn aiōnion ("life of the age to come"), the present possession of Kingdom life in Christ.
world (kosmos): The ordered first-century world needing redemptive deliverance from darkness and judgment.
has been judged already: Present-tense judicial verdict; rejecting the light results in immediate self-condemnation.
bridegroom / He must increase, but I must decrease: The formal passing of redemptive authority from the Old Covenant forerunner to Messiah.

Fulfillment Commentary

1) Nicodemus and the Confrontation of Old Covenant Lineage

Nicodemus approaches Jesus under the cover of night, addressing Him as a teacher endorsed by miraculous signs. This night visit symbolizes the spiritual darkness enveloping Israel’s ruling establishment. As a Pharisee and member of the Sanhedrin, Nicodemus embodied the highest peak of Old Covenant prestige, ancestral pedigree, and Torah expertise. Yet, Jesus immediately confronts this heritage, asserting that physical lineage, legal compliance, and religious status cannot grant entry into God's Kingdom. The inauguration of the Kingdom demands an entirely different origin—a birth from above. Jesus addresses Nicodemus not merely as a private seeker, but as a representative of first-century Israel whose covenant credentials were expiring. The old system was incapable of producing spiritual life, requiring the ruling class to surrender their reliance on physical descent from Abraham.

2) Water, Spirit, and Ezekiel’s Promised Regeneration

When Jesus speaks of being "born of water and Spirit," He is not introducing an abstract sacramental formula, but citing the explicit Old Covenant promises of national restoration found in Ezekiel 36:25–27 and Ezekiel 37. God promised that under the New Covenant, He would sprinkle clean water upon His people, cleanse them from idols, place His Spirit within them, and transform their hearts of stone into hearts of flesh. As "the teacher of Israel," Nicodemus was expected to recognize these iconic prophetic themes. Physical flesh can only propagate flesh; it cannot generate the resurrection life of the Kingdom age. The Spirit operates like the wind, sovereignly bestowing New Creation life beyond human control or levitical regulation. The old covenant administration was reaching its endpoint, replaced by the Spirit-breathed regeneration realized through the Son.

3) The Lifted Serpent and Deliverance from Covenant Curse

Jesus anchors His redemptive mission in the wilderness narrative of Numbers 21, where Moses raised a bronze serpent upon a pole to heal Israelites dying from venomous bites. Jesus declares that the Son of Man must be lifted up in the exact same manner. This lifting up encompasses both His crucifixion on Mount Calvary and His subsequent resurrection and ascension to divine glory. In the first-century context, Israel was suffering under the ultimate curse of the Law—spiritual death and alienation from God. Just as looking upon the bronze serpent brought physical healing to the dying camp in the wilderness, believing in the lifted Son imparts instant, permanent eternal life. Eternal life in John’s Gospel is the very life of the age to come, breaking into present history and delivering believers from the imminent judgment coming upon that covenant generation.

4) The Light Present and the First-Century Verdict

The famous declaration of John 3:16–19 highlights the immense scope of God's covenant love and the immediate nature of divine judgment. God sent His Son into the world not to condemn it, but to accomplish genuine salvation. However, for those who rejected the incarnate Light, judgment was not a distant event deferred to the end of physical time; it was already actively taking place in their historical present. Refusing the Son constituted self-condemnation. The first-century Judean leadership loved the darkness of their legalistic system because their works were exposed by Messiah's light. The eventual destruction of the Jerusalem temple in AD 70 was the public execution of a sentence that had already been passed when the religious authorities rejected the Son.

5) The Decreasing Forerunner and the Bridegroom’s Era

The final movement of John 3 resolves a dispute regarding baptism and ceremonial purification between the disciples of John the Baptist and certain Judean leaders. John the Baptist clarifies his temporary role, identifying himself not as the Messiah, but as the friend of the Bridegroom who rejoices at the Bridegroom's voice. His famous declaration, "He must increase, but I must decrease," marks the divine transition between two covenant eras. The preparatory ministry of the Old Covenant forerunner was reaching its appointed conclusion, making way for the permanent reign of the Son. The Father has entrusted all authority into the hands of the Son. Those who trust in Him possess eternal life immediately, while those who persist in Old Covenant disobedience remain under the divine wrath that consumed the old system.

New Creation Reality

As a believer in Christ, you possess the permanent life of the age to come right now. You are not waiting for future spiritual rebirth or striving under Old Covenant legalism to secure divine acceptance. Having been born from above through the Holy Spirit, your identity is rooted entirely in Christ’s finished work. You live under an open heaven, completely delivered from covenant condemnation, enjoying continuous union with the Son and resting securely in His eternal life.
