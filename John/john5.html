John 5 — The Son Gives Life — Moses Accuses

Immediate Context

During a feast of the Jews in Jerusalem, Jesus encounters an invalid man who had been paralyzed for thirty-eight years beside the pool of Bethesda. In healing this man on the Sabbath and commanding him to pick up his mat, Jesus directly challenges the rabbinic traditions of the religious leadership and detonates the Old Covenant Sabbath apparatus. When the Judean leaders persecute Jesus for breaking the Sabbath and claiming equality with God, Jesus delivers a formal defense of His divine Sonship. Speaking to the temple authorities in the first-century setting, Jesus announces that spiritual resurrection and judgment are actively underway in Him, warning them that Moses, in whom they set their hope, is their true accuser for rejecting the Messiah.

Scripture (WEB)

1After these things, there was a feast of the Jews, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem. 2Now in Jerusalem by the sheep gate, there is a pool, which is called in Hebrew, “Bethesda”, having five porches. 3In these lay a great multitude of those who were sick, blind, lame, or paralyzed, waiting for the moving of the water; 4for an angel went down at certain times into the pool, and stirred up the water. Whoever stepped in first after the stirring of the water was healed of whatever disease he had. 5A certain man was there, who had been sick for thirty-eight years.

6When Jesus saw him lying there, and knew that he had been sick for a long time, he asked him, “Do you want to be made well?” 7The sick man answered him, “Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up, but while I’m coming, another steps down before me.” 8Jesus said to him, “Arise, take up your mat, and walk.” 9Immediately, the man was made well, and took up his mat and walked. Now it was the Sabbath on that day. 10So the Jews said to him who was cured, “It is the Sabbath. It is not lawful for you to carry the mat.”

11He answered them, “He who made me well, the same said to me, ‘Take up your mat, and walk.’” 12Then they asked him, “Who is the man who said to you, ‘Take up your mat, and walk’?” 13But he who was healed didn’t know who it was, for Jesus had withdrawn, a crowd being in the place. 14Afterward Jesus found him in the temple, and said to him, “Behold, you are made well. Sin no more, so that nothing worse happens to you.” 15The man went away, and told the Jews that it was Jesus who had made him well.

16For this cause the Jews persecuted Jesus, and sought to kill him, because he did these things on the Sabbath. 17But Jesus answered them, “My Father is still working, so I am working, too.” 18For this cause therefore the Jews sought all the more to kill him, because he not only broke the Sabbath, but also called God his own Father, making himself equal with God. 19Jesus therefore answered them, “Most certainly, I tell you, the Son can do nothing of himself, but what he sees the Father doing. For whatever things he does, these the Son also does likewise. 20For the Father has affection for the Son, and shows him all things that he himself does. He will show him greater works than these, that you may marvel.

21For as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, even so the Son also gives life to whom he desires. 22For the Father judges no one, but he has given all judgment to the Son, 23that all may honor the Son, even as they honor the Father. He who doesn’t honor the Son doesn’t honor the Father who sent him. 24“Most certainly I tell you, he who hears my word, and believes him who sent me, has eternal life, and doesn’t come into judgment, but has passed out of death into life. 25Most certainly, I tell you, the hour comes, and now is, when the dead will hear the Son of God’s voice; and those who hear will live.

26For as the Father has life in himself, even so he gave to the Son also to have life in himself. 27He also gave him authority to execute judgment, because he is a son of man. 28Don’t marvel at this, for the hour comes, in which all that are in the tombs will hear his voice, 29and will come out; those who have done good, to the resurrection of life; and those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment. 30I can of myself do nothing. As I hear, I judge, and my judgment is righteous; because I don’t seek my own will, but the will of my Father who sent me.

31“If I testify about myself, my witness is not valid. 32It is another who testifies about me. I know that the testimony which he testifies about me is true. 33You have sent to John, and he has testified to the truth. 34But the testimony which I receive is not from man. However, I say these things that you may be saved. 35He was the burning and shining lamp, and you were willing to rejoice for a while in his light.

36But the testimony which I have is greater than that of John, for the works which the Father gave me to accomplish, the very works that I do, testify about me, that the Father has sent me. 37The Father himself, who sent me, has testified about me. You have neither heard his voice at any time, nor seen his form. 38You don’t have his word living in you; because you don’t believe him whom he sent. 39“You search the Scriptures, because you think that in them you have eternal life; and these are they which testify about me. 40Yet you will not come to me, that you may have life.

41I don’t receive glory from men. 42But I know you, that you don’t have God’s love in yourselves. 43I have come in my Father’s name, and you don’t receive me. If another comes in his own name, you will receive him. 44How can you believe, who receive glory from one another, and you don’t seek the glory that comes from the only God? 45“Don’t think that I will accuse you to the Father. There is one who accuses you, even Moses, on whom you have set your hope.

46For if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote about me. 47But if you don’t believe his writings, how will you believe my words?”

Fulfillment Notes

  • Bethesda's five porches represent the Old Covenant legal system, providing the appearance of mercy without the power to heal.
  • The thirty-eight years of infirmity echo Israel's wilderness wanderings, signaling the end of the nation's spiritual paralysis.
  • Healing on the Sabbath demonstrates that Christ is the true Sabbath rest, bringing an end to legalistic shadow-keeping.
  • Passing from death into life is a present spiritual transfer accomplished through faith in the Son during the inaugural Kingdom age.
  • Moses functions as the legal accuser of first-century Judea, condemning those who rested in the Law while rejecting Messiah.

Annotations

Bethesda: A house of mercy surrounded by five porches, representing the obsolete Old Covenant apparatus that advertised help but could not impart life.
thirty-eight years: Direct echo of Israel's 38-year wilderness delay (Deuteronomy 2:14), symbolizing long-standing covenant paralysis under the Law.
Sabbath: The seventh-day sign of the Mosaic Covenant, fulfilled and transcended by Christ who is the true rest for God's people.
passed out of death into life: Greek perfect tense indicating an accomplished spiritual reality, where believers possess eternal life in the present age.
hour comes, and now is: Temporal transition phrase referring to the inaugural resurrection of the covenant-dead during Messiah's earthly ministry.
hour comes: Refers to the imminent covenant judgment and corporate resurrection associated with Daniel 12 and the end of the Old Covenant order in AD 70.
accuses you, even Moses: The Mosaic Law acts as a prosecuting witness against unbelieving Judea, exposing their failure to recognize the Messiah of whom Moses wrote.

Fulfillment Commentary

1) Bethesda and the Failure of Old Covenant Structures

The pool of Bethesda with its five porches serves as a vivid physical picture of the Old Covenant system. Surrounding the pool lay a crowd of sick, blind, and paralyzed individuals waiting for the moving of the water, operating within a framework where only the fastest or strongest could obtain healing. This competitive, performance-based environment highlights the fundamental limitation of the Mosaic Law: it could identify human weakness and advertise divine mercy, yet possessed no internal power to heal or liberate the sufferer. Jesus bypasses the pool completely, speaking a direct word of command that immediately restores a man who had been paralyzed for thirty-eight years. This duration explicitly echoes the thirty-eight years of Israel’s wilderness wandering described in Deuteronomy 2:14, representing the long era of spiritual paralysis under the Law. By healing the man without the pool's water, Jesus demonstrates that true restoration originates solely from the Son, superseding the obsolete mechanisms of the Old Covenant order.

2) The Sabbath Controversy and Sovereign Rest

By commanding the healed man to pick up his mat and walk on the Sabbath, Jesus deliberately provoked a confrontation with the Judean religious authorities. In rabbinic tradition, carrying a mat on the Sabbath constituted a major violation of oral law. When challenged, Jesus defended His actions by declaring, "My Father is still working, so I am working, too." This assertion enraged the authorities because He not only dismantled their Sabbath regulations, but also claimed divine Sonship and functional equality with God. Under the Sinai administration, the seventh-day Sabbath was a temporary sign pointing toward the ultimate rest found in Messiah. Jesus revealed that God’s redemptive work did not cease on the Sabbath; rather, the Son possesses absolute divine authority to bestow Kingdom life and true rest. The Sabbath shadow was reaching its administrative fulfillment, yielding to the reality of the Son’s ongoing work of New Creation.

3) Two Hours of Resurrection and Covenant Judgment

In John 5:24–29, Jesus outlines two distinct dimensions of resurrection and authority. In verse 25, He speaks of the hour that "comes, and now is, when the dead will hear the Son of God’s voice; and those who hear will live." This present-tense declaration describes the inaugural spiritual resurrection occurring during His first-century ministry, as people dead in sin and covenant darkness were raised to eternal life through faith. In contrast, verses 28–29 describe the coming hour when "all that are in the tombs will hear his voice, and will come out; those who have done good, to the resurrection of life; and those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment." This language draws directly from Daniel 12:1–3, predicting the corporate resurrection and final covenant judgment associated with the end of the Jewish age. The AD 70 destruction of Jerusalem and the dismantling of the temple system marked the visible execution of this judgment, where the old house was dismantled and the Kingdom was fully established.

4) Searching Scriptures Without the Son

Jesus directly rebukes the Judean leaders for their approach to the Sacred Writings: "You search the Scriptures, because you think that in them you have eternal life; and these are they which testify about me." The Pharisees treated the text of the Law and Prophets as a self-contained repository of merit, assuming that diligent textual study and ritual compliance granted them automatic standing in the age to come. However, Jesus reveals that the Scriptures do not contain life within themselves; their entire divine purpose is to testify of the Son. By searching the text while rejecting the Person to whom the text pointed, the religious establishment demonstrated profound covenant blindness. They prized religious status and human praise over the divine glory revealed in Christ, isolating themselves from the very source of eternal life.

5) Moses as the Legal Accuser

Jesus concludes His defense with a devastating judicial reversal: "Don’t think that I will accuse you to the Father. There is one who accuses you, even Moses, on whom you have set your hope." The Judean leaders viewed Moses as their divine advocate and covenant protector, relying on their Mosaic identity to guarantee their elite status. Jesus exposes this fatal misconception by revealing that Moses was actually their chief prosecutor. Every line written by Moses—including the Pentateuch's types, sacrifices, and prophetic warnings—pointed directly to Jesus Christ. By rejecting Messiah, the authorities stood condemned by the very Law they claimed to defend. The Mosaic administration, which was designed to lead Israel to Christ, became a witness of judgment against an unrepentant generation that refused to enter the New Covenant reality.

New Creation Reality

In Christ, you have passed out of death into life and will never come into judgment. You are no longer sitting beside an earthly pool of striving, nor are you subject to the legalistic condemnation of the Law. The Son has granted you His own eternal life, establishing your identity in complete union with Him. You do not labor under the anxiety of performance-based acceptance; rather, you live in the constant reality of Messiah’s Sabbath rest, fully justified, eternally secure, and forever free from the accuser.
