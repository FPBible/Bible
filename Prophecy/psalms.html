Psalms — AD 70 Patterns & New Creation

Hebrew idioms and prophetic patterns the New Testament quotes for the covenant transition and the New Creation

Reading frame. These psalms are not primarily private devotion. They supply the royal, covenantal, and apocalyptic language the apostles and Jesus used when describing judgment on the old order, the enthronement of the Son, and the establishment of the unshakable kingdom. Read them as the Old Testament dictionary behind Matthew 24, Acts, Hebrews, and Revelation — not as postponed end-of-planet scripts.

1. Apocalyptic Imagery & Divine Judgment (“Coming on Clouds”)

  • Psalm 18:7–15 — Cosmic decreation language for a historical rescue; the template for “coming on clouds.”
  • Psalms 97 & 98 — The Lord comes to judge with righteousness; mountains melt; the kingdom arrives.

2. Messianic Enthronement & Reign of Christ

  • Psalm 2 — The Son enthroned; rebellious rulers shattered; cited in Acts and Revelation.
  • Psalm 110 — Sit at My right hand until enemies are a footstool; the NT’s most-quoted psalm.

3. Suffering, Vindication & the Passing of Old Zion

  • Psalm 22 — Suffering Servant, resurrection vindication, nations called; cross to Gentile mission.
  • Psalm 69 — Imprecatory judgment on persecutors; applied to unbelieving Israel and temple desolation.

4. Covenant Transition, New Creation & Everlasting Kingdom

  • Psalm 46 — Earth shaken; city of God secure; mirrors Hebrews 12’s unshakable kingdom.
  • Psalm 102:25–28 — Heavens and earth wear out like a garment; quoted in Hebrews 1 for the aging old order.
