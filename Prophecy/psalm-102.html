Psalm 102:25–28

“They will perish, but You remain; they will all wear out like a garment”

Key theme. Of old God laid the foundation of the earth; the heavens are the work of His hands. They will perish, but He remains. They wear out like a garment; He changes them like a robe. His years have no end. The children of His servants will dwell secure.

Garment and permanence

The contrast is absolute: created arrangements are changeable and perishable; God is not. The garment image is deliberate — something that can be changed and folded, not an eternal fixture.

Preterist connection

Hebrews 1:10–12 quotes this passage and applies it to the Son. In the letter’s argument, the point is not abstract cosmology for its own sake. It supports the superiority of the Son over angels and sets up the later claim that what is shaken — things that have been made — will be removed so that what cannot be shaken may remain (Hebrews 12).

In covenantal context, the “heavens and earth” passing away like a folded garment refers to the aging, decaying Old Covenant order giving way to the permanent New Covenant. The same letter says the first covenant was becoming obsolete and was near to disappearing (Hebrews 8:13). Psalm 102 supplies the poetic-theological warrant: what is made can be changed; the Son remains.

New Creation Reality

You do not anchor identity in a garment God has scheduled for change. You anchor in the One who remains. The New Covenant is not another robe to wear out; it is participation in the permanence of the Son’s reign and priesthood.
