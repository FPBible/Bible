Psalm 46

Earth shaken · mountains moved · the city of God not moved

Key theme. God is refuge and strength. Therefore we will not fear though the earth gives way and mountains fall into the heart of the sea. There is a river whose streams make glad the city of God. Nations rage; kingdoms totter; He makes wars cease. Be still, and know that He is God.

Shaking and security

The psalm holds two images together: cosmic and political upheaval on the one hand, and an unmoved city on the other. The security of God’s people is not the absence of shaking; it is the presence of God in the city He establishes.

Preterist connection

Psalm 46 mirrors the logic of Hebrews 12:22–28. There the readers have come to Mount Zion, the city of the living God — and are told that a shaking will remove what can be shaken, as of things that have been made, so that what cannot be shaken may remain. The unshakable kingdom is received with gratitude and reverent worship.

In covenantal reading, the “shaking” is not primarily a physics event. It is the removal of the old covenant administration — the made-with-hands order — in the crisis that reached its public end in AD 70. The city that remains is the New Jerusalem reality Hebrews already says the readers have come to.

New Creation Reality

God is still refuge when nations rage. The city of God is not secured by rebuilding the shaken system. It is secured by the God who is in her midst. Be still — not because nothing shakes, but because He is God, exalted among the nations.
