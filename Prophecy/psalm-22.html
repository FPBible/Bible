Psalm 22

Suffering Servant · vindication · all nations called

Key theme. Forsakenness, mockery, pierced hands and feet, garments divided — then the turn: God heard; the afflicted will eat and be satisfied; all the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the LORD; dominion belongs to the LORD.

From agony to worldwide worship

The psalm does not end in the dust of death. The sufferer’s deliverance becomes the occasion for public praise and for the nations’ turning. Posterity is served; a people yet unborn hear of righteousness that has been done.

Preterist connection

The Gospels explicitly map the crucifixion onto Psalm 22. Fulfilled reading keeps reading after the cross-lines: the same psalm ties the agony of the Servant to the generation that would see the gospel run to the Gentiles and the end of exclusive Jewish ritual as the defining center of God’s people.

Vindication is not only “He rose.” It is also the historical outcome the psalm names — ends of the earth turning, families of the nations worshiping — which the apostolic age announces and which the collapse of the old temple economy publicly confirms. The cross and the covenant transition belong to one story.

New Creation Reality

The nations are not an appendix to Israel’s private sorrow. They are in the psalm’s own horizon of vindication. In the New Creation people of God, worship is not gated by the old ritual center. The Servant’s deliverance has already opened that worldwide turn.
