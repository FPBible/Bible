Time Statements in the New Testament

Imminence • Nearness • “This Generation” • Coming Quickly • First-Century Horizon

Why the Time Statements Matter

The New Testament is saturated with time indicators. Jesus and the apostles repeatedly said the events of the Kingdom, the judgment, the end of the age, and the coming/parousia of the Son of Man were near, at hand, soon, about to occur, and tied to the generation then living.

These are not elastic metaphors stretched across millennia, nor were they written merely to keep every generation indefinitely “on tiptoe.” They were spoken and written to real first-century audiences with clear temporal force.

In the fulfilled view these statements cohere with the AD 30–70 transition: the cross, the outpouring of the Spirit, the end of the Old Covenant order, and the decisive judgment on “that wicked generation” culminating in the fall of Jerusalem and the Temple. The dividing wall is removed; the one new man in Christ stands; the Eighth Day reality of the New Covenant has arrived.

1. “At hand / Near / Drawing near” (eggizō / eggus)

These convey temporal proximity — “within reach,” as close as a hand on a door handle.

  • Matthew 3:2; 4:17; 10:7 — “The kingdom of heaven is at hand.”
  • Mark 1:15 — “The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand.”
  • Luke 10:9, 11 — “The kingdom of God has come near to you.”
  • Romans 13:11–12 — “Salvation is nearer to us now than when we first believed… the night is far gone; the day is at hand.”
  • Philippians 4:5 — “The Lord is at hand / near.”
  • Hebrews 10:25 — “the Day approaching.”
  • James 5:8 — “the coming of the Lord is at hand.”
  • 1 Peter 4:7 — “The end of all things is at hand.”
  • Revelation 1:3; 22:10 — “the time is near.”

2. “This generation will not pass away”

Jesus repeatedly anchors fulfillment to the generation then alive.

  • Matthew 23:36 — “Truly, I say to you, all these things will come upon this generation.”
  • Matthew 24:34; Mark 13:30; Luke 21:32 — “Truly, I say to you, this generation will not pass away until all these things take place.”

Compare also the judgment language of Matthew 23 and the parallel in 1 Thessalonians 2:14–16.

3. “Some standing here will not taste death”

Direct limitation to people physically present when Jesus spoke.

  • Matthew 16:28 — “Truly, I say to you, there are some standing here who will not taste death until they see the Son of Man coming in his kingdom.”
  • Mark 9:1 — “…until they see the kingdom of God after it has come with power.”
  • Luke 9:27 — “…until they see the kingdom of God.”

4. Mission and audience limitations

  • Matthew 10:23 — “You will not have gone through all the towns of Israel before the Son of Man comes.”
  • Matthew 26:64 (and parallels) — addressed to Caiaphas and those present: “from now on you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven.”

5. “Quickly / Soon / Shortly / Without delay” (tachu / en tachei)

Especially prominent in Revelation, bookended for emphasis, and consistent with ordinary usage of the word for prompt action in time.

  • Revelation 1:1 — “the things that must soon take place” (en tachei).
  • Revelation 1:3 — “the time is near.”
  • Revelation 2:16; 3:11 — “I am coming quickly.”
  • Revelation 22:6 — “the things that must soon take place.”
  • Revelation 22:7, 12, 20 — “I am coming quickly / soon.”
  • Hebrews 10:37 — “For yet a little while, and the coming one will come and will not delay.”
  • Romans 16:20 — “The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet.”
  • 1 Corinthians 7:29 — “the appointed time has grown very short.”

6. “About to” constructions (mellō)

Frequently used of wrath, judgment, the age to come, resurrection, and the Lord’s appearing as imminent for the original hearers and readers. Selected examples:

  • Matthew 3:7; Luke 3:7 — “the wrath about to come.”
  • Matthew 16:27 — “the Son of Man is about to come…”
  • Acts 17:31; 24:15, 25 — judgment / resurrection “about to” take place.
  • Romans 8:18 — “the glory about to be revealed.”
  • 2 Timothy 4:1 — Christ “who is about to judge the living and the dead.”
  • Hebrews 10:27 — “a fearful expectation of judgment… about to consume.”
  • Also: Hebrews 1:14; 2:5; 6:5; 9:11; 10:1; 13:14; 1 Peter 5:1; and others.

7. Related vivid imagery of imminence

  • Matthew 3:10; Luke 3:9 — “Even now the axe is laid to the root of the trees.”
  • Matthew 3:12; Luke 3:17 — “His winnowing fork is in his hand.”
  • James 5:9 — “the Judge is standing at the door.”
  • 1 John 2:18 — “it is the last hour.”

Observations for the Fulfilled Prophecy Bible

These statements are not scattered isolated remarks. They appear across Jesus’ teaching, the Synoptics, Paul, James, Peter, John, Hebrews, and Revelation. The original audiences were never told the events were “far off” or for a distant future age. Personal pronouns (“you,” “we,” “us”) and audience-specific language reinforce that the expected timeframe was within their lifetime or generation.

When read with first-century covenant horizon in view, the language coheres: the Old Covenant order was ending, judgment was falling on the generation that filled up the measure of its fathers’ sins, the remnant was being saved, the dividing wall removed, and the New Creation reality of the one new man in Christ was breaking in.

Physical mortality continues (return to dust was part of the original created order), but the spiritual / covenantal death and condemnation of the old Adamic / old-covenant order has been decisively dealt with.
