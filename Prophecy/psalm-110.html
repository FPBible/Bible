Psalm 110

“Sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies a footstool”

Key theme. Yahweh’s oracle to David’s Lord: sit at My right hand until enemies are made a footstool. Rule in the midst of enemies. A priest forever after Melchizedek. Judgment on nations; the King refreshed by the brook.

Session and subjection

The psalm joins two offices: royal session at God’s right hand, and perpetual priesthood. The “until” marks an active period — enemies being subjected while the King reigns, not an idle wait for a reign to begin.

Preterist connection

Psalm 110 is the most-quoted Old Testament passage in the New Testament (e.g. Acts 2:34–35; 1 Corinthians 15:25; Hebrews 1:13; 10:12–13). Peter’s Pentecost sermon already locates the session in the resurrection-ascension. Hebrews treats the seated priest as the proof of finished offering and ongoing administration.

Fulfilled reading traces that administration from Pentecost through the apostolic age to the AD 70 horizon, where the old temple system — and the condemning power structured around it — was publicly subjected under His feet. The enemies in view are not only generic spiritual abstractions; they include the covenantal adversaries of the Messiah’s claim and the order that rejected Him.

New Creation Reality

The priest-king is seated. His enemies are not negotiating a stalemate. The footstool promise is the logic of Hebrews’ “waiting until” and of the church’s confidence that the finished offering is matched by real historical subjection of the old adversarial order.
