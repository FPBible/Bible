Psalm 110

1. Immediate Context

Oracle of Yahweh to David’s Lord: session at the right hand until enemies are a footstool; rule in the midst of enemies; priesthood forever after Melchizedek. The most-quoted Old Testament passage in the New Testament. Session and subjection run together — an active “until,” not an idle wait for a reign to begin.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1Yahweh says to my Lord, “Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies your footstool for your feet.”

2Yahweh will send out the rod of your strength out of Zion. Rule among your enemies.

3Your people offer themselves willingly in the day of your power, in holy array. Out of the womb of the morning, you have the dew of your youth.

4Yahweh has sworn, and will not change his mind: “You are a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek.”

5The Lord is at your right hand. He will crush kings in the day of his wrath.

6He will judge among the nations. He will heap up dead bodies. He will crush the ruler of the whole earth.

7He will drink of the brook on the way; therefore he will lift up his head.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Acts 2:34–35 locates the session in the resurrection-ascension.
  • Hebrews treats the seated priest as proof of finished offering and ongoing administration (Heb 1:13; 10:12–13).
  • 1 Corinthians 15:25 cites the footstool promise for Christ’s reign until all enemies are subjected.
  • The “until” marks the period in which enemies are being made a footstool while the King already reigns.

4. Annotations

  • “Sit at my right hand” — royal and priestly session; authority shared with Yahweh.
  • Footstool — total subjection of enemies under the reigning King.
  • Order of Melchizedek — priest-king outside the Levitical line; Hebrews’ central argument.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

Psalm 110 is the spine of apostolic Christology. Peter’s Pentecost sermon already places Jesus at the right hand. Hebrews argues that the seated priest has offered once for all and waits until His enemies are made a footstool. That waiting is not delay of the kingdom; it is the administration of a kingdom already in force.

In the first-century horizon the enemies in view include the covenantal adversaries of the Messiah’s claim and the temple order that rejected Him. The public end of that order is the outworking of the footstool promise — not the start of the session. The priest-king is seated; His enemies are not negotiating a stalemate.

8. Key Idioms

  • Right hand — place of power and shared rule.
  • Footstool of your feet — enemies fully subjected under the King.

9. New Creation Reality

The finished offering is matched by real historical subjection of the old adversarial order. Identity is anchored in the seated priest-king, not in rebuilding what His reign has already displaced.

10. Reflection

Am I living as if the Son is still waiting to begin — or as if enemies are already under a seated Lord’s feet?
