The Dividing Wall Removed

The middle wall of partition in Ephesians 2 is the Old Covenant boundary system — broken by the cross and finished as public administration in AD 70.

The Wall in Ephesians 2

Paul names “the dividing wall of hostility” and ties it to “the law of commandments expressed in ordinances” (Ephesians 2:14–15). This is covenant language. The wall was the legal-religious structure that separated Israel under Moses from the nations, and that also produced hostility between the groups as the old age closed.

In the Temple complex a literal barrier warned Gentiles against entry on pain of death. Paul’s metaphor is not exhausted by stone, but the stone made the theology visible: graded holiness, restricted access, identity by ordinance.

How the Wall Came Down

Christ abolished the hostility in His flesh. The cross does not tweak the wall; it creates peace by making one body. Gentiles are no longer strangers and aliens but fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God (Ephesians 2:19).

The apostolic mission then enacts the removal: the Spirit falls on the uncircumcised (Acts 10), table fellowship is defended (Galatians 2), and the council refuses to impose Moses on the nations (Acts 15). The wall’s theology is already denied in the church’s practice before the Temple falls.

Hostility’s Last Stand

Those who clung to the wall as covenant standing became the persecutors of the Way. From Acts through the years before AD 70, the conflict is not abstract spirituality; it is the old administration resisting its own obsolescence — accusing, excluding, and aligning with power to suppress the gospel.

When the house fell, the wall had nowhere left to stand.
AD 70 ended the public architecture of separation. The New Covenant people remain defined by faith, not by courts of exclusion.

Pastoral Weight

The removed wall means you do not approach God through a second-class court. There is one access: Christ. There is one household. Attempts to rebuild ethnic or ritual ranks as the basis of standing contradict the peace the cross already made.

Read: Ephesians 2; Acts 10–11, 15; Galatians 2–3 (WEB)
