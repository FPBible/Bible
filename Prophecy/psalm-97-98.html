Psalms 97 & 98

The Lord comes to judge the earth with righteousness — lightnings, melting mountains, and the arrival of His reign

Key theme. Yahweh reigns. The world is summoned to rejoice. Lightning lights up the world; the earth sees and trembles; mountains melt like wax before the Lord. He comes to judge the world with righteousness and the peoples with equity.

Reign and theophany

Psalm 97 opens with “The LORD reigns” and moves immediately into storm-theophany and moral polarity: idolators are put to shame; Zion hears and is glad. Psalm 98 answers with a new song for the victory He has won, the revelation of His righteousness to the nations, and the climactic coming to judge.

This is not quiet private piety. It is public kingship language — the same stream that feeds later “coming” and “kingdom” proclamations.

Preterist connection

These psalms prefigure the final covenantal judgment of the old order and the arrival of His reigning kingdom in history. When the New Testament announces that the kingdom has drawn near, that the Son of Man comes in glory, and that judgment falls on the adversaries of that reign, it is drawing on this poetic-theological register: God shows up as king, the earth trembles, rivals collapse, and the faithful rejoice.

Mountains melting and earth trembling are stock theophanic images for the overthrow of what seemed permanent — including, in the first-century horizon, the temple-centered world that opposed the Messiah.

New Creation Reality

The Lord already reigns in the Son. The “new song” is not postponed until a different cosmos; it is the worship of a people who have seen His salvation revealed to the nations and the old adversarial order judged. Joy is the right response to a judgment that vindicates the kingdom.
