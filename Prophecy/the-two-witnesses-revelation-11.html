The Two Witnesses (Revelation 11)

The two witnesses prophesy in the city that is spiritually Sodom and Egypt — where the Lord was crucified — then are killed and raised as judgment shakes that world.

The Scene

Revelation 11 measures the temple, leaves the outer court to the nations, and introduces two witnesses who prophesy 1,260 days, clothed in sackcloth. They have authority like Moses and Elijah — shutting the sky, turning waters to blood, striking the earth with plagues. When they finish, the beast kills them in the great city “spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, where their Lord was crucified” (Revelation 11:8).

That last clause anchors the geography. The city in view is not an abstract future capital first; it is the city of the crucifixion — Jerusalem under prophetic indictment.

Witness in the Crisis Generation

The church’s testimony in Judea and Jerusalem, the prophetic lawsuit against the old order, and the pattern of rejected messengers fit the period before AD 70. The witnesses are not required to be two isolated celebrities; the vision communicates authorized testimony, Mosaic-Elijah power language, and martyrdom in the hostile city.

Their resurrection and ascent, followed by earthquake and the city’s partial collapse, signal vindication and impending full judgment — the same arc as the wider book: witness, suffering, divine reversal, the end of the persecuting center.

Where their Lord was crucified.
Any reading that severs the vision from that city must explain away the text’s own marker.

Nearness Language

Revelation frames its visions with “soon” and “near.” The two witnesses belong inside that frame. They are part of the story of the last days of the old age, not a detachable script for a different millennium as the primary referent.

New Creation Reality

The point for readers now is not to schedule two future individuals before history can continue. It is to recognize that God vindicated His witnesses and judged the city that killed them and their Lord. The testimony stands; the old court that condemned them does not.

Read: Revelation 11; 1:1–3; 22:6–10; Luke 13:33–35 (WEB)
