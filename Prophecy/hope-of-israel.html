The Hope of Israel

Paul’s “hope of Israel” is the resurrection hope and the Messiah’s fulfillment of the promises — not a rival program beside the gospel to the nations.

Paul on Trial

Before the council and before Agrippa, Paul frames his case as the hope of Israel: the promise made to the fathers, the resurrection of the dead, the Messiah who suffered and rose (Acts 23:6; 24:15; 26:6–8, 22–23). He is not on trial for inventing a Gentile religion; he is on trial for claiming that Israel’s own hope has arrived in Jesus.

The irony is sharp: the leadership that claims to guard Israel’s hope rejects the One in whom the hope is fulfilled, while Paul insists he worships the God of the fathers according to the Way.

Hope = Promise Kept

The hope of Israel is not “a future ethnic phase after the church is removed.” It is that God raises the dead, keeps covenant, and seats the Son of David. The nations’ inclusion is part of that same hope (Acts 26:23) — light to the people and to the Gentiles.

Same hope. Same Messiah. Same resurrection claim.
Paul does not abandon Israel’s story; he announces its climax.

After the Transition

When the Temple falls, the hope does not fall with it. The hope was never the permanence of the stone house. The remnant believes; the nations stream in; the one new man carries the fulfilled promises forward.

New Creation Reality

If you are in Christ, you share the hope of Israel in its fulfilled form — resurrection life, Spirit, and citizenship in the unshakable Kingdom — without needing a second map that restarts the old administration.

Read: Acts 23:6; 24:14–15; 26:6–23; Romans 15:8–12 (WEB)
