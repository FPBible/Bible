Understanding Jesus

What Is the Good News?

The Gospel is an announcement of life restored, not a system of performance.

The word "religion" often evokes images of rigid rules and the pressure to perform. It frames spiritual life as a transaction where acceptance is earned through behavior, pushing peace into an uncertain future.

The Gospel—literally "good news"—stands in direct contrast. It is not a list of demands, but a historical announcement of what God has already done to rescue humanity from the patterns of brokenness that separate us from Him.

1. Designed for Connection

Humanity was not created to exist on a hamster wheel of performance. We were designed for genuine peace, lasting purpose, and unconditional connection with our Creator.

2. The Fracture Within

The Bible uses the term sin to describe the declaration of independence from God. This choice resulted in spiritual fragmentation—evidenced by fear, shame, and isolation—creating a gap that human effort cannot bridge.

Our human efforts can manage the outward symptoms of brokenness, but they cannot heal the deep spiritual separation within.

3. The Rescue Operation

God bridged the gap. In the person of Jesus, God entered our history to absorb the weight of human failure. His life, death, and resurrection constitute the finished work of salvation.

"It is Finished"

Jesus' final words signify that the debt is paid and the barrier is removed. The door to God is wide open, requiring no further religious climbing.

4. A Gift, Not a Wage

Salvation is received through trust, not earned through merit. It is an invitation to inhabit a restored life right now, characterized by security in God's grace rather than fear of judgment.

A Question for Reflection

If the work is truly finished, what would it look like today to cease your striving and live from the reality of your acceptance in Christ?
