The Remnant of Israel

God keeps a remnant by grace; the remnant is saved into the one new man — not held in a separate covenant track beside the gospel.

Remnant in the Prophets and Paul

Isaiah’s remnant is the holy seed that survives judgment (Isaiah 6:13; 10:20–22). Paul cites the remnant logic in Romans 9–11: not all who are descended from Israel belong to Israel; a remnant is chosen by grace, not by works (Romans 11:5–6).

In the first century the remnant is the portion of Israel that receives the Messiah — the apostles, the thousands at Pentecost, the churches in Judea who believe. They are not a footnote; they are the proof that God’s word has not failed.

Remnant and the Nations

The remnant does not remain a sealed ethnic enclave while the nations wait for a different plan. The same gospel grafts in Gentiles as fellow heirs. The olive tree of Romans 11 has natural branches and wild branches; faith is the condition of standing, and arrogance is forbidden on either side.

Grace remnant. One tree. One gospel.
The remnant is defined by faith in the Messiah, not by preserving the wall.

AD 70 and the Remnant

Judgment falls on the impenitent order. The remnant is not destroyed with the house; it is already located in Christ. Historical catastrophe is not the erasure of Israel’s promises; it is the end of the administration that opposed their fulfillment in the Son.

New Creation Reality

To be part of God’s people is to be in the remnant-and-nations body Paul describes — the one new man. There is no second line of standing outside the Messiah for those who refuse Him.

Read: Isaiah 10:20–22; Romans 9–11; Ephesians 2:11–22 (WEB)
