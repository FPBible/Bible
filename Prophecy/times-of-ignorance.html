Times of Ignorance

Paul’s Areopagus line — God overlooked times of ignorance, but now commands all people everywhere to repent — marks the turning of the ages in the gospel’s arrival.

Acts 17

In Athens Paul declares the unknown God, the Creator who does not live in temples made by hands, and who made from one man every nation. He then says: the times of ignorance God overlooked, but now He commands all people everywhere to repent, because He has fixed a day on which He will judge the world in righteousness by a man He has appointed, and of this He has given assurance by raising Him from the dead (Acts 17:30–31).

“Now” is apostolic present. The resurrection has occurred. The command to repent is universal in scope because the risen man is Lord of all.

Ignorance and Revelation

Ignorance is not innocence. It is the relative divine patience toward nations outside the Sinai administration before the climactic revelation in Christ. With the gospel announced, the plea of not knowing is stripped of force. The same God who ordered the former seasons has spoken in a Son.

Overlooked then. Commanded now. Assured by resurrection.
The hinge is Christ, not a later calendar still empty of content.

Judgment Day in Context

Paul’s fixed day of judgment through the risen man fits the wider apostolic warning of a coming crisis on the old world and the accountability of all nations to the Lord. For the first-century horizon, that includes the Day on Jerusalem and the ongoing rule of the Son. Futurism is not required to make the Areopagus sermon coherent.

New Creation Reality

Repentance remains the call. Idols still fall. The times of ignorance are not a model for modern indifference; they are the backdrop that makes the “now” of the gospel urgent.
