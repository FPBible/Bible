The Divine Council

Scripture’s divine council imagery frames Yahweh’s rule among the heavenly host — and the collapse of rival claims when the Son is exalted and the old prosecutor’s court ends.

Council Imagery in Scripture

Psalm 82, 1 Kings 22, Job 1–2, and related texts picture God amid a heavenly assembly. The point is not polytheism; it is that Yahweh rules, judges the gods/powers, and is not a tribal deity among equals. Deuteronomy’s language of nations and hosts sits in the same world of discourse.

In that frame, “satan” functions as accuser/adversary in the legal-covenant sense — a role within the old order’s prosecutorial logic, not a second god in cosmic dualism. (See the Hub’s fuller treatment of Satan as covenantal accuser.)

Christ and the Powers

The New Testament announces the exaltation of the Son above every rule and authority (Ephesians 1:20–22; Colossians 2:15). Principalities are disarmed at the cross. The gospel is public truth before the powers, not a private myth.

As the old covenant administration ends, the court that used Law as a perpetual indictment loses its house. Accusation as system is answered by the Advocate and by the removal of the Temple order.

Yahweh rules the council. The Son is exalted. The accuser’s system falls.
No dualism — covenant fulfillment.

New Creation Reality

Believers are not spectators of an undecided war in heaven. They live under the reign of the risen Christ, with access the old courts could not sell and the old prosecutor could not finally block.

Read: Psalm 82; Job 1–2; Ephesians 1:20–23; Colossians 2:13–15 (WEB)
