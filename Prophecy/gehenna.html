Gehenna and Covenant Judgment

The Valley of Hinnom had a history: child-sacrifice, Josiah’s defilement, Jeremiah’s “valley of slaughter.” Jesus picks up a loaded place-name.

Plain

Jeremiah said the valley would be called the Valley of Slaughter when Jerusalem fell. Bodies, fire, worms — that is siege-and-aftermath language the first hearers already knew.

In Matthew

Matthew 5, 10, and 18 use Gehenna in discipleship warnings aimed at a people heading toward that city’s end. Reading every Gehenna text as a cosmology of souls skips the lawsuit Jesus is filing against that generation.

First-Century Horizon

When Jerusalem burned, the idiom became visible. That does not empty moral seriousness. It locates the warning where Jesus located it.

Scripture

  • Jeremiah 7:30–34; 19:1–13
  • Isaiah 66:24
  • Matthew 5:22, 29–30; 10:28; 23:33

New Creation Reality

The New Creation is not lived under a valley-threat from a city that already fell. Holiness now is life in the unshakable kingdom.
