Psalm 2

Enthronement of God’s Son — rebellious rulers shattered

Key theme. Nations rage; kings take counsel against the LORD and His Anointed. God answers by installing His King on Zion and declaring, “You are My Son; today I have begotten You.” The Son is to ask the nations as inheritance; He breaks rebellious rule with a rod of iron.

Enthronement, not delay

Psalm 2 is royal enthronement liturgy. The crisis is rebellion against Yahweh’s king. The resolution is not a distant postponed session; it is the decree that installs the Son and warns the rulers to kiss the Son lest He be angry. The psalm’s pressure is present-tense kingship confronting present-tense revolt.

Preterist connection

The New Testament cites Psalm 2 heavily. Acts 4:25–28 reads the conspiracy against Jesus as the nations’ raging. Acts 13:33 applies “You are My Son; today I have begotten You” to the resurrection. Revelation 2:27 takes up the rod-of-iron rule for the Messiah’s authority.

In fulfilled reading, Christ takes His throne at the resurrection and ascension and administers that reign through the apostolic age toward the public crushing of the persecuting Old Covenant authorities — and the humbling of imperial powers — in the first-century horizon culminating in AD 70. The psalm is not primarily a chart for a still-future political program; it is the script of the Son’s already-decreed reign and the folly of fighting it.

New Creation Reality

The Son is installed. The question for every generation after is not whether God will eventually get around to enthroning Him, but whether we rage against the decree or take refuge in Him. Blessing belongs to those who trust the Son — now.
