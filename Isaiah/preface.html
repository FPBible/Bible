Preface to Isaiah

The non-negotiable rules for reading the Prophet.

Covenant Lawsuit Prophetic Idiom The Servant New Creation

Isaiah supplies the vocabulary the New Testament uses for Messiah, Gentile inclusion, the judgment of the old system, and the New Covenant. If we misread how he speaks, we will misread what he says.

1. The Context: A Covenant Lawsuit

Isaiah is a covenant prosecutor addressing Judah and Israel in the eighth century — not a forecast of twenty-first-century geopolitics. Judgment language tracks Deuteronomy 28 and Leviticus 26. Those judgments prototype the close of the Old Covenant system in AD 70.

2. The Language: Prophetic Idiom (De-Creation)

Stars going dark, earth staggering, heavens rolled up — this is the collapse of a government, a religion, or an empire, not astrophysics. The same idiom is in Matthew 24 for the fall of Jerusalem.

3. The Trajectory: From Shadow to Substance

  • Zion rises from a hill in Jerusalem to the centre of New Covenant instruction (Isa 2).
  • Israel is condensed into the faithful Servant — Jesus Christ (Isa 49).
  • The nations are brought in (Isa 11).

The substance has arrived in Christ. The promises are not dragged back into a rebuilt stone house.

4. The Goal: The New Creation

“Heaven and earth” is the Hebrew idiom for the Temple-centred Old Covenant world. “New heavens and new earth” (Isa 65–66) is the New Covenant order.

This is not a later rebuilt planet. Isa 65:20 uses ordinary aging inside that world because the subject is covenant order, not the last enemy. The covering and the rebuke end (Isa 25:7–8). Death as condemnation ends. The Servant has made intercession (Isa 53), and all flesh can worship God freely through the Spirit.

5. Reader Responsibility

Look for Jesus, the cross, the nations, the remnant. Isaiah does not end in a two-thousand-year parenthesis. It ends in the present Kingdom.
