Hebrews 5

The True High Priest · Learned Obedience · A Call to Maturity

1. Immediate Context

Hebrews 5 introduces the heart of the letter’s argument: Jesus is not merely another priest — He belongs to an entirely different order. But the author pauses mid-argument. The problem is not lack of information; it is lack of readiness. The audience is stagnating at the very moment they need clarity and courage to leave the old system behind.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1For every high priest, being taken from among men, is appointed for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins.

2The high priest can deal gently with those who are ignorant and going astray, because he himself is also surrounded with weakness.

3Because of this, he must offer sacrifices for sins for the people, as well as for himself.

4Nobody takes this honor on himself, but he is called by God, just like Aaron was.

5So also Christ didn’t glorify himself to be made a high priest, but it was he who said to him, “You are my Son. Today I have become your father.”

6As he says also in another place, “You are a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek.”

7He, in the days of his flesh, having offered up prayers and petitions with strong crying and tears to him who was able to save him from death, and having been heard for his godly fear,

8though he was a Son, yet learned obedience by the things which he suffered.

9Having been made perfect, he became to all of those who obey him the author of eternal salvation,

10named by God a high priest after the order of Melchizedek.

11About him we have many words to say, and hard to interpret, seeing you have become dull of hearing.

12For although by this time you should be teachers, you again need to have someone teach you the rudiments of the first principles of the revelations of God. You have come to need milk, and not solid food.

13For everyone who lives on milk is not experienced in the word of righteousness, for he is a baby.

14But solid food is for those who are full grown, who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern good and evil.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Levitical priests are appointed from among men, subject to weakness, offering for their own sins too.
  • Christ is appointed by God as Son and as priest forever after Melchizedek — not by self-promotion.
  • The argument pauses: the audience needs solid food, not perpetual milk, at the crisis of the ages.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

Weakness was the old priesthood’s design limit

Aaronic priests sympathise because they share the same condition — and must offer for themselves. That system was commanded and real. It was also structurally incomplete. Hebrews is not mocking Levitical ministry; it is showing why it cannot be final.

Appointed by oath, not self-promotion

The Son does not grasp the honour. The Father who said “You are my Son” also said “You are a priest forever after Melchizedek.” Psalm 2 and Psalm 110 converge on one person. The next chapters will unpack what that order costs the Levitical claim.

Dullness at the hinge is the real crisis

The argument pauses because the audience is not ready. Milk versus solid food is not an IQ test. It is readiness to face Melchizedek and the New Covenant when retreat still feels safer. Maturity means senses trained to distinguish the good of pressing on from the evil of going back.

  • Melchizedek — non-Levitical forever priesthood (developed in ch. 7).
  • Milk / solid food — readiness for the transition argument, not mere intellect.

9. New Creation Reality

Your High Priest is not weak like Aaron’s line. He is the perfected Son who learned obedience and became the source of eternal salvation. Maturity means eating solid food — recognizing that the old order cannot compete with Him — and refusing to stay on milk when the transition demands courage.

10. Reflection

Where are you still choosing “milk” — familiar religious safety — when the letter is calling you to solid food about a better priesthood?
