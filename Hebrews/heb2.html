Hebrews 2

Pay Attention · The Son Shared Our Flesh · Bringing Many Sons to Glory

1. Immediate Context

Chapter 1 established the Son’s supremacy. Chapter 2 turns to the readers: therefore we must pay closer attention. The danger is drift — sliding back under pressure — not a sudden philosophical rejection. The Son’s incarnation and death are presented as the means by which He brings many sons to glory and destroys the one who held the power of death.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1Therefore we ought to pay greater attention to the things that were heard, lest we drift away.

2For if the word spoken through angels proved steadfast, and every transgression and disobedience received a just penalty,

3how will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation—which at the first having been spoken through the Lord, was confirmed to us by those who heard,

4God also testifying with them, both by signs and wonders, by various works of power, and by gifts of the Holy Spirit, according to his own will?

5For he didn’t subject the world to come, of which we speak, to angels.

6But one has somewhere testified, saying, “What is man, that you think of him? Or the son of man, that you care for him?

7You made him a little lower than the angels. You crowned him with glory and honor.

8You have put all things in subjection under his feet.” For in that he subjected all things to him, he left nothing that is not subject to him. But now we don’t yet see all things subjected to him.

9But we see him who has been made a little lower than the angels, Jesus, because of the suffering of death crowned with glory and honor, that by the grace of God he should taste of death for everyone.

10For it became him, for whom are all things, and through whom are all things, in bringing many children to glory, to make the author of their salvation perfect through sufferings.

11For both he who sanctifies and those who are sanctified are all from one, for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brothers,

12saying, “I will declare your name to my brothers. Among the congregation I will sing your praise.”

13Again, “I will put my trust in him.” Again, “Behold, here I am with the children whom God has given me.”

14Since then the children have shared in flesh and blood, he also himself in the same way partook of the same, that through death he might bring to nothing him who had the power of death, that is, the devil,

15and might deliver all of them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.

16For most certainly, he doesn’t give help to angels, but he gives help to the offspring of Abraham.

17Therefore he was obligated in all things to be made like his brothers, that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make atonement for the sins of the people.

18For in that he himself has suffered being tempted, he is able to help those who are tempted.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • If the old word spoken through angels was binding, neglect of the Son’s salvation is more serious — not less.
  • The Son tasted death for everyone and is not ashamed to call believers brothers.
  • Help is for Abraham’s seed; the devil’s death-power is broken for those He delivers.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

Drift is the first danger

Salvation announced by the Lord, confirmed by hearers, and borne witness by signs must not be neglected. The comparison with the message spoken through angels raises the stakes: greater revelation means greater accountability.

World subjected to the Son of Man

Psalm 8 is applied to Jesus: made lower than the angels for a little while, then crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death. The subjection of all things is not denied; what is not yet seen in full public display does not cancel the crowning already accomplished in His suffering and exaltation.

Pioneer of salvation through suffering

It was fitting that God, in bringing many sons to glory, perfect the pioneer of their salvation through sufferings. Sanctifier and sanctified are of one; He calls them brothers. The incarnation is not ornamental — He partook of flesh and blood to destroy the devil’s death-hold and free those enslaved by fear of death.

Merciful high priest

He had to be made like His brothers in every respect to become a merciful and faithful high priest in the service of God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people. Because He suffered when tempted, He is able to help those who are being tempted — including readers tempted to quit under covenant pressure.

  • Drift — gradual neglect under pressure, not only dramatic apostasy.
  • Pioneer / captain — the one who goes first into salvation’s fulfillment.
  • Fear of death — bondage broken by His death and priesthood.

9. New Creation Reality

The Son is not ashamed of His brothers. Help is present tense for the tempted. You do not return to fear under a system He already rendered obsolete; you hold to a salvation greater than the word that came through angels.

10. Reflection

Where is “drift” showing up — not as open denial, but as neglect of the greater salvation under pressure to retreat?
