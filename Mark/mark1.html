Mark 1

1. Immediate Context

Mark opens with the beginning of the Good News of Jesus Christ, the Son of God. John the Baptist prepares the way in the wilderness. Jesus is baptized, the Spirit descends, the Father speaks, and the Spirit drives him into the wilderness to be tempted. After John’s arrest, Jesus comes into Galilee announcing that the time is fulfilled and the Kingdom of God is at hand. He calls the first disciples and begins a ministry of authoritative teaching, casting out unclean spirits, and healing.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 The beginning of the Good News of Jesus Christ, the Son of God. 2 As it is written in the prophets, “Behold, I send my messenger before your face, who will prepare your way before you: 3 the voice of one crying in the wilderness, ‘Make ready the way of the Lord! Make his paths straight!’ ”

4 John came baptizing in the wilderness and preaching the baptism of repentance for forgiveness of sins. 5 All the country of Judea and all those of Jerusalem went out to him. They were baptized by him in the Jordan river, confessing their sins. 6 John was clothed with camel’s hair and a leather belt around his waist. He ate locusts and wild honey. 7 He preached, saying, “After me comes he who is mightier than I, the strap of whose sandals I am not worthy to stoop down and loosen. 8 I baptized you in water, but he will baptize you in the Holy Spirit.”

9 In those days, Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee, and was baptized by John in the Jordan. 10 Immediately coming up from the water, he saw the heavens parting and the Spirit descending on him like a dove. 11 A voice came out of the sky, “You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

12 Immediately the Spirit drove him out into the wilderness. 13 He was there in the wilderness forty days, tempted by Satan. He was with the wild animals; and the angels were serving him.

14 Now after John was taken into custody, Jesus came into Galilee, preaching the Good News of God’s Kingdom, 15 and saying, “The time is fulfilled, and God’s Kingdom is at hand! Repent, and believe in the Good News.”

16 Passing along by the sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and Andrew, the brother of Simon, casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen. 17 Jesus said to them, “Come after me, and I will make you into fishers for men.” 18 Immediately they left their nets, and followed him. 19 Going on a little further from there, he saw James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother, who were also in the boat mending the nets. 20 Immediately he called them, and they left their father, Zebedee, in the boat with the hired servants, and went after him.

21 They went into Capernaum, and immediately on the Sabbath day he entered into the synagogue and taught. 22 They were astonished at his teaching, for he taught them as having authority, and not as the scribes. 23 Immediately there was in their synagogue a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out, 24 saying, “Ha! What do we have to do with you, Jesus, you Nazarene? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are: the Holy One of God!” 25 Jesus rebuked him, saying, “Be quiet, and come out of him!” 26 The unclean spirit, convulsing him and crying with a loud voice, came out of him. 27 They were all amazed, so that they questioned among themselves, saying, “What is this? What can this new doctrine be? For with authority he commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey him!” 28 The report of him went out immediately into all the region of Galilee and its surrounding area.

29 Immediately, when they had come out of the synagogue, they came into the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John. 30 Now Simon’s wife’s mother lay sick with a fever, and immediately they told him about her. 31 He came and took her by the hand and raised her up. The fever left her immediately, and she served them.

32 At evening, when the sun had set, they brought to him all who were sick and those who were possessed by demons. 33 All the city was gathered together at the door. 34 He healed many who were sick with various diseases and cast out many demons. He didn’t allow the demons to speak, because they knew him.

35 Early in the morning, while it was still dark, he rose up and went out, and departed into a deserted place, and prayed there. 36 Simon and those who were with him searched for him. 37 They found him and told him, “Everyone is looking for you.” 38 He said to them, “Let’s go elsewhere into the next towns, that I may preach there also, because I came out for this reason.” 39 He went into their synagogues throughout all Galilee, preaching and casting out demons.

40 A leper came to him, begging him, kneeling down to him, and saying to him, “If you want to, you can make me clean.” 41 Being moved with compassion, he stretched out his hand, and touched him, and said to him, “I want to. Be made clean.” 42 When he had said this, immediately the leprosy departed from him and he was made clean. 43 He strictly warned him and immediately sent him out, 44 and said to him, “See that you say nothing to anybody, but go show yourself to the priest and offer for your cleansing the things which Moses commanded, for a testimony to them.” 45 But he went out, and began to proclaim it much, and to spread about the matter, so that Jesus could no more openly enter into a city, but was outside in desert places. People came to him from everywhere.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • The time is fulfilled · God’s Kingdom is at hand (v. 15): The appointed time has arrived; the Kingdom is near and present in the ministry of the Son.
  • He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit (v. 8): John’s water baptism points to the greater baptism Jesus administers.
  • You are my beloved Son (v. 11): The Father publicly identifies the Son at the opening of the public ministry.
  • Authority over unclean spirits (v. 27): The Kingdom advances by immediate, effective command over the demonic.
  • I came out for this reason (v. 38): Preaching the Good News stands as the stated purpose of the mission.

4. Annotations

  • Immediately (throughout): Mark’s characteristic urgency; the narrative presses forward without delay.
  • The Holy One of God (v. 24): The unclean spirit confesses Jesus’ identity while many still do not see it.
  • For a testimony to them (v. 44): The leper is sent to the priest under the Mosaic command, bearing witness inside the old order.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

1) The Prophetic Beginning and the Messenger

Mark does not open with genealogy or birth narrative. He opens with the beginning of the Good News of Jesus Christ, the Son of God. The prophets speak first. Malachi’s messenger and Isaiah’s voice in the wilderness converge in John. John comes baptizing in the wilderness and preaching a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins. Crowds from Judea and Jerusalem go out to him, confess their sins, and are baptized in the Jordan. John’s clothing and diet mark him as a prophet in the line of Elijah. His message is not self-referential. After me comes one mightier than I; I am not worthy to loosen the strap of his sandals. I baptized you in water; he will baptize you in the Holy Spirit. The way of the Lord is prepared. The messenger’s work is transitional. It gathers a people in repentance and points beyond itself to the One who brings the greater baptism and the Kingdom itself.

2) Baptism, Torn Heavens, and the Father’s Voice

Jesus comes from Nazareth of Galilee and is baptized by John in the Jordan. Immediately, as he comes up from the water, he sees the heavens parting and the Spirit descending on him like a dove. A voice comes out of the sky: You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. The heavens are torn open. The Spirit rests on the Son. The Father speaks identification and delight. This is not a private vision for Jesus alone; Mark records it as the public inauguration of the ministry. The same Spirit who descends will drive him into the wilderness. The same Father who speaks will remain the source of the Son’s authority. The baptism places Jesus inside the repentant movement John has gathered, yet the torn heavens and the voice set him apart as the unique Son. The old order’s heavens are opened; the Spirit is given; the Son is named.

3) Wilderness Testing and Angelic Service

Immediately the Spirit drives him out into the wilderness. He is there forty days, tempted by Satan. He is with the wild animals, and the angels serve him. The testing is real. The adversary is present. The animals and the angels frame a scene of both danger and provision. The forty days recall Israel’s wilderness period, yet here the true Son stands where the nation failed. The Spirit who descended at the Jordan is the same Spirit who drives him into the place of testing. Temptation is not an interruption of the mission; it is the first movement of the mission under the Spirit’s direction. The angels serve him. The wilderness does not defeat the Son. He emerges from it ready to announce the Kingdom.

4) The Central Announcement: Time Fulfilled, Kingdom at Hand

After John is taken into custody, Jesus comes into Galilee preaching the Good News of God’s Kingdom and saying, The time is fulfilled, and God’s Kingdom is at hand! Repent, and believe in the Good News. This is the hinge of the chapter and of the Gospel. The appointed time has arrived. The Kingdom is not a distant hope deferred to a later age. It is at hand in the person and ministry of the Son. The call is repentance and faith in the Good News itself. The announcement is made in Galilee after the messenger has been silenced. The transition is underway. John’s arrest marks the shift from preparation to the public advance of the Kingdom. Everything that follows in Mark 1—the call of disciples, the synagogue authority, the expulsion of unclean spirits, the healings, the cleansing of the leper—flows from this declaration. The time is fulfilled. The Kingdom is present and advancing.

5) The Call of the First Disciples

Passing along by the sea of Galilee, Jesus sees Simon and Andrew casting a net. Come after me, and I will make you into fishers for men. Immediately they leave their nets and follow him. A little further he sees James and John mending nets with their father Zebedee. Immediately he calls them; they leave their father in the boat with the hired servants and go after him. The response is immediate. Nets and family business are left. The call creates a new company. These men will become the core of the apostolic witness that carries the Good News through the remaining years of the old order and into the New Creation reality established by the cross, resurrection, and the end of the temple system. The call is not to a future Kingdom still far off; it is to follow the One in whom the Kingdom has drawn near.

6) Authority in Capernaum and Over Unclean Spirits

They enter Capernaum. On the Sabbath Jesus teaches in the synagogue. The people are astonished: he teaches as one having authority, and not as the scribes. Immediately a man with an unclean spirit cries out, Ha! What do we have to do with you, Jesus, you Nazarene? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are: the Holy One of God! Jesus rebukes him: Be quiet, and come out of him! The spirit convulses the man, cries with a loud voice, and comes out. Amazement spreads. What is this? A new teaching with authority—he commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey him. The report goes out into all the region of Galilee. The Kingdom’s advance is not merely verbal. It is demonstrated in effective authority over the powers that had held people. The unclean spirit recognizes the Holy One of God and knows the threat. The people see the authority. The old order’s scribes taught without this power. The Son commands, and the spirits obey.

7) Healing, Compassion, and the Priority of Preaching

From the synagogue they go to the house of Simon and Andrew. Simon’s mother-in-law lies sick with a fever. Jesus takes her by the hand and raises her up. The fever leaves immediately, and she serves them. At evening, when the sun has set, the whole city gathers at the door. He heals many who are sick with various diseases and casts out many demons. He does not allow the demons to speak, because they know him. Early the next morning, while it is still dark, he rises, goes out to a deserted place, and prays. Simon and the others search for him and report that everyone is looking for him. Jesus answers: Let’s go elsewhere into the next towns, that I may preach there also, because I came out for this reason. He goes throughout Galilee, preaching in their synagogues and casting out demons. Healing and deliverance accompany the mission, yet the stated purpose is preaching. The Good News of the Kingdom must be announced from town to town. Compassion is real—he touches, he raises, he heals—but the mission is not reduced to the alleviation of symptoms. The Kingdom is proclaimed, and the powers of the old age are displaced.

8) The Cleansing of the Leper and Testimony Inside the Old Order

A leper comes, begs, kneels, and says, If you want to, you can make me clean. Moved with compassion, Jesus stretches out his hand, touches him, and says, I want to. Be made clean. Immediately the leprosy departs and the man is made clean. Jesus strictly warns him, sends him out, and tells him to say nothing to anyone, but to go show himself to the priest and offer for his cleansing the things Moses commanded, for a testimony to them. The touch is significant. Under the old order, contact with a leper rendered a person unclean. Jesus touches and cleanses. The man is sent back into the system of the law as a living testimony. The old order still stands at this point in the narrative; the priest and the Mosaic offerings remain the public structure. Yet the cleansing itself is the work of the Kingdom that has drawn near. The testimony is given inside the structures that are already being surpassed by the presence and power of the Son.

9) The Arc of Mark 1 in the Covenant Transition

Mark 1 sets the entire Gospel in motion under one declaration: the time is fulfilled, and God’s Kingdom is at hand. The messenger prepares the way. The Son is baptized, identified by the Father, tested in the wilderness, and sent to preach. Disciples are called. Authority is displayed over teaching, unclean spirits, disease, and uncleanness. Preaching remains the stated reason for the mission. All of this occurs within the generation that will see the cross, the resurrection, the outpouring of the Spirit, the apostolic mission, and the end of the temple order in AD 70. The Kingdom announced as at hand is the Kingdom that advances through that generation and is established as the New Creation reality in which the people of God now live. Physical death continues; covenantal death and the condemnation of the old order do not. The beloved Son has been named. The time has been fulfilled. The Good News has begun, and its fulfillment in the first-century transition is complete. Those who hear the announcement today stand on the far side of that fulfillment, called to the same repentance and faith in the Good News that has already arrived and has already done its decisive work.

8. New Creation Reality

The time has been fulfilled. The Kingdom has come near. The beloved Son has been identified, tested, and has begun the ministry of authority and compassion. We live on the far side of that announcement, called to the same repentance and faith in the Good News that has been completed in the cross, resurrection, and the end of the old age. The Spirit who descended on the Son is the Spirit who indwells the people of the New Creation. Authority over the powers of the old order belongs to the risen Lord. The call remains: repent and believe the Good News.
