Mark 8

1. Immediate Context

Jesus feeds four thousand. The Pharisees demand a sign; none will be given to this generation. Peter confesses that Jesus is the Christ. Jesus begins to teach that the Son of Man must suffer, be rejected, be killed, and rise after three days. He calls the crowd to take up the cross. Whoever is ashamed of him in this adulterous and sinful generation, the Son of Man will be ashamed of when he comes in his Father’s glory with the holy angels.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1–10 Jesus has compassion on the multitude and feeds about four thousand with seven loaves and a few fish. Seven baskets of fragments remain.

11–13 The Pharisees seek a sign from heaven. Jesus sighs: “Why does this generation seek a sign? Most certainly I tell you, no sign will be given to this generation.”

14–21 He warns of the yeast of the Pharisees and of Herod. The disciples misunderstand about bread. “Don’t you understand yet?”

22–26 A blind man at Bethsaida is healed in stages; he sees clearly.

27–30 At Caesarea Philippi: “Who do men say that I am?” Peter: “You are the Christ.” He commands them to tell no one.

31–33 He begins to teach that the Son of Man must suffer many things, be rejected by the elders, chief priests, and scribes, be killed, and after three days rise again. Peter rebukes him; Jesus rebukes Peter: “Get behind me, Satan!”

34–38 “Whoever wants to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me… For whoever will be ashamed of me and of my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, the Son of Man also will be ashamed of him when he comes in his Father’s glory with the holy angels.”

Source: ebible.org

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • No sign will be given to this generation (v. 12): Explicit address to the generation then present.
  • The Son of Man must suffer… and after three days rise (v. 31): The necessary path is announced.
  • This adulterous and sinful generation (v. 38): The coming of the Son of Man in glory is tied to accountability within that generation.

4. Annotations

  • You are the Christ (v. 29): The confession that marks the turning point of the Gospel.
  • Take up his cross (v. 34): Discipleship is defined by the way of the cross before the cross has yet occurred.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

The confession at Caesarea Philippi is immediately followed by the first passion prediction. The Christ is the Son of Man who must suffer, be rejected, be killed, and rise. Discipleship is cross-shaped. The warning about being ashamed is directed at “this adulterous and sinful generation.” The coming of the Son of Man in the Father’s glory with the holy angels is the horizon of accountability for that generation. The Kingdom’s King will come in glory; the question is whether that generation will be found ashamed of him or not.

8. New Creation Reality

The Christ has suffered, been rejected, been killed, and has risen. The generation that was warned has faced the coming. We live as those who take up the cross after the cross, unashamed of the One who has already come in glory to judge and to save.

9. Reflection

Where does the call to deny self, take up the cross, and follow still confront the desire to save one’s life on one’s own terms?
