Mark 12

1. Immediate Context

Jesus tells the parable of the vineyard: the beloved son is killed by the farmers; the lord of the vineyard will come and destroy them and give the vineyard to others. The stone the builders rejected becomes the head of the corner. Questions about taxes, resurrection, and the greatest commandment follow. The poor widow gives all she had.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1–12 A man planted a vineyard and rented it to farmers. He sent servants; they were beaten and killed. He sent his beloved son last. They killed him and cast him out. “He will come and destroy the farmers, and will give the vineyard to others.” “The stone which the builders rejected was made the head of the corner.” They perceived he spoke the parable against them.

13–17 Taxes to Caesar: “Give to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s.”

18–27 Sadducees and the resurrection. God is not the God of the dead, but of the living.

28–34 The greatest commandment: love God with all the heart, soul, mind, and strength; love your neighbor as yourself. “You are not far from God’s Kingdom.”

35–37 How is the Christ David’s son if David calls him Lord?

38–40 Beware of the scribes who devour widows’ houses.

41–44 The poor widow puts in two small coins — all she had to live on.

Source: ebible.org

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • He will come and destroy the farmers (v. 9): The coming of the lord of the vineyard in judgment is announced against the leaders who kill the son.
  • The stone the builders rejected (v. 10–11): Already identified; the rejection is underway, the exaltation certain.
  • You are not far from God’s Kingdom (v. 34): The Kingdom is near; the scribe who answers wisely is close to it.

4. Annotations

  • Beloved son (v. 6): Echo of the baptism and transfiguration voice.
  • Give the vineyard to others (v. 9): Transfer of stewardship away from the unfaithful tenants.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

The parable is transparent: the farmers are the leaders; the servants are the prophets; the beloved son is Jesus. They will kill him. The lord of the vineyard will come and destroy them and give the vineyard to others. The rejected stone becomes the head of the corner. The judgment announced in the parable is the same judgment that will leave not one stone of the temple upon another (13:2). The Kingdom is near enough that a wise scribe is “not far” from it.

8. New Creation Reality

The beloved son has been killed and has become the head of the corner. The unfaithful tenants have been judged. The vineyard has been given to others. We live as those who love God and neighbor under the lordship of the rejected and exalted Stone.

9. Reflection

How does the certainty that the lord of the vineyard has already come and judged reshape the way you read the “coming” language of the rest of the New Testament?
