Mark 11

1. Immediate Context

Jesus enters Jerusalem on a young donkey amid cries of “Hosanna!” He looks around the temple and withdraws. The next day he curses a barren fig tree. He cleanses the temple, quoting Isaiah and Jeremiah. The withered fig tree becomes a lesson in faith. The chief priests, scribes, and elders challenge his authority; he refuses to answer their question.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1–11 Near Jerusalem, Jesus sends for a young donkey. He sits on it. Many spread garments and branches. “Hosanna! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord! Blessed is the kingdom of our father David that is coming!” He enters the temple, looks around, and goes to Bethany.

12–14 Hungry, he finds a fig tree with leaves but no fruit. “May no one ever eat fruit from you again!”

15–19 He enters the temple and drives out the sellers and money changers. “My house will be called a house of prayer for all the nations. But you have made it a den of robbers!” The chief priests and scribes seek how to destroy him.

20–26 The fig tree is withered from the roots. “Have faith in God… whoever may tell this mountain, ‘Be taken up and cast into the sea’…” Forgiveness is required in prayer.

27–33 By what authority? Jesus asks about John’s baptism. They answer, “We don’t know.” Neither will he tell them.

Source: ebible.org

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • The barren fig tree (v. 12–14, 20–21): Symbolic judgment on fruitless Israel; the tree withers from the roots.
  • House of prayer for all nations · den of robbers (v. 17): Isaiah’s vision and Jeremiah’s indictment converge on the temple.
  • This mountain (v. 23): Spoken opposite the temple mount; the faith that moves mountains is the faith that believes what God has already determined.

4. Annotations

  • Hosanna (v. 9–10): “Save, we pray”; the crowd acclaims the coming kingdom of David.
  • Not the season for figs (v. 13): The tree advertises fruit by its leaves but has none; the image is of false appearance.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

The entry is messianic and public. The fig tree is acted parable: leaves without fruit, then withering from the roots. The temple is cleansed with the words of Isaiah and Jeremiah. The authority question is turned back on those who would not receive John’s baptism. Judgment on the fruitless and the corrupt is already enacted in sign; the full throwing down of the stones is still to come (13:2).

8. New Creation Reality

The King has entered. The barren tree has withered. The house that was a den of robbers has been judged. We live as those who pray in faith, forgive, and belong to the house of prayer for all nations that the old temple could not be.

9. Reflection

Where does the image of the leafy but barren fig tree still confront the appearance of fruit without the reality?
