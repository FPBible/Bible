Mark 5

The Stronger One gives inaugural signs of plunder, cleansing, and life — pledges consummated when the Old Covenant house fell.

2. Scripture Text (WEB)

1 They came to the other side of the sea, into the country of the Gadarenes. 2 When he had come out of the boat, immediately a man with an unclean spirit met him out of the tombs. 3 He lived in the tombs. Nobody could bind him any more, not even with chains, 4 because he had been often bound with fetters and chains, and the chains had been torn apart by him, and the fetters broken in pieces. Nobody had the strength to tame him. 5 Always, night and day, in the tombs and in the mountains, he was crying out, and cutting himself with stones. 6 When he saw Jesus from afar, he ran and bowed down to him, 7 and crying out with a loud voice, he said, “What have I to do with you, Jesus, you Son of the Most High God? I adjure you by God, don’t torment me.” 8 For he said to him, “Come out of the man, you unclean spirit!”

9 He asked him, “What is your name?” He said to him, “My name is Legion, for we are many.” 10 He begged him much that he would not send them away out of the country. 11 Now on the mountainside there was a great herd of pigs feeding. 12 All the demons begged him, saying, “Send us into the pigs, that we may enter into them.” 13 At once Jesus gave them permission. The unclean spirits came out and entered into the pigs. The herd of about two thousand rushed down the steep bank into the sea, and they were drowned in the sea. 14 Those who fed the pigs fled, and told it in the city and in the country.

The people came to see what it was that had happened. 15 They came to Jesus, and saw him who had been possessed by demons sitting, clothed, and in his right mind, even him who had the legion; and they were afraid. 16 Those who saw it declared to them what happened to him who was possessed by demons, and about the pigs. 17 They began to beg him to depart from their region.

18 As he was entering into the boat, he who had been possessed by demons begged him that he might be with him. 19 He didn’t allow him, but said to him, “Go to your house, to your friends, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you and how he had mercy on you.” 20 He went his way, and began to proclaim in Decapolis how Jesus had done great things for him, and everyone marveled.

21 When Jesus had crossed back over in the boat to the other side, a great multitude was gathered to him; and he was by the sea. 22 Behold, one of the rulers of the synagogue, Jairus by name, came; and seeing him, he fell at his feet 23 and begged him much, saying, “My little daughter is at the point of death. Please come and lay your hands on her, that she may be made healthy, and live.” 24 He went with him, and a great multitude followed him, and they pressed upon him on all sides.

25 A certain woman who had a discharge of blood for twelve years, 26 and had suffered many things by many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was no better, but rather grew worse, 27 having heard the things concerning Jesus, came up behind him in the crowd and touched his clothes. 28 For she said, “If I just touch his clothes, I will be made well.” 29 Immediately the flow of her blood was dried up, and she felt in her body that she was healed of her affliction.

30 Immediately Jesus, perceiving in himself that the power had gone out from him, turned around in the crowd and asked, “Who touched my clothes?” 31 His disciples said to him, “You see the multitude pressing against you, and you say, ‘Who touched me?’” 32 He looked around to see her who had done this thing. 33 But the woman, fearing and trembling, knowing what had been done to her, came and fell down before him, and told him all the truth. 34 He said to her, “Daughter, your faith has made you well. Go in peace, and be cured of your disease.”

35 While he was still speaking, people came from the synagogue ruler’s house, saying, “Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?” 36 But Jesus, when he heard the message spoken, immediately said to the ruler of the synagogue, “Don’t be afraid, only believe.” 37 He allowed no one to follow him except Peter, James, and John the brother of James. 38 He came to the synagogue ruler’s house, and he saw an uproar, weeping, and great wailing. 39 When he had entered in, he said to them, “Why do you make an uproar and weep? The child is not dead, but is asleep.”

40 They ridiculed him. But he, having put them all out, took the father of the child, her mother, and those who were with him, and went in where the child was lying. 41 Taking the child by the hand, he said to her, “Talitha cumi!” which means, being interpreted, “Girl, I tell you, get up!” 42 Immediately the girl rose up and walked, for she was twelve years old. They were amazed with great amazement. 43 He strictly ordered them that no one should know this, and commanded that something should be given her to eat.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • These works are AD 30–33 inaugural signs — pledges of the Kingdom consummated on that generation in AD 70.
  • Legion is Satanic bondage and Old Covenant curse, not Rome; Rome is God’s later rod against Jerusalem.
  • The two twelves pledge Israel’s cleansing from uncleanness and raising from covenantal death.
  • “Asleep” marks the resuscitation as a type of the corporate resurrection out of Hades by the Parousia.
  • Decapolis hears the word while the Temple still stands, inside the horizon that ends that age.

4. Immediate Context

Jesus has stilled the sea and now steps onto the eastern shore, then returns to synagogue territory. He addresses unclean spirits, a Gentile region, a hemorrhaging woman under Moses’ purity law, and Jairus — a ruler of the synagogue whose daughter has died. The covenant moment is the ministry of the Stronger One in the last days of the Mosaic age: the Kingdom is at hand, the strong man is being bound, and these acts are public pledges to that generation of what the coming judgment and New Covenant life will complete when the Temple falls.

5. Study Commentary

1) Sign and consummation must not be collapsed

Mark 5 belongs to the earthly ministry, about a generation before the fall of Jerusalem. The chapter is full of power: an unclean host driven out, a twelve-year flow stopped, a dead child raised. Those events are real. They are not the Parousia. They are inaugural signs — pledges and types — given to the people who first saw them. The consummation named by Jesus for that same generation is AD 70: the Temple down, the Mosaic administration ended, Hades emptied of the covenant dead who belong to him. To treat the Gadarene shore as if it were already the end of the age, or to treat AD 70 as if it were only another exorcism, mixes two moments the text keeps in sequence. First the Stronger One appears and plunders in part. Then the old house falls and the pledge is made good in full.

2) Legion is not Rome

The name Legion tells the scale of the bondage: many, organised, holding a man among the tombs. It does not identify the spirit-host with the Roman empire. Pagan empires in the prophets are the rod of the Lord’s anger against a covenant people in revolt. Rome will play that part against apostate Jerusalem in the war that ends the old age. That is not this scene. Here the actors are unclean spirits — Satanic bondage, spiritual uncleanness, the curse-order that the law could name and could not break. Chains and fetters are the old administration’s tools. They fail. The man lives in graves because Old Covenant quarantine manages death; it does not raise the dead or evict the unclean host.

Mark 3 already said a divided kingdom has an end and that the strong man must be bound before his house is plundered. Mark 5 is a public pledge of that binding. The spirits know the Son and plead against torment. Their time is not an endless cosmic war after history. Their time is the closing window of the Mosaic age. The Son permits the host to enter the pigs; the herd drowns in the sea. That drowning is a sign of uncleanness expelled, not a map of Roman legions going under in Galilee. Sea-memory in Israel is judgment-water and the end of an oppressor’s boast. As sign, it pledges the coming eviction of Satanic accusation and Old Covenant curse. As history, it is still AD 30–33. The consummation of that eviction is the transition that ends in AD 70, when the accuser’s house in the old order loses its standing and the death-administration of the law is taken away.

3) Decapolis in the transition generation

The delivered man wants to remain in the boat. Jesus sends him to house, friends, and Decapolis. The nations hear mercy while priesthood and Temple still operate in Jerusalem. That is audience-relevant mission, not a postponed world-program. Mark 13 places witness to the nations inside the same generation that sees the sanctuary thrown down. The man who had lived under unclean bondage becomes a herald on Gentile ground as a pledge that the dividing wall is coming down. The region that begs Jesus to leave prefers familiar uncleanness to the presence that expels it. Jerusalem will make the same preference when the pledged judgment arrives. The sign is mercy already crossing the boundary. The consummation is one new man when the old boundary-system is removed.

4) Two twelves: uncleanness the law could not stop

On the western shore the signs turn toward Israel under Moses. A synagogue ruler falls at Jesus’ feet. On the road a woman has bled for twelve years. She has suffered many things of many physicians, spent all, and grown worse. That is the purity system in one body. Leviticus named her flow as uncleanness. Contact defiled. She lived at the edge of the holy things. Twelve is Israel’s number. A complete span of hemorrhage under an administration that could isolate her and could not restore her is Israel’s condition in the last days of that age: life leaking out while the physicians of the old house take their fee.

She touches his garment. Under Moses, uncleanness travelled and holiness was guarded by distance. In him, holiness travels and the flow dries. This is an inaugural pledge of Zechariah’s fountain opened for sin and for uncleanness. The fountain is not a civic project after another age. It is opened in his flesh for that generation, and it is consummated when the blood of the covenant ends the Temple’s shadow-cleansing. He names her Daughter — the same name Jairus uses for the child. The two twelves are one people in two signs: Israel unclean by the law’s verdict, and Israel as the synagogue’s child at the point of death. Both meet the Stronger One before the house falls. The sign is a woman clean in the crowd. The consummation is a people no longer managed by quarantine when the law that quarantined them has ended.

5) Sleep, resuscitation, and covenantal resurrection

The house report is the old world’s sentence: the daughter is dead; do not bother the Teacher. Jesus answers the ruler. He puts the mourners out. He calls the death sleep. Physical resuscitation follows: she rises, walks, and eats. That event is not the emptying of Hades. It is not the corporate resurrection of Daniel 12 and 1 Corinthians 15. It is a pledge of those things. Sleep is how the prophets spoke of the covenant dead whom God would raise when he restored his people. Daniel’s sleepers wake at the time of trouble for Daniel’s people — the time that closes in that generation with the destruction of the city and the sanctuary. Paul speaks of resurrection as the harvest movement out of death into the life of the last Adam, timed to the end of the old order, not to a later collapse of the physical world.

So the girl in Jairus’s house must be read at two levels that stay distinct. As history: a child in AD 30–33 is given back her breath. Physical mortality is not abolished; she will eat and she will one day return to dust as Adam’s children do. As type: the daughter of the synagogue is raised from the death-verdict of the old age by the word of the Son. That type is made good when the corporate body is brought out of Old Covenant death — condemnation, exclusion, Hades’ hold on the saints under the first administration — into full New Covenant life. AD 70 is that consummation: the death-house of the law falls, Hades is emptied of those who are his, and the people live in the presence the Temple only sketched. To shrink that to a private change of mood is to refuse both Daniel and Paul. To inflate the bedroom into the Parousia is to refuse Mark’s chronology.

6) What ended, and what replaced it

What the signs pledge, the transition finishes. Ended: the Mosaic age, the Temple administration, the priesthood that could not stop the hemorrhage, the law’s quarantine as the way of dealing with uncleanness, Satanic bondage as the standing curse-order over those under that law, and Hades as the hold on the covenant dead. Replaced: the Spirit, the New Creation, unbroken presence, a people made clean by his blood, and life in the Son that the grave-bound administration could not give. Rome remains, in this reading, the rod used against the apostate city — an instrument, not the spirit named Legion. The Kingdom Mark announced as at hand is present now because the pledged consummation has occurred. The signs were the beginning of the plunder. The fall of the house was the end of the old age.

8. Key Idioms & Language Notes

  • Legion: Scale of unclean/Satanic bondage. Not a cipher for Rome.
  • Tombs: Dwelling in death under an administration that quarantines and does not raise.
  • Twelve / twelve: Israel’s number on both signs — hemorrhage and the synagogue’s daughter.
  • Asleep: Prophetic idiom for the covenant dead awaiting raising; here a resuscitation that types the corporate resurrection.

9. New Creation Reality

The pledge has been kept. Bondage under the curse-order is not the name of those in the Son. The law that isolated the unclean has ended. The death-verdict of the old house does not stand. You live in the presence the signs pointed to: clean by his blood, raised with him out of Old Covenant death, joined to the people who heard mercy on both sides of the sea. Identity is union. Freedom is the end of condemnation. The Teacher is not too late.

10. Reflection

Where do you still treat a pledge already consummated — cleansing, life, presence — as if the old house were still standing?
