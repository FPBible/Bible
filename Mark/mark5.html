Mark 5

In the last days of the Mosaic house the occupier is evicted, the purity system is outrun, and the synagogue’s dying daughter is raised.

1. Immediate Context

Mark 3 announced that a divided kingdom has an end and that the strong man must be bound before his house is plundered. Mark 4 set the word loose as seed in that generation. Mark 5 is the plunder: Jesus crosses the sea into Gentile land, then returns to a synagogue ruler’s house. The signs are the Kingdom already at hand, stripping the old administration of its three claims — occupation, quarantine, and the death-verdict over Israel’s child.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 They came to the other side of the sea, into the country of the Gadarenes. 2 When he had come out of the boat, immediately a man with an unclean spirit met him out of the tombs. 3 He lived in the tombs. Nobody could bind him any more, not even with chains, 4 because he had been often bound with fetters and chains, and the chains had been torn apart by him, and the fetters broken in pieces. Nobody had the strength to tame him. 5 Always, night and day, in the tombs and in the mountains, he was crying out, and cutting himself with stones. 6 When he saw Jesus from afar, he ran and bowed down to him, 7 and crying out with a loud voice, he said, “What have I to do with you, Jesus, you Son of the Most High God? I adjure you by God, don’t torment me.” 8 For he said to him, “Come out of the man, you unclean spirit!”

9 He asked him, “What is your name?” He said to him, “My name is Legion, for we are many.” 10 He begged him much that he would not send them away out of the country. 11 Now on the mountainside there was a great herd of pigs feeding. 12 All the demons begged him, saying, “Send us into the pigs, that we may enter into them.” 13 At once Jesus gave them permission. The unclean spirits came out and entered into the pigs. The herd of about two thousand rushed down the steep bank into the sea, and they were drowned in the sea. 14 Those who fed the pigs fled, and told it in the city and in the country.

The people came to see what it was that had happened. 15 They came to Jesus, and saw him who had been possessed by demons sitting, clothed, and in his right mind, even him who had the legion; and they were afraid. 16 Those who saw it declared to them what happened to him who was possessed by demons, and about the pigs. 17 They began to beg him to depart from their region.

18 As he was entering into the boat, he who had been possessed by demons begged him that he might be with him. 19 He didn’t allow him, but said to him, “Go to your house, to your friends, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you and how he had mercy on you.” 20 He went his way, and began to proclaim in Decapolis how Jesus had done great things for him, and everyone marveled.

21 When Jesus had crossed back over in the boat to the other side, a great multitude was gathered to him; and he was by the sea. 22 Behold, one of the rulers of the synagogue, Jairus by name, came; and seeing him, he fell at his feet 23 and begged him much, saying, “My little daughter is at the point of death. Please come and lay your hands on her, that she may be made healthy, and live.” 24 He went with him, and a great multitude followed him, and they pressed upon him on all sides.

25 A certain woman who had a discharge of blood for twelve years, 26 and had suffered many things by many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was no better, but rather grew worse, 27 having heard the things concerning Jesus, came up behind him in the crowd and touched his clothes. 28 For she said, “If I just touch his clothes, I will be made well.” 29 Immediately the flow of her blood was dried up, and she felt in her body that she was healed of her affliction.

30 Immediately Jesus, perceiving in himself that the power had gone out from him, turned around in the crowd and asked, “Who touched my clothes?” 31 His disciples said to him, “You see the multitude pressing against you, and you say, ‘Who touched me?’” 32 He looked around to see her who had done this thing. 33 But the woman, fearing and trembling, knowing what had been done to her, came and fell down before him, and told him all the truth. 34 He said to her, “Daughter, your faith has made you well. Go in peace, and be cured of your disease.”

35 While he was still speaking, people came from the synagogue ruler’s house, saying, “Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?” 36 But Jesus, when he heard the message spoken, immediately said to the ruler of the synagogue, “Don’t be afraid, only believe.” 37 He allowed no one to follow him except Peter, James, and John the brother of James. 38 He came to the synagogue ruler’s house, and he saw an uproar, weeping, and great wailing. 39 When he had entered in, he said to them, “Why do you make an uproar and weep? The child is not dead, but is asleep.”

40 They ridiculed him. But he, having put them all out, took the father of the child, her mother, and those who were with him, and went in where the child was lying. 41 Taking the child by the hand, he said to her, “Talitha cumi!” which means, being interpreted, “Girl, I tell you, get up!” 42 Immediately the girl rose up and walked, for she was twelve years old. They were amazed with great amazement. 43 He strictly ordered them that no one should know this, and commanded that something should be given her to eat.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Legion’s eviction is Mark 3:27 in public: the strong man is bound in that generation, not reserved for a later cosmic war.
  • The two “twelves” are Israel under the old administration — hemorrhaging uncleanness and a synagogue daughter at the point of death.
  • “Not dead, but asleep” reclassifies covenant death; the raise happens inside the generation that also saw the Temple fall.
  • Decapolis hears mercy while the Temple still stands: the nations receive the word before the end of the Mosaic age.
  • The purity code isolates; his garment cleanses. Holiness in the New Covenant is contact, not quarantine.

4. Annotations

  • Legion (v. 9): A Roman occupation-name. The land’s unclean garrison meets its eviction notice.
  • Tombs (v. 2–5): Dwelling in death. The old order keeps its captives among graves it cannot open.
  • Twelve years / twelve years old (v. 25, 42): The full number of Israel, twice: unclean and dying under the same administration.
  • The child is not dead, but is asleep (v. 39): Prophetic sleep-language for covenant death from which God raises his people.
  • Talitha cumi (v. 41): The word that raises the daughter of the synagogue. The old house’s death-report does not stand.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

1) The Occupier’s Time Expires

Mark 3 already settled the eschatology of this scene. A kingdom divided against itself has an end. The strong man is bound, then his goods are taken. Mark 5 is that taking. Jesus arrives on the eastern shore as the Stronger One into occupied land. The man lives in the tombs because the administration that named him unclean had no power to bring him out of death. Chains belong to that administration. They fail. Night-and-day crying and self-cutting are the public face of a house still held by an occupier after the law has done all it can do.

The spirits name him Son of the Most High and plead against torment. They know their appointment. This is not an open-ended war waiting for the collapse of the physical universe. Their time is the time of that generation — the same horizon in which Jesus said the Kingdom was at hand and the strong man was being bound. “Legion” is the name of a massed occupying force. Unclean spirits hold the man the way an army holds a province. The plea not to be sent out of the country is a plea to keep the garrison in the land. The Son does not negotiate a withdrawal date beyond AD 70. He issues permission that ends in the sea.

Pigs are Gentile uncleanness made visible. Two thousand of them carry the garrison down the bank and drown. Sea in Israel’s memory is the water that swallowed Egypt’s host and the deep that marked exile. The unclean host is not relocated to a future battlefield. It is driven into judgment-water in the days of Messiah’s ministry. The land is cleared as a sign of what the next forty years complete: the occupier’s claim on the old covenant world ends. The man seated, clothed, and in his right mind is plunder already taken from the strong man’s house.

2) Decapolis Before the Temple Falls

The delivered man asks to stay in the boat — to remain with Israel’s Messiah on Israel’s side of the sea. Jesus sends him the other way: house, friends, Decapolis. The ten cities are Gentile ground. The first extended preacher of this mercy is a man who that morning lived among graves. The dividing wall is walked through here, while priesthood, sacrifice, and Temple still stand in Jerusalem.

Mark 13 places the witness to the nations inside the same generation that sees the sanctuary thrown down. Decapolis is that witness begun. The old map said holy land here, tombs and pigs there. The Kingdom at hand ignores the map. Mercy takes a man out of covenant-death and plants him as a voice among the nations before the old house is left desolate. The region that begs Jesus to leave prefers the familiar occupation to the presence that evicts it. That preference is the same preference Jerusalem will make. The man who proclaims is the remnant-sign: those drawn out of the death-order become the heralds of the order that remains.

3) Two Twelves: Hemorrhage and the Synagogue’s Daughter

The crossing back is the same eschatology on Israel’s side of the sea. A ruler of the synagogue falls at Jesus’ feet because his daughter is at the point of death. On the road a woman appears who has bled for twelve years. She has suffered under many physicians, spent everything, and grown worse. That is the old administration in one body. The purity code named her flow as uncleanness. Contact with her defiled. She lived at the edge of the holy things. The physicians are the systems that managed the symptom and could not stop the loss. Twelve years is Israel’s number over a complete span of covenant hemorrhage: life leaking out under a law that could isolate her and could not make her clean.

She touches his garment. Under Moses, holiness was fragile and uncleanness travelled. In him, holiness travels and uncleanness ends. Power goes out. The flow dries. Zechariah’s fountain opened for sin and for uncleanness is this body, this garment, this generation. He calls her Daughter — the same name the synagogue ruler uses for the child at home. The unclean woman and the dying girl are one people in two signs. Israel as excluded-by-blood. Israel as child-of-the-synagogue at the point of death. Both meet him before the house falls.

4) “Not Dead, but Asleep”

The report from Jairus’s house is the old world’s last word: “Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?” That sentence says the covenant child is finished and the Teacher has arrived too late. Jesus answers the ruler, not the report. Fear belongs to the death-verdict of the old age. Belief belongs to the word now speaking. He puts the mourners out. Professional wailing is the liturgy of a house that has accepted the verdict. He reclassifies the girl: not dead, but asleep.

Sleep is how the prophets spoke of Israel in exile and of the dead whom God would raise when he restored his people. Daniel’s sleepers, Isaiah’s dust-dwellers, Ezekiel’s bones — the language is covenant restoration, not a timetable for the end of the physical universe. Mark places that language in a synagogue ruler’s bedroom in the generation of Messiah. Physical death is not abolished in the room; the girl will eat bread and will one day return to dust as Adam’s children do. What is broken is the claim that death has closed the story of the daughter of Israel. “Talitha cumi” is the New Covenant word over that daughter. She walks. She is twelve. The number closes. The full measure of the old people is raised by a voice, not by another round of Temple medicine.

This is the resurrection Mark’s timing texts concern: passage out of the death-order of the Mosaic age into the life of the Son. His own grave will shortly empty as the firstfruits. The Temple will fall as the old death-house. Those in him live now in the order those two events opened. The sign in Jairus’s house is the world-change already underway.

5) What the Old Age Lost in This Chapter

Three claims of the ending age are stripped. The occupier’s claim on the land and on a human body — gone into the sea. The purity system’s claim that uncleanness is managed by distance — ended at a garment. The synagogue’s claim that its daughter is dead and the Teacher is too late — answered by a girl walking and eating. What replaces them is already present: a mind seated in peace on Gentile soil, a Daughter sent into shalom, a child of twelve given food in her father’s house. That is the Kingdom Mark announced as at hand.

The generation that watched Legion drown, the hemorrhage stop, and the girl rise is the generation that watched not one stone left on another. The signs and the ruin belong to one horizon. The strong man bound in Mark 3 is plundered in Mark 5 and evicted from the old covenant world when the house falls. The nations already hearing in Decapolis are the nations who receive the salvation the Jerusalem leadership refuses. The fountain opened in his flesh is the cleansing the Day of Atonement only sketched. The raise in the synagogue house is the life the grave-bound administration could not give. Nothing in this chapter is stored for a later eschaton. The transfer is public. The old age is losing its prisoners while it still stands. The New Creation is the order in which those prisoners now live.

8. New Creation Reality

The occupier’s time is over. The purity system is not your mediator. The death-report over the daughter of the covenant does not stand. You live on the side of the sea where the man sits clothed, the hemorrhage has stopped, and the girl has been told to get up. Presence has replaced quarantine. The age that isolated and condemned has ended. Life in the Son is the order that remains.

9. Reflection

Which verdict of the old age — occupation, uncleanness, or “too late” — are you still treating as if the Teacher had not already spoken?
