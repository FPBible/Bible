Mark 5

The Stronger One plunders curse-bondage, ends the law’s uncleanness, and raises the daughter of the old house. That transfer is finished.

Scripture Text (WEB)

1 They came to the other side of the sea, into the country of the Gadarenes. 2 When he had come out of the boat, immediately a man with an unclean spirit met him out of the tombs. 3 He lived in the tombs. Nobody could bind him any more, not even with chains, 4 because he had been often bound with fetters and chains, and the chains had been torn apart by him, and the fetters broken in pieces. Nobody had the strength to tame him. 5 Always, night and day, in the tombs and in the mountains, he was crying out, and cutting himself with stones. 6 When he saw Jesus from afar, he ran and bowed down to him, 7 and crying out with a loud voice, he said, “What have I to do with you, Jesus, you Son of the Most High God? I adjure you by God, don’t torment me.” 8 For he said to him, “Come out of the man, you unclean spirit!”

9 He asked him, “What is your name?” He said to him, “My name is Legion, for we are many.” 10 He begged him much that he would not send them away out of the country. 11 Now on the mountainside there was a great herd of pigs feeding. 12 All the demons begged him, saying, “Send us into the pigs, that we may enter into them.” 13 At once Jesus gave them permission. The unclean spirits came out and entered into the pigs. The herd of about two thousand rushed down the steep bank into the sea, and they were drowned in the sea. 14 Those who fed the pigs fled, and told it in the city and in the country.

The people came to see what it was that had happened. 15 They came to Jesus, and saw him who had been possessed by demons sitting, clothed, and in his right mind, even him who had the legion; and they were afraid. 16 Those who saw it declared to them what happened to him who was possessed by demons, and about the pigs. 17 They began to beg him to depart from their region.

18 As he was entering into the boat, he who had been possessed by demons begged him that he might be with him. 19 He didn’t allow him, but said to him, “Go to your house, to your friends, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you and how he had mercy on you.” 20 He went his way, and began to proclaim in Decapolis how Jesus had done great things for him, and everyone marveled.

21 When Jesus had crossed back over in the boat to the other side, a great multitude was gathered to him; and he was by the sea. 22 Behold, one of the rulers of the synagogue, Jairus by name, came; and seeing him, he fell at his feet 23 and begged him much, saying, “My little daughter is at the point of death. Please come and lay your hands on her, that she may be made healthy, and live.” 24 He went with him, and a great multitude followed him, and they pressed upon him on all sides.

25 A certain woman who had a discharge of blood for twelve years, 26 and had suffered many things by many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was no better, but rather grew worse, 27 having heard the things concerning Jesus, came up behind him in the crowd and touched his clothes. 28 For she said, “If I just touch his clothes, I will be made well.” 29 Immediately the flow of her blood was dried up, and she felt in her body that she was healed of her affliction.

30 Immediately Jesus, perceiving in himself that the power had gone out from him, turned around in the crowd and asked, “Who touched my clothes?” 31 His disciples said to him, “You see the multitude pressing against you, and you say, ‘Who touched me?’” 32 He looked around to see her who had done this thing. 33 But the woman, fearing and trembling, knowing what had been done to her, came and fell down before him, and told him all the truth. 34 He said to her, “Daughter, your faith has made you well. Go in peace, and be cured of your disease.”

35 While he was still speaking, people came from the synagogue ruler’s house, saying, “Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?” 36 But Jesus, when he heard the message spoken, immediately said to the ruler of the synagogue, “Don’t be afraid, only believe.” 37 He allowed no one to follow him except Peter, James, and John the brother of James. 38 He came to the synagogue ruler’s house, and he saw an uproar, weeping, and great wailing. 39 When he had entered in, he said to them, “Why do you make an uproar and weep? The child is not dead, but is asleep.”

40 They ridiculed him. But he, having put them all out, took the father of the child, her mother, and those who were with him, and went in where the child was lying. 41 Taking the child by the hand, he said to her, “Talitha cumi!” which means, being interpreted, “Girl, I tell you, get up!” 42 Immediately the girl rose up and walked, for she was twelve years old. They were amazed with great amazement. 43 He strictly ordered them that no one should know this, and commanded that something should be given her to eat.

Fulfillment Notes

  • The strong man Mark 3 bound is plundered in the generation that also sees the Temple fall.
  • Legion is Satanic bondage and Old Covenant curse — the uncleanness the law named and could not break.
  • The two twelves are Israel under that administration: unclean by verdict, dying as the synagogue’s child.
  • “Asleep” names the covenant dead; this raising is kept when Hades is emptied at the end of that age.
  • Decapolis hears mercy while the sanctuary still stands.

Immediate Context

Jesus has stilled the sea. He now addresses an unclean host on the eastern shore, then a hemorrhaging woman and a synagogue ruler on the western shore. The hearers live under Moses’ purity law, synagogue authority, and the curse-bondage that law could name and could not break. The covenant moment is the Kingdom already announced as at hand, in the years that close when the Temple falls. These works matter then as the public beginning of the plunder promised in Mark 3.

Study Commentary

1) The tombs are the world Moses could describe and could not open

The man lives among graves. Chains belong to the old administration. They fail. Night-and-day crying is curse-bondage after the law has done all it can do. Mark 3 said a divided kingdom has an end and that the strong man is bound before his goods are taken. This shore is that taking begun. The spirits name him Son of the Most High and plead against torment. Their appointment is the closing of the Mosaic age. The Kingdom at hand is present as eviction.

Legion names the scale of the bondage: many, holding a human life in death. The host is unclean spirit — Satanic grip, the curse the law diagnosed. Pigs receive what the man is freed from. The sea takes the herd. In Israel’s memory water is where an oppressor’s boast ends. The man seated, clothed, and in his right mind is plunder already in the Stronger’s hand. The region that begs Jesus to leave prefers the familiar curse to the presence that ends it. Jerusalem makes that same preference when the house falls.

2) Decapolis hears while the old sanctuary still stands

The delivered man wants the boat. Jesus gives him house, friends, and the ten cities. Mercy crosses the boundary the old map guarded while priesthood and Temple still operate in Jerusalem. Mark 13 places that witness inside the generation that sees the sanctuary thrown down. The man who lived under curse-bondage becomes a herald on Gentile ground because the dividing wall is coming down in that same span. The finish of that movement is one new man when the boundary-system is removed.

3) Twelve years of blood is Israel under the physicians of Moses

A synagogue ruler falls at his feet. On the road a woman has bled twelve years, suffered many physicians, spent all, and grown worse. Leviticus named her flow as uncleanness. Contact defiled. She lived at the edge of the holy things. Twelve is Israel’s number. A complete span of hemorrhage under an administration that isolates and does not restore is that people’s condition in the last days of the age: life leaking out while the old house takes its fee.

She touches his garment. Under Moses uncleanness travelled and holiness was kept by distance. In him holiness travels and the flow dries. Zechariah’s fountain opened for sin and for uncleanness is this body, in this generation. That fountain is finished when his blood ends the Temple’s shadow-cleansing and the law that quarantined her is taken away. He calls her Daughter — Jairus’s name for the child. The two twelves are one people: Israel unclean by verdict, Israel dying as the synagogue’s child. Both meet him before the house falls.

4) Sleep is the name he gives the old house’s dead

The report from Jairus’s house is the ending age’s sentence over its own child: the daughter is dead; do not bother the Teacher. He answers the ruler. He puts the mourners out. He calls the death sleep. The prophets used that word for the covenant dead God would raise when he restored his people. Daniel times that waking to the trouble of Daniel’s people — the destruction of city and sanctuary in that generation. Paul times the harvest out of death to the end of the old order, the reign of the last Adam, the swallowing of the death that belonged to the first administration.

The girl rises, walks, and eats. A child in those years is given back her breath. Dust will still receive her; physical mortality was not abolished in the room. What is pledged is the corporate raising: the daughter of the synagogue brought out of Old Covenant death — condemnation, exclusion, Hades’ hold on the saints under the first priesthood — into the life of the Son. That passage is completed when the death-house of the law falls, Hades is emptied of those who are his, and the people live in the presence the Temple only sketched. The bedroom is the pledge. The fall of the house is the keeping of it.

5) What ended, and what presence gave

The old administration lost three claims in this chapter’s pledge, and lost them in full when the house fell. It lost the right to hold a human life in curse-bondage. It lost quarantine as the way of dealing with uncleanness. It lost the last word over the daughter of Israel. Ended with that age: the Mosaic law as covenant jurisdiction, the Temple and its priesthood, condemnation as standing status under that law, and Hades as the hold on the covenant dead. Given in their place: the Spirit, a people made clean by his blood, one new man, unbroken presence, New Creation life in the Son. The Stronger One who bound the strong man in the days of his flesh finished the plunder when not one stone of that house was left on another. The Kingdom Mark announced as at hand is the order in which those prisoners now live.

New Creation Reality

The plunder is finished. Curse-bondage is not your name. The law that isolated the unclean has ended. The death-verdict of the old house does not stand over those in the Son. You live in the presence the signs pledged and the fallen Temple confirmed: clean by his blood, raised with him out of Old Covenant death, seated in the life the first administration could not give. Identity is union. Freedom is the end of condemnation.

Reflection

Where are you still living as if the old house had the last word, when presence has already replaced it?
