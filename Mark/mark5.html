Mark 5

The strong man is bound, the unclean are made clean, and death yields to the word of the Son.

1. Immediate Context

Jesus has just stilled the storm and now steps onto the eastern shore of the sea, into Gentile territory. A man no chain could hold meets him from the tombs. Legion is expelled. The region begs Jesus to leave. The delivered man is sent home as a witness in Decapolis. Crossing back, Jesus is met by Jairus, a synagogue ruler whose daughter is dying. On the way a woman with a twelve-year flow of blood touches his garment and is healed. Word comes that the child is dead. Jesus takes her by the hand and she rises. These signs occur inside the generation to whom he said the time was fulfilled and the Kingdom was at hand.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 They came to the other side of the sea, into the country of the Gadarenes. 2 When he had come out of the boat, immediately a man with an unclean spirit met him out of the tombs. 3 He lived in the tombs. Nobody could bind him any more, not even with chains, 4 because he had been often bound with fetters and chains, and the chains had been torn apart by him, and the fetters broken in pieces. Nobody had the strength to tame him. 5 Always, night and day, in the tombs and in the mountains, he was crying out, and cutting himself with stones. 6 When he saw Jesus from afar, he ran and bowed down to him, 7 and crying out with a loud voice, he said, “What have I to do with you, Jesus, you Son of the Most High God? I adjure you by God, don’t torment me.” 8 For he said to him, “Come out of the man, you unclean spirit!”

9 He asked him, “What is your name?” He said to him, “My name is Legion, for we are many.” 10 He begged him much that he would not send them away out of the country. 11 Now on the mountainside there was a great herd of pigs feeding. 12 All the demons begged him, saying, “Send us into the pigs, that we may enter into them.” 13 At once Jesus gave them permission. The unclean spirits came out and entered into the pigs. The herd of about two thousand rushed down the steep bank into the sea, and they were drowned in the sea. 14 Those who fed the pigs fled, and told it in the city and in the country.

The people came to see what it was that had happened. 15 They came to Jesus, and saw him who had been possessed by demons sitting, clothed, and in his right mind, even him who had the legion; and they were afraid. 16 Those who saw it declared to them what happened to him who was possessed by demons, and about the pigs. 17 They began to beg him to depart from their region.

18 As he was entering into the boat, he who had been possessed by demons begged him that he might be with him. 19 He didn’t allow him, but said to him, “Go to your house, to your friends, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you and how he had mercy on you.” 20 He went his way, and began to proclaim in Decapolis how Jesus had done great things for him, and everyone marveled.

21 When Jesus had crossed back over in the boat to the other side, a great multitude was gathered to him; and he was by the sea. 22 Behold, one of the rulers of the synagogue, Jairus by name, came; and seeing him, he fell at his feet 23 and begged him much, saying, “My little daughter is at the point of death. Please come and lay your hands on her, that she may be made healthy, and live.” 24 He went with him, and a great multitude followed him, and they pressed upon him on all sides.

25 A certain woman who had a discharge of blood for twelve years, 26 and had suffered many things by many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was no better, but rather grew worse, 27 having heard the things concerning Jesus, came up behind him in the crowd and touched his clothes. 28 For she said, “If I just touch his clothes, I will be made well.” 29 Immediately the flow of her blood was dried up, and she felt in her body that she was healed of her affliction.

30 Immediately Jesus, perceiving in himself that the power had gone out from him, turned around in the crowd and asked, “Who touched my clothes?” 31 His disciples said to him, “You see the multitude pressing against you, and you say, ‘Who touched me?’” 32 He looked around to see her who had done this thing. 33 But the woman, fearing and trembling, knowing what had been done to her, came and fell down before him, and told him all the truth. 34 He said to her, “Daughter, your faith has made you well. Go in peace, and be cured of your disease.”

35 While he was still speaking, people came from the synagogue ruler’s house, saying, “Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?” 36 But Jesus, when he heard the message spoken, immediately said to the ruler of the synagogue, “Don’t be afraid, only believe.” 37 He allowed no one to follow him except Peter, James, and John the brother of James. 38 He came to the synagogue ruler’s house, and he saw an uproar, weeping, and great wailing. 39 When he had entered in, he said to them, “Why do you make an uproar and weep? The child is not dead, but is asleep.”

40 They ridiculed him. But he, having put them all out, took the father of the child, her mother, and those who were with him, and went in where the child was lying. 41 Taking the child by the hand, he said to her, “Talitha cumi!” which means, being interpreted, “Girl, I tell you, get up!” 42 Immediately the girl rose up and walked, for she was twelve years old. They were amazed with great amazement. 43 He strictly ordered them that no one should know this, and commanded that something should be given her to eat.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • The strong man’s house is plundered in the days when the Kingdom was already announced as at hand.
  • Uncleanness that the law could isolate for twelve years ends at a touch of his garment.
  • Death in Jairus’s house yields to a word spoken inside that generation, not to a postponed age.
  • The delivered man is sent as a witness among the nations of Decapolis while the old administration still stands.
  • Synagogue authority kneels; Temple-adjacent purity rules are outrun by the presence of the Son.

4. Annotations

  • Son of the Most High God (v. 7): Legion names him correctly. Recognition is not repentance. Authority is not negotiated.
  • Legion (v. 9): A Roman term for a massed force. The oppression is vast. It leaves at a word.
  • Your faith has made you well (v. 34): Faith receives what is already present in him. The crowd presses; one touch draws power.
  • The child is not dead, but is asleep (v. 39): He names the death as subject to his command. Ridicule meets the house; the girl walks.
  • Talitha cumi (v. 41): Aramaic kept in the record. The command is intimate and immediate.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

1) The Tombs and the Strong Man

Jesus steps out of the boat into a graveyard world. The man who meets him lives among the dead. Chains have failed. Night and day he cries and cuts himself with stones. This is not a private medical scene. It is the open display of an occupied house. Israel’s Scriptures had spoken of a Stronger One who would bind the strong man and plunder his goods. That word is enacted on this shore. Legion confesses the Son of the Most High and begs not to be tormented. The spirits know their time. They ask leave to enter the pigs. Jesus permits it. Two thousand rush down the bank and drown. The man who could not be tamed sits clothed and in his right mind. The region sees it and is afraid. They beg Jesus to depart. Deliverance exposes a world that preferred the familiar occupation to the presence of the Holy One.

The old order managed uncleanness by isolation. Tombs, chains, and distance were the tools. None of them held. The Kingdom does not manage Legion. It expels Legion. What the law could name and quarantine, the Son dismisses with a sentence. The drowning herd is not a puzzle about livestock. It is the visible end of a hidden occupation. The land is cleared. The man is restored. The word that stills the sea now stills the graveyard.

2) Sent to Decapolis

The delivered man wants to stay in the boat. Jesus refuses him that place and gives him another: house, friends, and a commission to tell what the Lord has done and how mercy met him. He goes and proclaims in Decapolis. The nations of the ten cities hear the report while the Temple still stands in Jerusalem. Gentile soil receives the first extended witness of this man’s mercy. The dividing wall is already being walked through. The man who lived among the dead becomes a preacher of life on the wrong side of the sea. That movement is the shape of the age that was opening: mercy crossing the boundary the old administration marked and guarded.

3) Twelve Years of Blood and the End of Quarantine

On the western shore a synagogue ruler falls at Jesus’ feet. His daughter is at the point of death. Jesus goes with him. The crowd presses. A woman who has bled for twelve years — spent all, suffered much, grown worse — comes up behind him and touches his clothes. Under the law her condition made her a source of uncleanness. She had lived at the edge of the holy. She touches him anyway. Immediately the flow dries. Power goes out from him. He stops the procession to name her. She tells the whole truth. He calls her Daughter. Faith has made her well. Peace is spoken over a body the physicians could not restore and the purity code could not admit.

Twelve years of hemorrhage and twelve years of the girl’s life sit in one chapter on purpose. The number marks completeness in the old counting. What the law completed as exclusion, the Son completes as restoration. The garment of Jesus does what the courts of the Temple could not do for this woman. Holiness here is not contaminated by her touch. Holiness heals the touch. That reversal is the New Covenant already arriving in a body: the unclean are not managed at a distance; they are made clean by contact with him.

4) “The Child Is Not Dead, but Asleep”

While he is still speaking, the word from Jairus’s house arrives: the daughter is dead; do not bother the Teacher. Jesus answers the ruler before grief can close the door. “Don’t be afraid, only believe.” He takes Peter, James, and John. The house is loud with professional mourning. He puts the crowd out. Ridicule is the last sound of the old reading of death. He takes the child’s hand and speaks Aramaic into a corpse: “Girl, I tell you, get up.” She rises and walks. She is twelve. He tells them to give her food. Resurrection here is not a lecture about the end of the physical universe. It is the Son reclaiming a daughter in a synagogue ruler’s house while that generation still stands. Death is real. The word is stronger. Physical mortality was not abolished in that room; the dominion of death over this child was broken by his voice. The same voice would shortly walk into his own grave and leave it empty. The signs of Mark 5 are the public pledge of that authority.

5) What Ended and What Stood

Three occupations end in one movement: Legion’s hold on the man, the law’s hold of uncleanness on the woman, and death’s hold on the girl. Chains end. Quarantine ends. The wailing ends. What replaces them is a seated mind, a named daughter in peace, and a walking child given bread. This is the Kingdom that was at hand — not a postponed world after history, but the present rule of the Son in the days of the old house. The synagogue ruler kneels. Decapolis hears mercy. The tombs lose a resident. The purity system loses a twelve-year case. Death loses a twelve-year-old girl. The generation that watched these things is the generation that also watched the Temple fall. The Stronger One had already bound the strong man. The house that refused him was left desolate. What cannot be shaken remains: the authority that speaks and Legion flees, the holiness that is touched and heals, the life that takes the dead by the hand.

Mark places this chapter between the stilling of the sea and the sending of the Twelve. Creation, the nations, the synagogue, the unclean, and the dead are all put under one Lord. That is the covenant transfer in narrative form. The old administration named boundaries. The Son crosses them and restores what they could not restore. The New Creation is not waiting for another age to begin this work. It is the order in which this Lord already reigns, and in which the people he has made clean and alive now live.

8. New Creation Reality

The strong man is bound. Uncleanness is not your name. Death does not have the last word over those who are in the Son. You live under the authority that expelled Legion, that named a bleeding woman Daughter, and that took a child by the hand. Presence replaces quarantine. The word that raised her still holds those who belong to him.

9. Reflection

Where have you treated isolation or fear as if they were stronger than the word that sat the man down, stopped the blood, and told the girl to get up?
