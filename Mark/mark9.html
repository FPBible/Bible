Mark 9

1. Immediate Context

Jesus declares that some standing there will not taste death until they see the Kingdom of God come with power. Six days later the Transfiguration occurs. Elijah has already come. A boy with a mute spirit is delivered. Jesus again predicts his death and resurrection. The disciples argue about greatness; the first must be last and servant of all.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 He said to them, “Most certainly I tell you, there are some standing here who will in no way taste death until they see God’s Kingdom come with power.”

2–8 After six days Jesus takes Peter, James, and John up a high mountain. He is transfigured; his clothing becomes glistening white. Elijah and Moses appear. A cloud overshadows them; a voice: “This is my beloved Son. Listen to him.” Suddenly they see Jesus only.

9–13 They are not to tell until the Son of Man has risen. Elijah has come, and they did to him whatever they wanted.

14–29 A boy with a mute spirit is brought; the disciples could not cast it out. “I believe. Help my unbelief!” Jesus commands the spirit to come out and never enter again. “This kind can come out by nothing but by prayer and fasting.”

30–32 He teaches that the Son of Man is being handed over, will be killed, and on the third day will rise. They do not understand.

33–37 Argument about who is greatest. “If any man wants to be first, he shall be last of all, and servant of all.” A child is set in the middle.

38–50 One casting out demons in Jesus’ name is not to be forbidden. Causing little ones to stumble brings severe warning. Salt and peace.

Source: ebible.org

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Some standing here who will not taste death until they see God’s Kingdom come with power (v. 1): Explicit time statement bound to people then alive.
  • Elijah has come (v. 13): The expected forerunner has already appeared and been treated as written.
  • The Son of Man is being handed over… on the third day he will rise (v. 31): The path is fixed and near.

4. Annotations

  • Transfiguration (v. 2–8): A proleptic vision of the Kingdom’s power and the beloved Son’s glory.
  • Help my unbelief (v. 24): Faith and unbelief coexist; the cry is itself an act of faith.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

The solemn assurance of verse 1 sets the horizon: some of those standing there would see the Kingdom come with power before they died. The Transfiguration immediately follows as a partial preview. Elijah has already come in John. The passion is again predicted. Greatness is redefined as servanthood. The generation that heard “some standing here” is the generation within which the Kingdom came with power — in the cross, resurrection, the outpouring of the Spirit, and the judgment that ended the old order.

8. New Creation Reality

Some who stood there did see the Kingdom come with power. Elijah has come. The Son has been glorified, handed over, killed, and raised. We live on the far side of that coming, called to be last and servant of all.
