Mark 13

1. Immediate Context

Leaving the temple, Jesus predicts that not one stone will be left upon another. On the Mount of Olives the disciples ask when these things will be and what the sign will be. Jesus answers with warnings, the abomination of desolation, great oppression, cosmic signs, and the coming of the Son of Man in clouds. He closes with the solemn assurance: this generation will not pass away until all these things happen.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 As he went out of the temple, one of his disciples said to him, “Teacher, see what kind of stones and what kind of buildings!” 2 Jesus said to him, “Do you see these great buildings? There will not be left here one stone on another, which will not be thrown down.”

3 As he sat on the Mount of Olives opposite the temple, Peter, James, John, and Andrew asked him privately, 4 “Tell us, when will these things be? What is the sign that these things are all about to be fulfilled?”

5–13 “Be careful that no one leads you astray. For many will come in my name, saying, ‘I am he!’ and will lead many astray. When you hear of wars and rumors of wars, don’t be troubled. For those must happen, but the end is not yet. For nation will rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom. There will be earthquakes in various places. There will be famines and troubles. These things are the beginning of birth pains. But watch yourselves, for they will deliver you up to councils. You will be beaten in synagogues. You will stand before rulers and kings for my sake, for a testimony to them. The Good News must first be preached to all the nations. When they lead you away and deliver you up, don’t be anxious beforehand what you will say, but say whatever will be given you in that hour. For it is not you who speak, but the Holy Spirit. Brother will deliver up brother to death, and the father his child. Children will rise up against parents and cause them to be put to death. You will be hated by all men for my name’s sake, but he who endures to the end will be saved.”

14–23 “But when you see the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, standing where it ought not” (let the reader understand), “then let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains, and let him who is on the housetop not go down, nor enter in, to take anything out of his house. Let him who is in the field not return back to take his cloak. But woe to those who are with child and to those who nurse babies in those days! Pray that your flight won’t be in the winter. For in those days there will be oppression, such as there has not been the like from the beginning of the creation which God created until now, and never will be. Unless the Lord had shortened the days, no flesh would have been saved; but for the sake of the chosen ones, whom he picked out, he shortened the days. Then if anyone tells you, ‘Look, here is the Christ!’ or, ‘Look, there!’ don’t believe it. For there will arise false christs and false prophets, and will show signs and wonders, that they may lead astray, if possible, even the chosen ones. But you watch. “Behold, I have told you all things beforehand.”

24–27 “But in those days, after that oppression, the sun will be darkened, the moon will not give its light, the stars will be falling from the sky, and the powers that are in the heavens will be shaken. Then they will see the Son of Man coming in clouds with great power and glory. Then he will send out his angels, and will gather together his chosen ones from the four winds, from the ends of the earth to the ends of the sky.”

28–31 “Now from the fig tree, learn this parable. When the branch has now become tender, and produces its leaves, you know that the summer is near; even so you also, when you see these things coming to pass, know that it is near, at the doors. Most certainly I say to you, this generation will not pass away until all these things happen. Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away.”

32–37 “But of that day or that hour no one knows, not even the angels in heaven, nor the Son, but only the Father. Watch, keep alert, and pray; for you don’t know when the time is. It is like a man, traveling to another country, having left his house, and given authority to his servants, and to each one his work, and also commanded the doorkeeper to keep watch. Watch therefore, for you don’t know when the lord of the house is coming, whether at evening, or at midnight, or when the rooster crows, or in the morning; lest coming suddenly he might find you sleeping. What I tell you, I tell all: Watch!”

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Not one stone on another (v. 2): The temple’s complete destruction is predicted and fulfilled in AD 70.
  • This generation will not pass away until all these things happen (v. 30): The decisive time statement. All the things of the discourse — including the coming of the Son of Man in clouds — are bound to the generation then living.
  • It is near, at the doors (v. 29): When the signs are seen, the fulfillment is imminent for that generation.
  • The Son of Man coming in clouds (v. 26): Daniel 7 language of vindication and authority, enacted in the judgment that ended the old order.

4. Annotations

  • Abomination of desolation (v. 14): Daniel’s phrase; the signal for flight from Judea. “Let the reader understand” points to a recognisable historical referent. Luke 21:20 identifies surrounding armies.
  • This generation (v. 30): The same generation addressed throughout the Gospel (8:12, 8:38, 9:19); not a distant future race.
  • Heaven and earth (v. 31): Covenant-order language for the old world of Torah and temple; not the physical cosmos.
  • Coming in clouds (v. 26): Prophetic judgment and vindication idiom (Daniel 7:13–14), not a global physical spectacle deferred for millennia.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

1) The Question Is About the Temple

The disciples point out the great buildings. Jesus answers: there will not be left here one stone on another. The private question on the Mount of Olives is therefore about that event: when will these things be, and what is the sign that they are about to be fulfilled? The frame is the temple’s destruction. Wars, earthquakes, famines, persecution, the abomination, flight from Judea, unparalleled oppression, cosmic signs, and the coming of the Son of Man in clouds are all placed within the answer to that question. The discourse is not a timeline for the end of the physical world. It is the outline of the covenant crisis that ends the Mosaic age and vindicates the Son of Man.

2) Birth Pains and the Preaching to the Nations

Nation against nation, earthquakes, famines, and troubles are the beginning of birth pains. The disciples will be delivered to councils, beaten in synagogues, and stand before rulers. The Good News must first be preached to all the nations. Brother will deliver up brother. They will be hated for his name’s sake; the one who endures to the end will be saved. These are the conditions of the transition generation. The gospel’s advance to the nations is not a postponement of the end; it is a requirement that is met within the first century. The birth pains lead to the birth of the New Covenant order, not to an endless deferral.

3) The Abomination and the Local Flight

When you see the abomination of desolation standing where it ought not, those in Judea must flee to the mountains. The one on the housetop must not go down; the one in the field must not return for a cloak. Woe to those with child or nursing in those days. Pray that the flight is not in winter. The details are local, geographic, and time-bound. They fit a siege of Jerusalem and a flight from Judea. They do not fit a global end-of-the-world scenario. Luke’s parallel names the surrounding of Jerusalem by armies as the signal. The first-century believers who heeded the warning escaped the siege. The oppression of those days was unique in covenant history: the end of the temple, the priesthood, and the city that had rejected the Son.

4) Cosmic Signs and the Coming in Clouds

After that oppression, the sun is darkened, the moon does not give its light, the stars fall, and the powers in the heavens are shaken. Then they will see the Son of Man coming in clouds with great power and glory. He will send his angels and gather his chosen ones from the four winds. In the Hebrew prophetic register, sun, moon, and stars language depicts the collapse of rulers and covenant order. Isaiah used it of Babylon; Ezekiel of Egypt. Daniel’s Son of Man comes with the clouds of heaven to receive dominion. Mark’s language is that register. The coming in clouds is the vindication of the rejected Messiah and the judgment of the old order. The gathering of the chosen is the New Covenant assembly drawn from the ends of the earth — no longer centered on a temple that is about to fall.

5) This Generation and the Passing of Heaven and Earth

From the fig tree learn: when you see these things, know that it is near, at the doors. Most certainly, this generation will not pass away until all these things happen. Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away. The time-lock is absolute. “This generation” is the generation Jesus addressed. From the prediction to the fall of the temple is a biblical generation. Heaven and earth — the covenant stability of the old order — pass away. His words remain. The call to watch is a call to that generation. It remains a call to readiness for all who live under the finished work. The temple fell. The old order was judged. The Son of Man came in the clouds of heaven in the sense of Daniel 7. We live in the age that followed that coming.

8. New Creation Reality

This generation did not pass away until all these things happened. The temple is gone. The old order has been judged. The Son of Man has come in the clouds with power and glory. We live in the age that followed that coming — the New Covenant reality in which his words remain and the call is still to watch, not for a deferred catastrophe, but in the settled confidence of a finished transition.
