Preface — How to Read Mark

Mark is the shortest of the four Gospels and the most urgent. The word “immediately” drives the narrative forward. Jesus comes into Galilee announcing that the time is fulfilled and the Kingdom of God is at hand. The Gospel moves rapidly through healing, confrontation with the religious leaders, the gathering of the Twelve, the journey to Jerusalem, the cross, and the empty tomb.

Mark’s Jesus is the Son of God and the Son of Man who came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many. The Olivet Discourse in chapter 13 places the coming of the Son of Man and the end of the age within the lifetime of that generation: “This generation will not pass away until all these things are accomplished.”

What this Gospel shows

  • The time is fulfilled; the Kingdom of God is at hand.
  • Authority over unclean spirits, disease, nature, and death is already exercised.
  • The Son of Man must suffer, be rejected, be killed, and rise after three days.
  • This generation would not pass away until all the things of the Olivet Discourse took place.
  • The Good News begins with the baptism and ends with the empty tomb and the risen Lord’s charge.

How to read it

Read Mark as the announcement that the decisive time has arrived. The Kingdom is not postponed; it is at hand. The cross is not a detour; it is the ransom. The coming of the Son of Man in the clouds is not a distant event for some other generation; it is bound to the generation that heard the words. The Gospel calls for repentance and faith in the Good News that has already begun.
