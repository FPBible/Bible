Romans 4

Abraham Fulfilled, Promise Realised

1. Immediate Context

Romans 4 explains that righteousness apart from the Law was always God’s intention. Paul does not invent a new method of salvation. He shows that the covenant shift now revealed in Christ is the same pattern God used with Abraham: gift, not wage; promise, not badge; faith that takes God at His word when life looks like death.

2. Scripture (WEB)

Source (verbatim): https://ebible.org/web/ROM04.htm

1 What then will we say that Abraham, our forefather, has found according to the flesh?
2 For if Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about, but not toward God.
3 For what does the Scripture say? “Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness.”
4 Now to him who works, the reward is not counted as grace, but as something owed.
5 But to him who doesn’t work, but believes in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is accounted for righteousness.
6 Even as David also pronounces blessing on the man to whom God counts righteousness apart from works:
7 “Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, whose sins are covered.
8 Blessed is the man whom the Lord will by no means charge with sin.”
9 Is this blessing then pronounced only on the circumcised, or on the uncircumcised also? For we say that faith was accounted to Abraham for righteousness.
10 How then was it counted? When he was in circumcision, or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision.
11 He received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had while he was in uncircumcision, that he might be the father of all those who believe, though they might be in uncircumcision, that righteousness might also be accounted to them.
12 He is the father of circumcision to those who not only are of the circumcision, but who also walk in the steps of that faith of our father Abraham, which he had in uncircumcision.
13 For the promise to Abraham and to his offspring that he would be heir of the world wasn’t through the law, but through the righteousness of faith.
14 For if those who are of the law are heirs, faith is made void, and the promise is made of no effect.
15 For the law produces wrath; for where there is no law, neither is there disobedience.
16 For this cause it is of faith, that it may be according to grace, to the end that the promise may be sure to all the offspring, not to that only which is of the law, but to that also which is of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all.
17 As it is written, “I have made you a father of many nations.” This is in the presence of him whom he believed: God, who gives life to the dead, and calls the things that are not, as though they were.
18 Against hope, Abraham in hope believed, to the end that he might become a father of many nations, according to that which had been spoken, “So will your offspring be.”
19 Without being weakened in faith, he didn’t consider his own body, already having been worn out, (he being about a hundred years old), and the deadness of Sarah’s womb.
20 Yet, looking to the promise of God, he didn’t waver through unbelief, but grew strong through faith, giving glory to God,
21 and being fully assured that what he had promised, he was also able to perform.
22 Therefore it also was “credited to him for righteousness.”
23 Now it was not written that it was accounted to him for his sake alone,
24 but for our sake also, to whom it will be accounted, who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,
25 who was delivered up for our trespasses, and was raised for our justification.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Before circumcision (vv. 9–12) — Abraham is father of uncircumcised believers first in the story, then of the circumcised who walk in that same faith.
  • Before the Law — Sinai cannot be the source of a promise given 430 years earlier (cf. Galatians 3:17).
  • Life from the dead (vv. 17–22) — resurrection language describes God creating a people from barrenness; it is fulfilled in Christ’s resurrection.
  • For us also (vv. 23–25) — the same accounting now belongs to those who believe on the One raised from the dead.

4. Annotations

  • Counted / reckoned — courtroom gift, not earned wage (vv. 4–5).
  • Heir of the world — covenantal scope of the family, not a later land program as the point of the chapter.
  • Father of many nations — Genesis 17 arriving in the multi-national assemblies of Paul’s day.
  • Delivered up / raised — justification located in the finished work of Jesus (v. 25).

5. Study Commentary

Abraham dismantles Law-righteousness from inside Israel’s story

If anyone could be the mascot of flesh-privilege, it would be Abraham. Paul takes him and shows that he was counted righteous by faith before circumcision, before Sinai, before the temple. The badge came later as a seal of a righteousness he already had.

That is devastating for a last-days Judaism that treated circumcision and Torah as the door. Abraham is father of the uncircumcised who believe, and of the circumcised who share that faith — not father of a closed genealogy.

The promise is a people, not a wage

“To him who works, the reward is not counted as grace, but as debt.” Abraham did not put God in his debt. He believed the God who calls the dead to life and names things that are not as though they were. The inheritance of the world is the expansion of that family beyond Israel’s flesh, fulfilled as the gospel runs among the nations before the old age ends.

This is why Paul can say the promise is sure to all the seed — not only those of the Law, but those of Abraham’s faith. The one new man is Abraham’s family as God always told the story.

Resurrection is how the promise is confirmed

Abraham faced his own body as good as dead, and Sarah’s womb. He still gave glory to God. That pattern is not a metaphor bolted onto Christ. It is fulfilled in Christ: delivered up for our trespasses, raised for our justification.

The people of God are a resurrection people. They do not wait for a later accounting to become Abraham’s children. They are counted now because the God who raised Jesus has already spoken.

Faith is participation in a completed promise

Paul writes “it was not written for his sake alone… but for us also.” The Roman believers — Jew and Gentile — are already inside the story. Faith is not waiting for God to become generous. It is receiving a generosity already public in the resurrection.

New Creation life is not earned kinship. It is promised kinship, now realised in Christ.

9. New Creation Reality

Faith is not waiting. It is participation. Abraham’s promise is no longer anticipated. It is realised in a people formed by the Spirit, not by Law or lineage. You live from a completed promise, not toward an uncertain future.

10. Reflection

Where might I still treat God’s promise as something to earn or wait for, instead of something already given in Christ?
