Romans 14

Shared Life After the Law

1. Immediate Context

Romans 14 addresses tensions inside mixed Jewish–Gentile communities after the Law has reached its end as a life-system. Some still honour days and abstain from foods as a matter of conscience. Others know the earth is the Lord’s. Paul will not let either group rebuild a court of condemnation inside the body. Christ died and lived again that He might be Lord of both.

2. Scripture (WEB)

Source (verbatim): https://ebible.org/web/ROM14.htm

1 Now accept one who is weak in faith, but not for disputes over opinions.
2 One man has faith to eat all things, but he who is weak eats only vegetables.
3 Don’t let him who eats despise him who doesn’t eat. Don’t let him who doesn’t eat judge him who eats, for God has accepted him.
4 Who are you who judge another’s servant? To his own lord he stands or falls. Yes, he will be made to stand, for God has power to make him stand.
5 One man esteems one day as more important. Another esteems every day alike. Let each man be fully assured in his own mind.
6 He who observes the day, observes it to the Lord; and he who does not observe the day, to the Lord he does not observe it. He who eats, eats to the Lord, for he gives God thanks. He who doesn’t eat, to the Lord he doesn’t eat, and gives God thanks.
7 For none of us lives to himself, and none dies to himself.
8 For if we live, we live to the Lord. Or if we die, we die to the Lord. If therefore we live or die, we are the Lord’s.
9 For to this end Christ died, rose, and lived again, that he might be Lord of both the dead and the living.
10 But you, why do you judge your brother? Or you again, why do you despise your brother? For we will all stand before the judgment seat of Christ.
11 For it is written, “ ‘As I live,’ says the Lord, ‘to me every knee will bow. Every tongue will confess to God.’ ”
12 So then each one of us will give account of himself to God.
13 Therefore let’s not judge one another any more, but judge this rather, that no man put a stumbling block in his brother’s way, or an occasion for falling.
14 I know and am persuaded in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean of itself; except that to him who considers anything to be unclean, to him it is unclean.
15 Yet if because of food your brother is grieved, you walk no longer in love. Don’t destroy with your food him for whom Christ died.
16 Then don’t let your good be slandered,
17 for God’s Kingdom is not eating and drinking, but righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit.
18 For he who serves Christ in these things is acceptable to God and approved by men.
19 So then, let’s follow after things which make for peace, and things by which we may build one another up.
20 Don’t overthrow God’s work for food’s sake. All things indeed are clean, however it is evil for that man who creates a stumbling block by eating.
21 It is good to not eat meat, drink wine, nor do anything by which your brother stumbles, is offended, or is made weak.
22 Do you have faith? Have it to yourself before God. Happy is he who doesn’t judge himself in that which he approves.
23 But he who doubts is condemned if he eats, because it isn’t of faith; and whatever is not of faith is sin.
24 Now to him who is able to establish you according to my Good News and the preaching of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery which has been kept secret through long ages,
25 but now is revealed, and by the Scriptures of the prophets, according to the commandment of the eternal God, is made known for obedience of faith to all the nations;
26 to the only wise God, through Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory forever! Amen.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • God received him (v. 3) — the weak one is already received; you do not get a veto.
  • Lord of the dead and the living (v. 9) — Christ’s lordship over the whole household, not food’s lordship.
  • Kingdom (v. 17) — righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit; already the definition of the community.
  • Whatever is not of faith is sin (v. 23) — conscience in transition, not a new Sinai of scruples.

4. Annotations

  • Weak in faith — still bound in conscience to old boundary-markers, not morally inferior as a person.
  • Days — the old calendar losing jurisdiction.
  • Judgment seat of God — each will give account; stop playing that seat inside the assembly.
  • Stumbling block — using liberty to crush a brother still leaving the old house.

5. Study Commentary

The chapter collapses if the Law is still the master

If Sinai still governs foods and days as covenant boundary, Paul’s whole argument is impossible. He treats those things as disputable, not as the faith once delivered. God has received the person. Who are you to judge another’s servant?

This is post-Law community life in real time: people at different speeds leaving an old world, commanded not to rebuild it as a test of belonging.

Christ is Lord of days, tables, and people

The one who observes the day observes it to the Lord. The one who eats eats to the Lord. None of us lives to himself. Whether we live or die, we are the Lord’s. That sentence relocates every old marker under Christ’s lordship.

He died and lived again for this: to be Lord of both dead and living. The calendar does not get to be Lord. The menu does not get to be Lord.

Stop occupying God’s judgment seat

Why do you judge your brother? We will all stand before the judgment seat of God. Isaiah 45:23 is quoted: every knee will bow. In a generation about to see the old house judged, the assemblies are told not to run a shadow-court over vegetables.

The prophetic “every knee” is the same lordship already named in Philippians 2. It is not an excuse to despise. It is a reason to leave judgment with God.

The kingdom is already this: righteousness, peace, and joy

The kingdom of God is not eating and drinking, but righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit. That is present-tense kingdom. Pursue what makes for peace. Do not tear down the work of God for the sake of food.

Liberty is real. Love governs liberty. Faith toward God in a time of transition will look like patience with people still washing their hands on the way out of the old world — without letting them make washing the gospel.

9. New Creation Reality

Belonging no longer depends on food, days, or scruples. It rests on Christ alone — and that reality calls for patience as people grow out of old frameworks, without letting the old frameworks become the gospel again.

10. Reflection

Where might I still measure faithfulness by inherited habits, instead of by love within the New Creation community?
