Romans 6

Dead to the Old World, Alive in the New

1. Immediate Context

Romans 6 answers a misunderstanding created by grace. If righteousness is given apart from the Law, does that mean sin no longer matters? Paul’s answer is not moral panic. It is identity. Believers have died to the covenant world where sin and death ruled. Baptism marks that transfer. The old master has lost jurisdiction.

2. Scripture (WEB)

Source (verbatim): https://ebible.org/web/ROM06.htm

1 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound?
2 May it never be! We who died to sin, how could we live in it any longer?
3 Or don’t you know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death?
4 We were buried therefore with him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, so we also might walk in newness of life.
5 For if we have become united with him in the likeness of his death, we will also be part of his resurrection;
6 knowing this, that our old man was crucified with him, that the body of sin might be done away with, so that we would no longer be in bondage to sin.
7 For he who has died has been freed from sin.
8 But if we died with Christ, we believe that we will also live with him,
9 knowing that Christ, being raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over him!
10 For the death that he died, he died to sin one time; but the life that he lives, he lives to God.
11 Thus consider yourselves also to be dead to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord.
12 Therefore don’t let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts.
13 Also, do not present your members to sin as instruments of unrighteousness, but present yourselves to God as alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness to God.
14 For sin will not have dominion over you, for you are not under law, but under grace.
15 What then? Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace? May it never be!
16 Don’t you know that when you present yourselves as servants and obey someone, you are the servants of whomever you obey, whether of sin to death, or of obedience to righteousness?
17 But thanks be to God that, whereas you were bondservants of sin, you became obedient from the heart to that form of teaching to which you were delivered.
18 Being made free from sin, you became bondservants of righteousness.
19 I speak in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh; for as you presented your members as servants to uncleanness and to wickedness upon wickedness, even so now present your members as servants to righteousness for sanctification.
20 For when you were servants of sin, you were free from righteousness.
21 What fruit then did you have at that time in the things of which you are now ashamed? For the end of those things is death.
22 But now, being made free from sin and having become servants of God, you have your fruit of sanctification and the result of eternal life.
23 For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Died to sin (v. 2) — past-tense identity, not a goal on a list.
  • Baptism into death (vv. 3–4) — transfer out of the old world into newness of life.
  • Not under law (v. 14) — sin’s mastery is tied to Law’s administration; that administration is ending.
  • Present tense: “consider yourselves dead… but alive to God” (v. 11) — reckon what is true, don’t postpone it.

4. Annotations

  • Old man crucified — the Adamic/old-covenant self judged in Christ.
  • Newness of life — New Creation walk, not a later body-swap as the point.
  • Slaves of righteousness — belonging has changed masters.
  • Wages / gift — death as sin’s pay; eternal life as God’s gift in Christ (v. 23).

5. Study Commentary

Identity first, then walk

Paul does not command believers to die to sin so that God might accept them. He declares that they have already died. “We who died to sin, how could we live in it any longer?” The gospel creates a new location before it issues a new ethic.

This is the opposite of religious striving. You do not kill the old man by effort. The old man was crucified with Christ. What remains is to live from the new place.

Baptism is passage, not a repeatable reset

Immersion signifies being joined to Christ’s death and raised to walk in newness of life. It is covenant transfer — out of Adam’s world into Christ’s. It is not a cycle of re-entry every time you fail. The grave you went through with Christ is not reopened every Sunday.

In the last days of the old age, this sign was explosive. It said: I no longer belong to the world that is perishing. I belong to the Son whom God has raised.

Sin’s reign ends where Law’s jurisdiction ends

“Sin will not have dominion over you, for you are not under law, but under grace.” Paul ties sin’s mastery to Law’s administration. That is why Romans cannot be read as “try harder under the same Sinai.” Grace is a new government.

When the temple world still stood, this claim was contested. Judaizing voices wanted believers back under the old master in holy clothing. Paul says that is a return to slavery. AD 70 will take down the building. Romans 6 already takes down the claim.

Present your members to the new King

Because you are alive from the dead, present your body to God as instruments of righteousness. This is not the old fear-system with nicer language. It is the reasonable service of people who have changed worlds.

The wage sin pays is death. The gift God gives is eternal life in Christ Jesus. You are not negotiating wages. You are living from a gift.

9. New Creation Reality

Freedom is not the absence of struggle. Freedom is knowing which world you belong to. You do not fight sin in order to become alive. You live alive because sin’s authority has already ended for those under grace.

10. Reflection

Where might I still think of myself as someone trying to die, instead of someone who already lives?
