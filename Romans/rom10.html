Romans 10

Righteousness Proclaimed, the Word Already Near

1. Immediate Context

Romans 10 continues Paul’s grief and argument: Israel has a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge. They seek to establish their own righteousness and will not submit to God’s. Christ is the end of the Law for righteousness to everyone who believes. The word is not far off. It is in the mouth and in the heart — and it has already been preached among the nations.

2. Scripture (WEB)

Source (verbatim): https://ebible.org/web/ROM10.htm

1 Brothers, my heart’s desire and my prayer to God is for Israel, that they may be saved.
2 For I testify about them that they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge.
3 For being ignorant of God’s righteousness, and seeking to establish their own righteousness, they didn’t subject themselves to the righteousness of God.
4 For Christ is the fulfillment of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes.
5 For Moses writes about the righteousness of the law, “The one who does them will live by them.”
6 But the righteousness which is of faith says this, “Don’t say in your heart, ‘Who will ascend into heaven?’ (that is, to bring Christ down);
7 or, ‘Who will descend into the abyss?’ (that is, to bring Christ up from the dead.)”
8 But what does it say? “The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart;” that is, the word of faith which we preach:
9 that if you will confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved.
10 For with the heart one believes resulting in righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made resulting in salvation.
11 For the Scripture says, “Whoever believes in him will not be disappointed.”
12 For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek; for the same Lord is Lord of all, and is rich to all who call on him.
13 For, “Whoever will call on the name of the Lord will be saved.”
14 How then will they call on him in whom they have not believed? How will they believe in him whom they have not heard? How will they hear without a preacher?
15 And how will they preach unless they are sent? As it is written: “How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the Good News of peace, who bring glad tidings of good things!”
16 But they didn’t all listen to the glad news. For Isaiah says, “Lord, who has believed our report?”
17 So faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.
18 But I say, didn’t they hear? Yes, most certainly, “Their sound went out into all the earth, their words to the ends of the world.”
19 But I ask, didn’t Israel know? First Moses says, “I will provoke you to jealousy with that which is no nation. I will make you angry with a nation void of understanding.”
20 Isaiah is very bold and says, “I was found by those who didn’t seek me. I was revealed to those who didn’t ask for me.”
21 But about Israel he says, “All day long I stretched out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people.”

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Christ the end of the Law (v. 4) — telos: goal and termination of Torah as the righteousness-system.
  • The word is near (vv. 6–8) — Deuteronomy 30 applied to the gospel already spoken, not a future climb.
  • Whoever (vv. 11–13) — Joel’s “whoever calls” includes the nations now.
  • Their voice went out (v. 18) — Psalm 19 cited as accomplished mission-range in Paul’s horizon (cf. Colossians 1:23).

4. Annotations

  • Zeal without knowledge — sincerity is not righteousness.
  • Confess / believe — allegiance to the risen Lord, the same Jesus Jerusalem rejected.
  • Beautiful feet — Isaiah 52’s gospel-runners already on the roads of the empire.
  • A disobedient and contrary people (v. 21) — Isaiah 65 spoken over that generation’s refusal.

5. Study Commentary

Zeal cannot replace revelation

Paul bears witness: they have a zeal for God. He does not sneer at it. He names its limit. Seeking to establish their own righteousness, they did not subject themselves to the righteousness of God. Christ is the end of the Law for righteousness. The race is over. The tape is broken. Continuing to run Sinai as if the Messiah had not come is a refusal, not a virtue.

This is still the temptation: add a system beside Christ and call it seriousness. Romans 10 calls it ignorance of God’s righteousness.

The word is near — stop climbing

Paul hears Deuteronomy 30 not as a future ladder into heaven or a descent into the abyss, but as the nearness of the gospel word. “The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart.” Justification does not require a pilgrimage into the unseen. It requires receiving the word of faith that Paul preaches.

Confessing Jesus as Lord and believing God raised Him is not a later-day formula. It is first-century allegiance in a world where Caesar and temple both claimed lordship.

The mission of that generation is not theoretical

How will they hear without a preacher? Paul is describing the actual apostolic sending. Then he quotes Psalm 19: their sound went out into all the earth. Then Isaiah 53: not all obeyed. Then Isaiah 65: all day long I stretched out my hands.

This is the last-days mission Jesus named — the gospel to the nations, then the end of the age (Matthew 24:14). Romans 10 says the word has gone out. Israel’s contrary response is happening inside that same horizon, not in a vacuum.

Whoever calls on the name of the Lord

Joel’s outpouring language — “whoever” — is the collapse of ethnic gatekeeping. There is no distinction between Jew and Greek in this calling. The same Lord is rich toward all who call on Him.

New Creation people do not keep the word far away as if God were still hidden behind a veil. The word is near. The Lord is risen. Call on Him.

You do not fetch Christ down or bring Him up. The word is near you. Righteousness is not a private Sinai you keep in parallel with Jesus. The Lord who was raised is rich toward all who call on Him.

10. Reflection

Where might I still treat the word as far away — something to climb toward — instead of a near word to be confessed?
