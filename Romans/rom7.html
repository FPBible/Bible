Romans 7

Released from the Law, Joined to Another

1. Immediate Context

Romans 7 explains how believers were released from the Law in order to belong to the risen Christ and bear fruit to God. Paul uses marriage: death ends a husband’s legal claim. He then lets the Law-age speak in the first person — the experience of knowing the good and being unable to produce life under that administration. The chapter is a funeral for the old husband, not a charter for perpetual defeat.

2. Scripture (WEB)

Source (verbatim): https://ebible.org/web/ROM07.htm

1 Or don’t you know, brothers (for I speak to men who know the law), that the law has dominion over a man for as long as he lives?
2 For the woman that has a husband is bound by law to the husband while he lives, but if the husband dies, she is discharged from the law of the husband.
3 So then if, while the husband lives, she is joined to another man, she would be called an adulteress. But if the husband dies, she is free from the law, so that she is no adulteress, though she is joined to another man.
4 Therefore, my brothers, you also were made dead to the law through the body of Christ, that you would be joined to another, to him who was raised from the dead, that we might produce fruit to God.
5 For when we were in the flesh, the sinful passions which were through the law worked in our members to bring out fruit to death.
6 But now we have been discharged from the law, having died to that in which we were held; so that we serve in newness of the spirit, and not in oldness of the letter.
7 What shall we say then? Is the law sin? May it never be! However, I wouldn’t have known sin except through the law. For I wouldn’t have known coveting unless the law had said, “You shall not covet.”
8 But sin, finding occasion through the commandment, produced in me all kinds of coveting. For apart from the law, sin is dead.
9 I was alive apart from the law once, but when the commandment came, sin revived and I died.
10 The commandment which was for life, this I found to be for death;
11 for sin, finding occasion through the commandment, deceived me, and through it killed me.
12 Therefore the law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy, righteous, and good.
13 Did then that which is good become death to me? May it never be! But sin, that it might be shown to be sin, was producing death in me through that which is good; that through the commandment sin might become exceedingly sinful.
14 For we know that the law is spiritual, but I am fleshly, sold under sin.
15 For I don’t understand what I am doing. For I don’t practice what I desire to do; but what I hate, that I do.
16 But if what I don’t desire, that I do, I consent to the law that it is good.
17 So now it is no more I that do it, but sin which dwells in me.
18 For I know that in me, that is, in my flesh, dwells no good thing. For desire is present with me, but I don’t find it doing that which is good.
19 For the good which I desire, I don’t do; but the evil which I don’t desire, that I practice.
20 But if what I don’t desire, that I do, it is no more I that do it, but sin which dwells in me.
21 I find then the law that, while I desire to do good, evil is present.
22 For I delight in God’s law after the inward person,
23 but I see a different law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity under the law of sin which is in my members.
24 What a wretched man I am! Who will deliver me out of the body of this death?
25 I thank God through Jesus Christ, our Lord! So then with the mind, I myself serve God’s law, but with the flesh, sin’s law.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Death ends jurisdiction (vv. 1–6) — the Law binds a person only as long as he lives; union with Christ’s death ends that claim.
  • Newness of the Spirit (v. 6) — the replacement of “oldness of the letter.”
  • The Law is holy (v. 12) — the problem is not the Law’s character but its inability to give life to flesh.
  • Who will deliver? (v. 24) — the cry is answered immediately in 7:25 and 8:1, not left open for centuries.

4. Annotations

  • Husband / wife — legal union; death severs the bond.
  • Letter vs Spirit — two ministries (cf. 2 Corinthians 3).
  • Indwelling sin — the old man’s operation under Law, not a denial of chapter 6’s identity.
  • Law of sin in my members — the inner war of the old administration.

5. Study Commentary

The old husband is dead

A woman is bound to her husband while he lives. If he dies, she is free to marry another. Paul applies this to those who were under the Law: they died to the Law through the body of Christ, so they may be joined to Him who was raised. Fruit to God comes from the new union, not from the corpse of the old one.

This is why returning to Torah as a life-system is spiritual adultery in Paul’s logic. The first husband is not improved. He is dead. AD 70 will bury the public house of that husband. Romans 7 already reads the marriage certificate as expired.

The Law is good — and it cannot raise the dead

Paul will not let anyone say the Law is sin. The Law is holy, righteous, and good. It names coveting. It exposes. What it cannot do is create the life it commands. When the commandment came, sin sprang to life, and the man died. That is the old covenant’s story in miniature.

The Law is a perfect x-ray. It is not a surgeon. Christ is the one who dies and rises. The Spirit is the one who writes on hearts.

The “I” of verses 14–25 is the Law-age talking

The present-tense agony — “what I hate, that I do” — has been used to baptise perpetual Christian defeat. Coming after chapter 6’s “dead to sin,” that reading fights the letter. Paul is dramatising life under the old husband: knowing the good, agreeing that the Law is spiritual, and discovering that flesh cannot complete it.

The wretched-man cry is real. It is the cry of the old world. The answer is not “try the Law more sincerely.” The answer is “thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord” — and then Romans 8’s “no condemnation.”

Deliverance is a Person, not a postponed mood

“Who will deliver me from the body of this death?” is answered, not shelved. Deliverance is Jesus Christ our Lord. The split of v. 25 (mind serving God, flesh serving sin’s law) is the tension of a generation leaving one world and entering another — and it is resolved by the Spirit’s law of life in chapter 8, not by waiting for physical immortality to make obedience possible.

Physical death remains. Condemnation does not. That distinction is the difference between New Creation hope and fear-religion.

  • Oldness of the letter — the first husband’s administration.

9. New Creation Reality

You are not married to a corpse. You are joined to the risen Christ. The Law can still tell the truth about good and evil; it cannot be your husband. Life is in the Spirit, and the cry of Romans 7 is not your address.

10. Reflection

Where might I still live as though the old husband were alive — asking the Law to give what only the risen Christ gives?
