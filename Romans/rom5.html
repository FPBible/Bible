Romans 5

Reconciliation Established, Life Reigning

1. Immediate Context

Romans 5 flows from justification apart from the Law. Paul now names the present consequences of that verdict: peace with God, access, and life reigning. He then widens the lens: two humanities — Adam and Christ — two jurisdictions of death and life. This is not a lecture on biology. It is the transfer of the people of God out of Adamic/old-covenant condemnation into the reign of grace.

2. Scripture (WEB)

Source (verbatim): https://ebible.org/web/ROM05.htm

1 Being therefore justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ;
2 through whom we also have our access by faith into this grace in which we stand. We rejoice in hope of the glory of God.
3 Not only this, but we also rejoice in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces perseverance;
4 and perseverance, proven character; and proven character, hope;
5 and hope doesn’t disappoint us, because God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who was given to us.
6 For while we were yet weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly.
7 For one will hardly die for a righteous man. Yet perhaps for a good person someone would even dare to die.
8 But God commends his own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.
9 Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we will be saved from God’s wrath through him.
10 For if while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we will be saved by his life.
11 Not only so, but we also rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received the reconciliation.
12 Therefore, as sin entered into the world through one man, and death through sin, so death passed to all men because all sinned.
13 For until the law, sin was in the world; but sin is not charged when there is no law.
14 Nevertheless death reigned from Adam until Moses, even over those whose sins weren’t like Adam’s disobedience, who is a foreshadowing of him who was to come.
15 But the free gift isn’t like the trespass. For if by the trespass of the one the many died, much more did the grace of God and the gift by the grace of the one man, Jesus Christ, abound to the many.
16 The gift is not as through one who sinned; for the judgment came by one to condemnation, but the free gift followed many trespasses to justification.
17 For if by the trespass of the one, death reigned through the one; so much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness reign in life through the one, Jesus Christ.
18 So then as through one trespass, all men were condemned; even so through one act of righteousness, all men were justified to life.
19 For as through the one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, even so through the obedience of the one, many will be made righteous.
20 The law came in that the trespass might abound; but where sin abounded, grace abounded more exceedingly,
21 that as sin reigned in death, even so grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Peace already: “Being therefore justified… we have peace with God” (v. 1) — present possession.
  • Access: “we have our access by faith into this grace in which we stand” (v. 2) — standing, not climbing.
  • Much more: the repeated “much more” (vv. 9, 10, 15, 17, 20) is the logic of a completed reconciliation, not a postponed one.
  • Two realms: Adam’s transgression / Christ’s act — old humanity under death, new humanity under grace.

4. Annotations

  • Peace — end of covenantal hostility, not a mood.
  • Wrath — the same first-century verdict from which the justified are saved (v. 9).
  • Adam — head of the old humanity; death’s jurisdiction.
  • Grace reigns — the New Covenant’s government, already in force.

5. Study Commentary

Peace is a status, not a spiritual weather report

Paul does not say believers will one day feel peaceful. He says that having been justified, they have peace with God through the Lord Jesus Christ. Hostility ended. Access opened. Standing granted. The old covenant world trained people to approach God through distance, veil, and sacrifice. Romans 5 says the distance is closed.

Suffering in this chapter is not evidence that peace is incomplete. It produces endurance because the love of God has already been poured out in the Spirit. The inner witness matches the outer verdict.

Reconciliation is received, then lived

“While we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son.” Reconciliation is God’s act toward enemies, not the reward of the already-cleaned. “Much more, being reconciled, we will be saved by his life.” The life of the risen Son now governs those who have been brought near.

This is why moralism cannot survive Romans 5. You do not reconcile yourself in order to be loved. You live because you have been reconciled. The New Creation does not begin with self-repair. It begins with a finished peace.

Adam and Christ are two worlds

Death reigned from Adam. That reign is the old humanity’s story — sin, condemnation, and the Law entering later to make the trespass obvious (v. 20). Christ is not a second chance inside Adam’s world. He is another head. Those who receive the abundance of grace reign in life through the One man.

Physical mortality continues; Paul knows that. What has changed is the jurisdiction. Condemnation is not the last word over those in Christ. The old Adamic/covenant death — alienation, curse, exclusion — is met by a reigning life. AD 70 will publicly end the administration that kept that curse hanging over the old house. The reign of grace does not wait for that date to become true; the date displays what the cross has already done.

Where sin abounded, grace abounded more

The Law entered that the trespass might abound — not because God loves ruin, but because the old administration had to be shown as unable to give life. Grace does not dribble in as a supplement to Sinai. It over-abounds, and it reigns through righteousness to eternal life in Christ Jesus.

Eternal life here is the life of the age to come arriving in the Messiah’s people. You do not wait for grace to become the government. Grace already reigns.

8. Key Idioms

  • Peace with God — ended hostility.
  • Reign — which world governs you: death or grace.
  • Much more — the surplus of Christ’s act over Adam’s.

9. New Creation Reality

Life with God does not begin after fear is removed. Fear is removed because reconciliation is already complete. You live in a world where grace reigns, and condemnation no longer defines identity — even while mortal bodies still return to dust.

10. Reflection

Where might I still relate to God as though reconciliation were partial, instead of resting in the peace already established?
