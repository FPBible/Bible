Syria Palaestina

The Covenant, the Curse and the Land

The name on the map is older than the argument over it.

In AD 135, after crushing the last great Jewish revolt, Rome renamed the province of Judaea Syria Palaestina. The name has been in use for nearly nineteen hundred years, and it is still argued over today. This book starts with the name and follows the story behind it.

That story begins with a covenant sworn at Sinai and a land held on written conditions. It runs through the prophets who were sent and refused, through the Son who was sent last of all, and through the siege and burning of Jerusalem in AD 70. It then follows the second ruin under Hadrian and asks what the New Testament says became of the promises, the land, and the old division between Jew and Gentile.

Inside the Book

  • A close reading of Deuteronomy 28 set beside Josephus’s account of the siege.
  • The parable of the tenants, the woes of Matthew 23, and the Olivet Discourse in their first-century setting.
  • The second war under Hadrian, and what the sources say about how the province got its new name.
  • A full chapter on Romans 9 to 11 that sets out the main competing readings fairly.
  • The better country of Hebrews, and what it means for how Christians read the land.
This is a theological and historical argument, not a political one. It does not tell the reader how to vote or how to read the present conflict, and it is explicit that nothing in it is an argument against the Jewish people.
