Acts 16

1. Immediate Context

Acts 16 records the Spirit-directed advance of the gospel into Europe. After delivering the decrees of the Jerusalem Council and strengthening the churches, Paul and his companions are forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word in Asia and are not allowed to enter Bithynia. In Troas Paul receives a vision of a man of Macedonia pleading, “Come over into Macedonia and help us.” They conclude that the Lord has called them and immediately set sail. In Philippi, a Roman colony, the Lord opens the heart of Lydia, a seller of purple. A slave girl with a spirit of divination is delivered. Paul and Silas are beaten with rods, thrown into the inner prison, and secured in the stocks. At midnight they pray and sing hymns. An earthquake opens the doors and loosens every bond. The jailer and his household believe and are baptized. The magistrates, learning that Paul and Silas are Roman citizens, release them with fear. The first church in Europe is established. The gospel continues its advance to the nations while the Temple still stands.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 He came to Derbe and Lystra; and behold, a certain disciple was there, named Timothy, the son of a Jewess who believed, but his father was a Greek. 2 The brothers who were at Lystra and Iconium gave a good testimony about him. 3 Paul wanted to have him go out with him, and he took and circumcised him because of the Jews who were in those parts, for they all knew that his father was a Greek. 4 As they went on their way through the cities, they delivered the decrees to them to keep which had been ordained by the apostles and elders who were at Jerusalem. 5 So the assemblies were strengthened in the faith, and increased in number daily.

6 When they had gone through the region of Phrygia and Galatia, they were forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word in Asia. 7 When they had come opposite Mysia, they tried to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit didn’t allow them. 8 Passing by Mysia, they came down to Troas. 9 A vision appeared to Paul in the night. There was a man of Macedonia standing, begging him and saying, “Come over into Macedonia and help us.” 10 When he had seen the vision, immediately we sought to go out to Macedonia, concluding that the Lord had called us to preach the Good News to them.

11 Setting sail therefore from Troas, we made a straight course to Samothrace, and the day following to Neapolis; 12 and from there to Philippi, which is a city of Macedonia, the foremost of the district, a Roman colony. We were staying some days in this city. 13 On the Sabbath day we went outside of the city by a riverside, where we supposed there was a place of prayer, and we sat down and spoke to the women who had come together. 14 A certain woman named Lydia, a seller of purple, of the city of Thyatira, one who worshiped God, heard us. The Lord opened her heart to listen to the things which were spoken by Paul. 15 When she and her household were baptized, she begged us, saying, “If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come into my house and stay.” So she persuaded us.

16 As we were going to prayer, a certain girl having a spirit of divination met us, who brought her masters much gain by fortune telling. 17 Following Paul and us, she cried out, “These men are servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to us a way of salvation!” 18 She was doing this for many days. But Paul, becoming greatly annoyed, turned and said to the spirit, “I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her!” It came out that very hour.

19 But when her masters saw that the hope of their gain was gone, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the rulers. 20 When they had brought them to the magistrates, they said, “These men, being Jews, are agitating our city 21 and advocate customs which it is not lawful for us to accept or to observe, being Romans.” 22 The multitude rose up together against them and the magistrates tore their clothes from them, then commanded them to be beaten with rods. 23 When they had laid many stripes on them, they threw them into prison, charging the jailer to keep them safely. 24 Having received such a command, he threw them into the inner prison and secured their feet in the stocks.

25 But about midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them. 26 Suddenly there was a great earthquake, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken; and immediately all the doors were opened, and everyone’s bonds were loosened. 27 The jailer, being roused out of sleep and seeing the prison doors open, drew his sword and was about to kill himself, supposing that the prisoners had escaped. 28 But Paul cried with a loud voice, saying, “Don’t harm yourself, for we are all here!”

29 He called for lights, sprang in, fell down trembling before Paul and Silas, 30 brought them out, and said, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?” 31 They said, “Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household.” 32 They spoke the word of the Lord to him, and to all who were in his house. 33 He took them the same hour of the night and washed their stripes, and was immediately baptized, he and all his household. 34 He brought them up into his house and set food before them, and rejoiced greatly with all his household, having believed in God.

35 But when it was day, the magistrates sent the sergeants, saying, “Let those men go.” 36 The jailer reported these words to Paul, saying, “The magistrates have sent to let you go; now therefore come out and go in peace.” 37 But Paul said to them, “They have beaten us publicly without a trial, men who are Romans, and have cast us into prison! Do they now release us secretly? No, most certainly, but let them come themselves and bring us out!” 38 The sergeants reported these words to the magistrates, and they were afraid when they heard that they were Romans, 39 and they came and begged them. When they had brought them out, they asked them to depart from the city. 40 They went out of the prison and entered into Lydia’s house. When they had seen the brothers, they encouraged them, then departed.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • The Spirit forbids and the Spirit calls (vv. 6–10): The Holy Spirit both closes doors (Asia, Bithynia) and opens the way through a vision. The advance into Europe is explicitly Spirit-directed.
  • The Lord opened her heart (v. 14): Lydia’s conversion is attributed to the Lord’s sovereign action. Faith is the result of God opening the heart to receive the word.
  • “Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household” (v. 31): The same simple gospel announced to the jailer. Salvation is by faith in the Lord Jesus, and it extends to the household.
  • Praying and singing hymns in the stocks (v. 25): The New Covenant response to suffering. Joy and worship in the midst of affliction become a witness to the other prisoners.
  • Deliverance and baptism the same night (vv. 26–34): Earthquake, open doors, loosened bonds, the jailer’s conversion, and immediate baptism. The gospel produces both physical and spiritual liberation.

4. Annotations

  • Timothy circumcised (v. 3): Not for salvation (the Council has already settled that), but for the sake of mission among the Jews who knew his father was Greek. A matter of removing an unnecessary offense.
  • “We” sections begin (v. 10): Luke joins the party at Troas. The narrative becomes first-person, indicating the author’s presence.
  • Philippi, a Roman colony (v. 12): A strategic city with special privileges. The gospel now enters a thoroughly Roman environment in Europe.
  • Place of prayer by the riverside (v. 13): With no synagogue mentioned, the team goes to a riverside gathering of women. The first convert in Europe is a woman, Lydia of Thyatira.
  • Spirit of divination (v. 16): Literally a “python spirit,” associated with the oracle at Delphi. The girl speaks truth about the messengers, yet Paul casts the spirit out because the source is demonic and the exploitation is real.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

Spirit-Directed Guidance

After the Jerusalem Council, Paul and his companions strengthen the churches and deliver the decrees. Yet when they attempt to continue westward into Asia and then north into Bithynia, the Holy Spirit forbids them. In Troas a vision of a Macedonian man appears. The team immediately concludes that the Lord has called them. The advance into Europe is not the result of human strategy alone; it is the Spirit both closing and opening doors. The New Covenant mission remains under the direct leadership of the risen Lord through His Spirit.

Lydia and the Opened Heart

In Philippi there is no synagogue. The team finds a group of women gathered for prayer by the river. Lydia, a God-fearer and seller of purple from Thyatira, listens. Luke records that “the Lord opened her heart to listen to the things which were spoken by Paul.” Her conversion and the baptism of her household are presented as the Lord’s work. Hospitality immediately follows. The first church in Europe begins in the home of a businesswoman whose heart God has opened.

Deliverance and Opposition

A slave girl with a spirit of divination follows the team, declaring the truth that they are servants of the Most High God. Paul, annoyed after many days, commands the spirit to come out in the name of Jesus Christ. The spirit leaves, and with it the masters’ hope of profit. Economic loss produces fierce opposition. Paul and Silas are seized, beaten with rods without trial, and thrown into the inner prison with their feet fastened in the stocks. The cost of disrupting the powers of darkness and the economic systems that profit from them is real.

Midnight Worship and Earthquake

At midnight Paul and Silas are praying and singing hymns to God, and the other prisoners are listening. Suddenly a great earthquake shakes the foundations, opens every door, and loosens every bond. The jailer, assuming the prisoners have escaped, prepares to kill himself. Paul stops him. The question comes: “What must I do to be saved?” The answer is simple and consistent with the entire New Covenant message: “Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household.” The word is spoken, the household believes, the wounds are washed, and baptism follows the same hour of the night. Joy fills the house.

Roman Citizenship and Departure

When the magistrates send word to release them, Paul refuses a quiet dismissal. They have been beaten publicly without trial as Roman citizens. The magistrates come and beg them. Paul and Silas leave the prison, encourage the brothers at Lydia’s house, and depart. The church in Philippi is left with a clear testimony of both the power of the gospel and the integrity of its messengers.

Transition Generation Significance

Acts 16 shows the gospel crossing from Asia Minor into Europe under the explicit direction of the Holy Spirit. A businesswoman, a delivered slave girl, and a Roman jailer and his household form the nucleus of the first European church. Salvation by faith in the Lord Jesus is proclaimed without addition. Suffering and joy, imprisonment and earthquake, midnight hymns and household baptism — all occur while the Temple still stands and the old covenant age is still running its course. The light continues to go to the nations.

8. New Creation Reality

Acts 16 displays the New Creation mission under the direct guidance of the Spirit. Doors are closed and a new door is opened. A heart is opened by the Lord. A demonic spirit is expelled in the name of Jesus. Prisoners sing hymns at midnight. An earthquake loosens every bond. A jailer and his household believe and are baptized the same night. Salvation is announced simply: believe in the Lord Jesus Christ. The first church in Europe is formed from a businesswoman, a delivered slave, and a Roman official’s household. This is the one new man taking root on a new continent while the old covenant structures still stand. The joy of the Lord is the strength of His people even in the stocks.

9. Reflection

Paul and Silas sang hymns to God while their feet were fastened in the stocks. How does their midnight worship challenge the ways we respond to injustice or suffering? What does the sequence of the Lord opening Lydia’s heart, and the simple call to “believe in the Lord Jesus Christ,” teach us about the source of genuine conversion?
