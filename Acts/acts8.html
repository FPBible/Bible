Acts 8

1. Immediate Context

Acts 8 opens with the first great wave of persecution following Stephen’s martyrdom. The scattering of the believers is not a setback but the sovereign means by which the gospel advances exactly as Jesus had commanded in Acts 1:8 — from Jerusalem into Judea and Samaria. Philip’s ministry in Samaria and his encounter with the Ethiopian eunuch demonstrate that the New Covenant message is breaking out of its old covenant boundaries. What the religious leaders in Jerusalem intended as destruction becomes the catalyst for the gospel reaching “the ends of the earth” within the short window before AD 70.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 Saul was consenting to his death. A great persecution arose against the assembly which was in Jerusalem in that day. They were all scattered abroad throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria, except for the apostles. 2 Devout men buried Stephen and lamented greatly over him. 3 But Saul ravaged the assembly, entering into every house and dragging both men and women off to prison.

4 Therefore those who were scattered abroad went around preaching the word. 5 Philip went down to the city of Samaria and proclaimed to them the Christ. 6 The multitudes listened with one accord to the things that were spoken by Philip when they heard and saw the signs which he did. 7 For unclean spirits came out of many of those who had them. They came out, crying with a loud voice. Many who had been paralyzed and lame were healed. 8 There was great joy in that city.

9 But there was a certain man, Simon by name, who used to practice sorcery in the city and amazed the people of Samaria, making himself out to be some great one, 10 to whom they all listened, from the least to the greatest, saying, “This man is that great power of God.” 11 They listened to him because for a long time he had amazed them with his sorceries. 12 But when they believed Philip preaching good news concerning God’s Kingdom and the name of Jesus Christ, they were baptized, both men and women. 13 Simon himself also believed. Being baptized, he continued with Philip. Seeing signs and great miracles occurring, he was amazed.

14 Now when the apostles who were at Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent Peter and John to them, 15 who, when they had come down, prayed for them, that they might receive the Holy Spirit; 16 for as yet he had fallen on none of them. They had only been baptized in the name of Christ Jesus. 17 Then they laid their hands on them, and they received the Holy Spirit. 18 Now when Simon saw that the Holy Spirit was given through the laying on of the apostles’ hands, he offered them money, 19 saying, “Give me also this power, that whomever I lay my hands on may receive the Holy Spirit.” 20 But Peter said to him, “May your silver perish with you, because you thought you could obtain the gift of God with money! 21 You have neither part nor lot in this matter, for your heart is not right before God. 22 Repent therefore of this, your wickedness, and ask God if perhaps the thought of your heart may be forgiven you. 23 For I see that you are in the poison of bitterness and in the bondage of iniquity.”

24 Simon answered, “Pray for me to the Lord, that none of the things which you have spoken happen to me.”

25 They therefore, when they had testified and spoken the word of the Lord, returned to Jerusalem, and preached the Good News to many villages of the Samaritans.

26 Then an angel of the Lord spoke to Philip, “Arise, and go toward the south to the way that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza. This is a desert.” 27 He arose and went; and behold, there was a man of Ethiopia, a eunuch of great authority under Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, who was over all her treasure, who had come to Jerusalem to worship. 28 He was returning and sitting in his chariot, and was reading the prophet Isaiah.

29 The Spirit said to Philip, “Go near, and join yourself to this chariot.”

30 Philip ran to him, and heard him reading Isaiah the prophet, and said, “Do you understand what you are reading?”

31 He said, “How can I, unless someone explains it to me?” He begged Philip to come up and sit with him. 32 Now the passage of the Scripture which he was reading was this,

“He was led as a sheep to the slaughter.
As a lamb before his shearer is silent,
so he doesn’t open his mouth.
33 In his humiliation, his judgment was taken away.
Who will declare His generation?
For his life is taken from the earth.”

34 The eunuch answered Philip, “I ask you, about whom does the prophet say this? About himself, or about someone else?”

35 Philip opened his mouth, and beginning from this Scripture, preached to him about Jesus. 36 As they went on the way, they came to some water, and the eunuch said, “Behold, here is water. What is keeping me from being baptized?”

37 Philip said, “If you believe with all your heart, you may.” He answered, “I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.” 38 He commanded the chariot to stand still, and they both went down into the water, both Philip and the eunuch, and he baptized him. 39 When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught Philip away, and the eunuch didn’t see him any more, for he went on his way rejoicing. 40 But Philip was found at Azotus. Passing through, he preached the Good News to all the cities until he came to Caesarea.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Persecution as Mission Catalyst (vv. 1-4): The scattering of the church fulfills Jesus’ words in Acts 1:8. What the Jerusalem leaders meant for evil, God used to spread the gospel into Samaria.
  • Samaria Receives the Word (vv. 5-8, 14-17): The gospel reaches the historic enemies of Israel. The Spirit is given through the apostles’ hands, confirming the inclusion of Samaria in the New Covenant people.
  • Simon the Sorcerer (vv. 9-13, 18-24): A warning against trying to control spiritual power with money or old covenant thinking. Simon represents those who want the benefits of the New Covenant without a changed heart.
  • The Ethiopian Eunuch (vv. 26-40): The first recorded African convert. A man excluded under the old covenant Law (Deut 23:1) is now freely welcomed into the New Covenant through faith in Jesus. This is a powerful sign of the breaking down of old barriers.

4. Annotations

  • Scattered abroad (v. 1, 4): The Greek word *diaspeirō* is the same root as “diaspora.” The church becomes a scattered, mobile witness rather than a Jerusalem-centered institution.
  • Simon (v. 9ff): “Simon Magus.” His desire to buy spiritual power later gave rise to the term “simony” (buying church offices).
  • Eunuch (v. 27): A high-ranking official who was physically excluded from full participation in the Temple under the Mosaic Law. His conversion shows the New Covenant removing old covenant barriers.
  • Isaiah 53 (vv. 32-33): The Suffering Servant passage. Philip uses it as the starting point to preach Jesus as the crucified and risen Messiah.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

The Scattering That Advanced the Mission

The death of Stephen triggered the first major persecution. Instead of crushing the movement, it fulfilled Jesus’ strategic command: the gospel must go to “all Judea and Samaria.” The believers who were scattered “went around preaching the word.” Persecution became the vehicle of mission. This pattern would repeat throughout the first century — opposition often accelerated the spread of the gospel before the old covenant world came to its end in AD 70.

Samaria: The First Major Expansion

Philip’s ministry in Samaria is deeply significant. Samaria had been the historic rival and enemy of Judah for centuries. Yet here the gospel is received with joy. The apostles Peter and John are sent to confirm the work and pray for the Holy Spirit to fall on the new believers. This is not merely ethnic expansion — it is the breaking down of the old covenant dividing wall. The same Spirit that fell at Pentecost is now poured out on former enemies, showing that the New Covenant is not limited to physical Israel.

Simon and the Danger of Mixing Old and New

Simon’s attempt to buy the power of the Spirit is a stark warning. He represents those who want the supernatural benefits of the New Covenant while retaining old covenant (or pagan) ways of control and status. Peter’s sharp rebuke shows that the gift of God cannot be purchased or manipulated. Heart transformation, not external power, is what marks true participation in the New Creation.

The Ethiopian Eunuch: Firstfruits of the Nations

The conversion of the Ethiopian eunuch is one of the most beautiful pictures in Acts. Under the old covenant, a eunuch was excluded from the assembly of the Lord (Deuteronomy 23:1). Yet here, a man from the ends of the earth who had come to worship in Jerusalem is now freely baptized into Christ. Philip explains Isaiah 53 and shows him that Jesus is the Suffering Servant who opens the way for all who believe. The eunuch goes away rejoicing — the first known African convert and a powerful sign that the gospel is for every nation and every person previously excluded.

Philip Caught Away

The Spirit supernaturally transports Philip after the baptism. This dramatic exit underscores that the mission is under divine direction. Philip continues preaching through the coastal cities until he reaches Caesarea, where he later hosts Paul (Acts 21:8). The gospel is on the move.

8. New Creation Reality

Acts 8 shows us that the New Covenant is not confined to one city, one ethnicity, or one physical condition. Through the scattering and the ministry of Philip, the gospel reaches historic enemies (Samaria) and those previously excluded (the eunuch). We live in the reality that the dividing walls have been broken down. The Spirit is poured out freely on all who believe in Jesus — not because of lineage, status, or ritual purity, but because of faith in the finished work of Christ. This is our inheritance in the New Creation.

9. Reflection

The gospel advanced most powerfully when the church was scattered by persecution. How does knowing that God can turn opposition into opportunity for the spread of the New Covenant message encourage you in your own life and witness today?
