Acts 5

1. Immediate Context

Acts 5 marks a critical intensification in the life of the early church. It opens with the first instance of internal "covenantal judgment" within the New Creation body, demonstrating that the Holy Spirit is the guardian of the new Temple's purity. Following this, the narrative records the massive expansion of the Gospel through signs and wonders, leading to the second arrest and trial of the apostles. This chapter serves as a theological bridge, proving that the New Covenant community is the authentic "Israel of God," operating with the judicial and miraculous authority that once resided in the Tabernacle and Temple.

2. Scripture (WEB)

Source (verbatim): https://ebible.org/web/ACT05.htm

1 But a certain man named Ananias, with Sapphira, his wife, sold a possession, 2 and kept back part of the price, his wife also being aware of it, and brought a certain part, and laid it at the apostles’ feet. 3 But Peter said, “Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit, and to keep back part of the price of the land? 4 While you kept it, didn’t it remain your own? After it was sold, wasn’t it in your power? How is it that you have conceived this thing in your heart? You haven’t lied to men, but to God.”
5 Ananias, hearing these words, fell down and died. Great fear came on all who heard these things. 6 The young men arose and wrapped him up, and they carried him out and buried him. 7 About three hours later, his wife, not knowing what had happened, came in. 8 Peter answered her, “Tell me whether you sold the land for so much.” She said, “Yes, for so much.” 9 But Peter said to her, “How is it that you have agreed together to tempt the Spirit of the Lord? Behold, the feet of those who have buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out.”
10 She fell down immediately at his feet, and died. The young men came in and found her dead, and they carried her out and buried her by her husband. 11 Great fear came on the whole assembly, and on all who heard these things.
12 By the hands of the apostles many signs and wonders were done among the people. They were all with one accord in Solomon’s porch. 13 None of the rest dared to join them, however the people honored them. 14 More believers were added to the Lord, multitudes of both men and women, 15 so that they even carried out the sick into the streets, and laid them on cots and mattresses, so that as Peter came by, at the least his shadow might overshadow some of them. 16 The multitude also from the cities around Jerusalem came together, bringing sick people, and those who were tormented by unclean spirits: and they were all healed.
17 But the high priest rose up, and all those who were with him (which is the sect of the Sadducees), and they were filled with jealousy, 18 and laid hands on the apostles, and put them in public custody. 19 But an angel of the Lord opened the prison doors by night, and brought them out, and said, 20 “Go stand and speak in the temple to the people all the words of this Life.”
21 When they heard this, they entered into the temple about daybreak, and taught. But the high priest came, and those who were with him, and called the council together, and all the senate of the children of Israel, and sent to the prison to have them brought. 22 But the officers who came didn’t find them in the prison. They returned and reported, 23 “We found the prison shut and locked, and the guards standing before the doors, but when we opened them, we found no one inside.”
24 Now when the high priest, the captain of the temple, and the chief priests heard these words, they were very perplexed concerning them and what might grow from this. 25 One came and told them, “Behold, the men whom you put in prison are in the temple, standing and teaching the people.” 26 Then the captain went with the officers, and brought them without violence, for they were afraid of the people, that they might be stoned.
27 When they had brought them, they set them before the council. The high priest questioned them, 28 saying, “Didn’t we strictly command you not to teach in this name? Behold, you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching, and intend to bring this man’s blood on us.”
29 But Peter and the apostles answered, “We must obey God rather than men. 30 The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom you killed, hanging him on a tree. 31 God exalted him with his right hand to be a Prince and a Savior, to give repentance to Israel, and remission of sins. 32 We are his witnesses of these things; and so also is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to those who obey him.”
33 But they, when they heard this, were cut to the heart, and determined to kill them. 34 But one stood up in the council, a Pharisee named Gamaliel, a teacher of the law, honored by all the people, and commanded to put the apostles out for a little while. 35 He said to them, “You men of Israel, be careful concerning these men what you are about to do. 36 For before these days Theudas rose up, making himself out to be somebody; to whom a number of men, about four hundred, joined themselves: who was slain; and all, as many as obeyed him, were dispersed, and came to nothing. 37 After this man, Judas of Galilee rose up in the days of the enrollment, and drew away some people after him. He also perished; and all, as many as obeyed him, were scattered abroad. 38 Now I tell you, withdraw from these men, and leave them alone. For if this counsel or this work is of men, it will be overthrown. 39 But if it is of God, you will not be able to overthrow it, and you would be found even to be fighting against God.”
40 They agreed with him. Summoning the apostles, they beat them and commanded them not to speak in the name of Jesus, and let them go. 41 They therefore departed from the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the Name. 42 Every day, in the temple and at home, they didn’t cease to teach and to preach Jesus as the Christ.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • The Judgment on the House (v. 5-10): The immediate deaths of Ananias and Sapphira mirror the strange fire of Nadab and Abihu (Lev 10), signaling that the "House of God" is now the apostolic community.
  • Repentance to Israel (v. 31): Peter emphasizes that the opportunity for covenantal restoration was still open to the nation during this transitional generation.
  • Filled Jerusalem (v. 28): The high priest admits the Gospel had saturated the "great city" within a few short years, fulfilling the commission of Acts 1:8.
  • Shadow Healing (v. 15): A demonstration of the "greater works" promised by Jesus (John 14:12) before the close of the age.

4. Annotations

  • Satan (v. 3): In a covenantal sense, the "adversary" or the spirit of the old system seeking to corrupt the purity of the new remnant.
  • Solomon’s Porch (v. 12): A colonnade in the Temple where the church gathered publicly, claiming the most sacred ground for the New Covenant.
  • The Sect of the Sadducees (v. 17): The ruling class who rejected resurrection and was most threatened by the "power of the age to come."
  • Words of this Life (v. 20): A reference to the New Creation life made available through the indwelling Spirit.
  • Gamaliel (v. 34): A renowned Pharisee and teacher of Paul; his counsel unintentionally protected the growth of the church during its infancy.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

The Judicial Purging of the New Temple Foundation

The episode with Ananias and Sapphira is often misunderstood as a random act of severity. However, in the framework of fulfilled eschatology, it is a judicial sign. Just as God purged the Tabernacle at its inauguration (Nadab and Abihu) and the Land at the entry into Canaan (Achan), He now purges the New Covenant "assembly." The Holy Spirit was demonstrating that the true Temple is not made of stones, but of people. For this new "spiritual house" to remain the light of the world during the transition of the ages, its foundation had to be guarded against the leaven of hypocrisy. This judgment established the "Fear of the Lord" as the boundary of the New Creation.

Covenantal Validation Through Signs and Wonders

The "many signs and wonders" (v. 12) performed in Solomon's Porch were the legal credentials of the apostles. In the Hebrew mindset, a new covenant required the "signs of an apostle" to validate that God had indeed changed His method of dealing with humanity. The shadow of Peter healing the sick (v. 15) was a graphic illustration that the presence of God had left the Holy of Holies and was now moving through the streets in the bodies of the believers. This mass healing was a foretaste of the "restoration of all things" that was reaching its climax as the Old Covenant world faded away.

The Jealousy of the Old Priesthood

The reaction of the High Priest and the Sadducees (v. 17) was "jealousy"—the classic biblical response of an older, rejected system toward the new, favored one. The Sadducees were the "masters of the house" who saw their authority evaporating. Their arrest of the apostles was a desperate attempt to maintain control over the "covenant world" that was legally no longer theirs. This conflict represents the "war in heaven" (prophetically speaking) played out on earth: the old rulers of the darkness of this world fighting against the rising Sun of Righteousness.

Angelic Deliverance and the Command to Speak 'Life'

The angelic intervention (v. 19-20) proved that the "heavenly host" was now in the service of the New Covenant. The command to "speak in the temple... the words of this Life" was a direct provocation. God was not hiding His new work; He was putting it on display in the very center of the Old system. The "Life" they spoke of was the resurrection life that rendered the Temple sacrifices obsolete. This angelic jailbreak signaled that no human or "covenantal" prison could contain the Spirit that had been unleashed at Pentecost.

The Rejection of the Sanhedrin's Jurisdiction

Peter’s response to the Sanhedrin—"We must obey God rather than men"—was a formal rejection of their judicial authority. He further heightened the tension by charging them with the death of the "Prince and Savior" (v. 31). By using the term *archegos* (Prince/Author/Captain), Peter was identifying Jesus as the new Moses—the one who leads the people into the inheritance. The offer of "repentance to Israel" was the final call to the nation before the "last days" ended in the fires of AD 70. The Sanhedrin was effectively being told that their court was no longer the supreme court of the Kingdom.

Gamaliel and the Shaking of Human Counsel

Gamaliel’s advice (v. 38-39) provides a profound historical marker. He recognized that if the movement was "of God," it could not be overthrown. Within the fulfilled framework, we see that the New Covenant work *was* of God and it *was not* overthrown, despite the total destruction of the Jewish state in AD 70. The fact that the Church survived and flourished while the Sanhedrin and the Temple "came to nothing" (to use Gamaliel's phrase) is the ultimate historical proof of the apostolic testimony. The apostles' "rejoicing" in suffering (v. 41) showed that they had already entered a reality that was immune to the shakings of the earth.

8. New Creation Reality

The "Fear of the Lord" seen in Acts 5 is not a fear of random punishment, but a deep reverence for the presence of the Holy Spirit who now dwells within us. We are the Temple where God's purity resides. Because the work is 'of God' and has been proven by the survival of the Church through the end of the Old Covenant age, we walk with the same 'boldness' and 'rejoicing' as the apostles, knowing that no earthly power can overthrow the finished work of Christ.
