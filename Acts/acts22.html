Acts 22

1. Immediate Context

Acts 22 records Paul’s defense before the Jerusalem crowd that has just tried to kill him. Standing on the stairs of the Antonia fortress, he speaks to them in the Hebrew language. The silence deepens. He establishes his Jewish credentials: born in Tarsus, brought up in Jerusalem at the feet of Gamaliel, trained in the strict manner of the law, zealous for God as they all are. He recounts his former persecution of the Way — binding and delivering both men and women to prison, even traveling to Damascus with letters from the high priest to bring believers bound to Jerusalem for punishment. He then describes the light from heaven, the voice of Jesus of Nazareth whom he was persecuting, his blindness, the restoration of sight through Ananias (a devout man according to the law), and the commission to be a witness to all men of what he has seen and heard. He tells of a later vision in the temple in which the Lord commanded him to leave Jerusalem quickly because they would not receive his testimony, and sent him far away to the Gentiles. At the word “Gentiles” the crowd erupts: “Rid the earth of this fellow, for he isn’t fit to live!” They throw off their cloaks and toss dust into the air. The commander orders Paul brought into the barracks to be examined by scourging. When Paul asks whether it is lawful to scourge a Roman citizen uncondemned, the proceedings stop. The commander, learning that Paul is a Roman by birth, is afraid because he has bound him. The next day he frees Paul from his bonds and brings him before the chief priests and the whole council. The transition generation continues to hear the story of the persecutor turned apostle and the collision over the Gentile mission while the Temple still stands.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 “Brothers and fathers, listen to the defense which I now make to you.”

2 When they heard that he spoke to them in the Hebrew language, they were even more quiet. He said, 3 “I am indeed a Jew, born in Tarsus of Cilicia, but brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel, instructed according to the strict tradition of the law of our fathers, being zealous for God, even as you all are today. 4 I persecuted this Way to the death, binding and delivering into prisons both men and women, 5 as also the high priest and all the council of the elders testify, from whom also I received letters to the brothers, and traveled to Damascus to bring them also who were there to Jerusalem in bonds to be punished.

6 “As I made my journey and came close to Damascus, about noon suddenly a great light shone around me from the sky. 7 I fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to me, ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?’ 8 I answered, ‘Who are you, Lord?’ He said to me, ‘I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you persecute.’

9 “Those who were with me indeed saw the light and were afraid, but they didn’t understand the voice of him who spoke to me. 10 I said, ‘What shall I do, Lord?’ The Lord said to me, ‘Arise, and go into Damascus. There you will be told about all things which are appointed for you to do.’ 11 When I couldn’t see for the glory of that light, being led by the hand of those who were with me, I came into Damascus.

12 “One Ananias, a devout man according to the law, well reported of by all the Jews who lived in Damascus, 13 came to me, and standing by me said to me, ‘Brother Saul, receive your sight!’ In that very hour I looked up at him. 14 He said, ‘The God of our fathers has appointed you to know his will, and to see the Righteous One, and to hear a voice from his mouth. 15 For you will be a witness for him to all men of what you have seen and heard. 16 Now why do you wait? Arise, be baptized, and wash away your sins, calling on the name of the Lord.’

17 “When I had returned to Jerusalem and while I prayed in the temple, I fell into a trance 18 and saw him saying to me, ‘Hurry and get out of Jerusalem quickly, because they will not receive testimony concerning me from you.’ 19 I said, ‘Lord, they themselves know that I imprisoned and beat in every synagogue those who believed in you. 20 When the blood of Stephen, your witness, was shed, I also was standing by, consenting to his death, and guarding the cloaks of those who killed him.’

21 “He said to me, ‘Depart, for I will send you out far from here to the Gentiles.’ ”

22 They listened to him until he said that; then they lifted up their voice and said, “Rid the earth of this fellow, for he isn’t fit to live!”

23 As they cried out, threw off their cloaks, and threw dust into the air, 24 the commanding officer commanded him to be brought into the barracks, ordering him to be examined by scourging, that he might know for what crime they shouted against him like that. 25 When they had tied him up with straps, Paul asked the centurion who stood by, “Is it lawful for you to scourge a man who is a Roman, and not found guilty?”

26 When the centurion heard it, he went to the commanding officer and told him, “Watch what you are about to do, for this man is a Roman!” 27 The commanding officer came and asked him, “Tell me, are you a Roman?” He said, “Yes.” 28 The commanding officer answered, “I bought my citizenship for a great price.” Paul said, “But I was born a Roman.”

29 Immediately those who were about to examine him departed from him, and the commanding officer also was afraid when he realized that he was a Roman, because he had bound him. 30 But on the next day, desiring to know the truth about why he was accused by the Jews, he freed him from the bonds and commanded the chief priests and all the council to come together, and brought Paul down and set him before them.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • “I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you persecute” (v. 8): The risen Lord identifies so closely with His people that to persecute them is to persecute Him. The same identification that stopped Saul now stands at the center of Paul’s testimony.
  • “You will be a witness for him to all men” (v. 15): The commission is to all people. The scope is already universal in the word spoken by Ananias.
  • “I will send you out far from here to the Gentiles” (v. 21): The decisive word that ends the crowd’s patience. The Gentile mission is not Paul’s idea; it is the Lord’s direct command given in the temple itself.
  • The eruption at the word “Gentiles” (v. 22): The crowd listens carefully to the account of Paul’s Jewish zeal and even to the heavenly vision — until the Gentiles are named as the object of the Lord’s sending. The dividing line is the inclusion of the nations.
  • Roman citizenship (vv. 25–29): The legal status that prevents scourging and forces the commander to proceed more carefully. God uses even Roman law to protect His servant for further witness.

4. Annotations

  • Hebrew language (v. 2): Paul addresses the crowd in their own language (Aramaic/Hebrew). The silence deepens because he is one of them, speaking as a fellow Jew.
  • At the feet of Gamaliel (v. 3): The most respected Pharisaic teacher of the generation. Paul’s credentials are impeccable by the standards of the crowd.
  • Ananias, a devout man according to the law (v. 12): Paul emphasizes that the man who restored his sight and conveyed the commission was himself a law-observant, well-respected Jew. The message did not come through a lawless channel.
  • The temple vision (vv. 17–21): Paul locates the commission to the Gentiles in a trance while praying in the temple. The sending to the nations is announced in the very heart of the old covenant center.
  • Born a Roman (v. 28): Paul’s citizenship is by birth, superior to the commander’s purchased status. The legal protection is real and immediately effective.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

Establishing Common Ground

Paul begins where the crowd is. He is a Jew, raised in Jerusalem, trained under Gamaliel in the strict manner of the law, zealous for God just as they are. He once persecuted the Way to the death, binding men and women and traveling to Damascus with official letters to bring believers back for punishment. The high priest and the whole council of elders can testify to these things. He is not a stranger or a renegade; he is one of them who has been stopped and redirected by the Lord Himself.

The Damascus Encounter

The light from heaven, the voice, the question “Why are you persecuting me?”, the identification “I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you persecute,” the blindness, the leading by the hand into Damascus — these are the same events Luke has already recorded in Acts 9. Paul now tells them in the first person to the very people who share his former zeal. Ananias, a devout man according to the law and well spoken of by all the Jews in Damascus, restores his sight and conveys the commission: the God of their fathers has appointed him to know His will, to see the Righteous One, to hear a voice from His mouth, and to be a witness to all men of what he has seen and heard. Baptism and the washing away of sins follow as he calls on the name of the Lord.

The Temple Commission to the Gentiles

Paul then recounts a later experience while praying in the temple in Jerusalem. In a trance he sees the Lord telling him to leave Jerusalem quickly because the people will not receive his testimony. Paul protests that they know his former record — the imprisonments, the beatings in the synagogues, his consent to the death of Stephen. The Lord’s answer is decisive: “Depart, for I will send you out far from here to the Gentiles.” The commission to the nations is given in the temple itself. The crowd has listened in silence through the entire account of Paul’s Jewish credentials, his former persecution, the heavenly vision, and the restoration of sight. The moment the Gentiles are named as the object of the Lord’s sending, the silence shatters.

The Eruption and the Citizenship

“Rid the earth of this fellow, for he isn’t fit to live!” They throw off their cloaks and toss dust into the air. The commander, unable to understand the reason for the uproar, orders Paul brought inside to be examined by scourging. As the straps are being tied, Paul asks whether it is lawful to scourge a Roman citizen who has not been found guilty. The centurion reports it; the commander comes and confirms the citizenship. Those who were about to examine him withdraw. The commander is afraid because he has bound a Roman. The next day, seeking to know the real accusation, he frees Paul from the bonds and brings him before the chief priests and the whole council. The legal protection of Roman citizenship opens the next stage of witness.

Transition Generation Significance

Acts 22 places the story of the persecutor-turned-apostle before the very crowd that still embodies the zeal Paul once shared. The offense is not the heavenly vision or the claim that Jesus of Nazareth spoke from heaven. The offense is the Lord’s command to send him far away to the Gentiles. The inclusion of the nations remains the flashpoint. The same temple that still stands is the place where the commission to the Gentiles was given. The collision between the New Covenant mission to the nations and the still-present old covenant center is now public and verbal. Paul will next stand before the council itself.

8. New Creation Reality

Acts 22 places the New Creation commission in the mouth of the former persecutor standing on the stairs of the fortress, speaking to the crowd that still shares his old zeal. The Lord who stopped him on the road is the same Lord who, while he prayed in the temple, sent him far away to the Gentiles. The offense is not the claim of a heavenly vision; the offense is the inclusion of the nations. The one new man is being formed from both Jew and Gentile, and the announcement of that reality still provokes fury in the shadow of the still-standing temple. Roman citizenship becomes the means by which the witness is preserved for the next stage. The word continues to go forward even when the crowd cries that the messenger is not fit to live.

9. Reflection

The crowd listened carefully to everything Paul said about his Jewish credentials, his former persecution, and even the heavenly vision — until he mentioned the Gentiles. What does this reveal about the particular offense of the Gentile mission? How does Paul’s willingness to identify fully with his hearers (“zealous for God, even as you all are today”) while still telling the whole truth shape the way we bear witness across deep differences?
