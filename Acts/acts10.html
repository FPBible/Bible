Acts 10

1. Immediate Context

Acts 10 records one of the most decisive turning points in the transition generation. After the conversion of Saul and a season of peace for the churches in Judea, Galilee, and Samaria, the Lord now opens the door of the New Covenant fully to the Gentiles. A devout Roman centurion named Cornelius receives a vision. Simultaneously, Peter receives a vision that dismantles the categories of clean and unclean as they had been applied to people. When Peter preaches in Cornelius’s house, the Holy Spirit falls on uncircumcised Gentiles exactly as He had on the Jewish believers at Pentecost. This event demonstrates that the dividing wall of hostility is being removed and that the “one new man” in Christ is being formed while the Temple still stands and before the old covenant world is judged in AD 70.

2. Scripture (WEB)

1 Now there was a certain man in Caesarea, Cornelius by name, a centurion of what was called the Italian Regiment, 2 a devout man, and one who feared God with all his house, who gave gifts for the needy generously to the people, and always prayed to God. 3 At about the ninth hour of the day, he clearly saw in a vision an angel of God coming to him and saying to him, “Cornelius!”

4 He, fastening his eyes on him and being frightened, said, “What is it, Lord?”

He said to him, “Your prayers and your gifts to the needy have gone up for a memorial before God. 5 Now send men to Joppa, and get Simon, who is also called Peter. 6 He is staying with a tanner named Simon, whose house is by the seaside.”

7 When the angel who spoke to him had departed, Cornelius called two of his household servants and a devout soldier of those who waited on him continually. 8 Having explained everything to them, he sent them to Joppa.

9 Now on the next day as they were on their journey and got close to the city, Peter went up on the housetop to pray at about noon. 10 He became hungry and desired to eat, but while they were preparing, he fell into a trance. 11 He saw heaven opened and a certain container descending to him, like a great sheet let down by four corners on the earth, 12 in which were all kinds of four-footed animals of the earth, wild animals, reptiles, and birds of the sky. 13 A voice came to him, “Rise, Peter, kill and eat!”

14 But Peter said, “Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten anything that is common or unclean.”

15 A voice came to him again the second time, “What God has cleansed, you must not call unclean.” 16 This was done three times, and immediately the thing was received up into heaven.

17 Now while Peter was very perplexed in himself what the vision which he had seen might mean, behold, the men who were sent by Cornelius, having made inquiry for Simon’s house, stood before the gate, 18 and called and asked whether Simon, who was also called Peter, was lodging there. 19 While Peter was pondering the vision, the Spirit said to him, “Behold, three men seek you. 20 But arise, get down, and go with them, doubting nothing; for I have sent them.”

21 Peter went down to the men, and said, “Behold, I am he whom you seek. Why have you come?”

22 They said, “Cornelius, a centurion, a righteous man and one who fears God, and well spoken of by all the nation of the Jews, was directed by a holy angel to invite you to his house, and to listen to what you say.” 23 So he called them in and provided a place to stay.

On the next day Peter arose and went out with them, and some of the brothers from Joppa accompanied him. 24 On the next day they entered into Caesarea. Cornelius was waiting for them, having called together his relatives and his near friends. 25 When Peter entered, Cornelius met him, fell down at his feet, and worshiped him. 26 But Peter raised him up, saying, “Stand up! I myself am also a man.” 27 As he talked with him, he went in and found many gathered together. 28 He said to them, “You yourselves know how it is an unlawful thing for a man who is a Jew to join himself or come to one of another nation, but God has shown me that I shouldn’t call any man unholy or unclean. 29 Therefore I also came without complaint when I was sent for. I ask therefore, why did you send for me?”

30 Cornelius said, “Four days ago, I was fasting until this hour; and at the ninth hour, I prayed in my house, and behold, a man stood before me in bright clothing 31 and said, ‘Cornelius, your prayer is heard, and your gifts to the needy are remembered in the sight of God. 32 Send therefore to Joppa and summon Simon, who is also called Peter. He is staying in the house of a tanner named Simon, by the seaside. When he comes, he will speak to you.’ 33 Therefore I sent to you at once, and it was good of you to come. Now therefore we are all here present in the sight of God to hear all things that have been commanded you by God.”

34 Peter opened his mouth and said, “Truly I perceive that God doesn’t show favoritism; 35 but in every nation he who fears him and works righteousness is acceptable to him. 36 The word which he sent to the children of Israel, preaching good news of peace by Jesus Christ—he is Lord of all— 37 you yourselves know what happened, which was proclaimed throughout all Judea, beginning from Galilee, after the baptism which John preached; 38 how God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power, who went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with him. 39 We are witnesses of everything he did both in the country of the Jews and in Jerusalem; whom they also killed, hanging him on a tree. 40 God raised him up the third day and gave him to be revealed, 41 not to all the people, but to witnesses who were chosen before by God, to us, who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead. 42 He commanded us to preach to the people and to testify that this is he who is appointed by God as the Judge of the living and the dead. 43 All the prophets testify about him, that through his name everyone who believes in him will receive remission of sins.”

44 While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell on all those who heard the word. 45 They of the circumcision who believed were amazed, as many as came with Peter, because the gift of the Holy Spirit was also poured out on the Gentiles. 46 For they heard them speaking in other languages and magnifying God.

Then Peter answered, 47 “Can anyone forbid these people from being baptized with water? They have received the Holy Spirit just like us.” 48 He commanded them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. Then they asked him to stay some days.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • “What God has cleansed, you must not call unclean” (v. 15): The vision is not primarily about dietary freedom but about people. God declares the Gentiles cleansed by the blood of Christ; the old covenantal categories of clean/unclean that separated Jew and Gentile are being set aside.
  • God shows no favoritism (vv. 34–35): Peter’s confession is the theological heart of the chapter. Acceptability before God is no longer based on physical descent, circumcision, or Torah observance, but on fearing God and believing the gospel.
  • The Spirit falls on uncircumcised Gentiles (vv. 44–46): Exactly as at Pentecost, the Spirit is given without the intermediate steps of the Law. This is the decisive sign that the New Covenant includes the nations on the same basis as believing Jews.
  • “Just like us” (v. 47): Peter recognizes that the Gentiles have received the same gift. There is one baptism of the Spirit for the one new man.
  • Baptism follows the Spirit (v. 48): Water baptism is administered after the clear evidence of Spirit reception, confirming that the outward sign follows the inward reality of New Covenant inclusion.

4. Annotations

  • Cornelius, a centurion of the Italian Regiment (v. 1): A Roman officer of some rank, stationed in Caesarea, the Roman administrative center of Judea. He is a “God-fearer” — a Gentile who worshiped the God of Israel without full conversion to Judaism.
  • Ninth hour (v. 3): 3:00 p.m., the hour of the afternoon temple sacrifice and prayer. God meets Cornelius in the rhythm of Jewish piety, yet will lead him beyond it.
  • Simon the tanner (v. 6): Tanners worked with dead animals and were considered ceremonially unclean. Peter is already lodging in a place that stretched Jewish purity boundaries.
  • The sheet vision (vv. 11–16): Repeated three times for emphasis. The command “kill and eat” confronts Peter’s lifelong scruples. The interpretation is given immediately in the arrival of the Gentile messengers.
  • “I myself am also a man” (v. 26): Peter refuses worship. The gospel levels all human distinctions of status or ethnicity.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

The Sovereign Orchestration

Two visions, carefully timed, bring a Roman centurion and a Jewish apostle together. Neither initiated the contact. God Himself is dismantling the wall that had stood for centuries. Cornelius is already a man of prayer and generosity; Peter is already a Spirit-filled apostle. Yet both require a direct intervention from heaven before the next step of the New Covenant can be taken. This is not human strategy or progressive openness; it is the Lord of the harvest directing the transition generation.

The Vision That Reinterprets Purity

Peter’s protest — “I have never eaten anything that is common or unclean” — reveals how deeply the old covenant purity system shaped Jewish identity. The voice from heaven does not merely relax food laws; it redefines the categories themselves. “What God has cleansed” points forward to the cross, where Christ made both Jew and Gentile clean through His blood. The immediate application is to people, not animals. When the messengers from Cornelius arrive, the Spirit makes the meaning plain: go with them, doubting nothing. The vision prepares Peter to enter a Gentile house without the old scruples that would have kept the gospel locked inside ethnic Israel.

God Shows No Favoritism

Peter’s opening words in Cornelius’s house are revolutionary: “Truly I perceive that God doesn’t show favoritism.” Under the old covenant, Israel stood in a unique, privileged relationship to God. Now, in the New Covenant, that privilege is opened to every nation on the same terms — fear of God and faith in the Messiah. This is the outworking of the Abrahamic promise that in him all the families of the earth would be blessed. It is also the practical dismantling of the “dividing wall of hostility” that Paul later describes in Ephesians 2. The one new man is being created in real time, while the Temple still stands and the old order still has a few decades left.

The Spirit Falls Without Distinction

While Peter is still speaking, the Holy Spirit falls on all who hear the word. The Jewish believers who accompanied Peter are astonished. The same gift that had been poured out on them at Pentecost is now given to uncircumcised Gentiles. There is no laying on of hands, no prior circumcision, no requirement to keep the Law of Moses. The Spirit Himself is the seal of full inclusion. Peter’s response is immediate and logical: if God has already given them the Spirit, who can forbid water baptism? The order is significant — Spirit first, then the outward sign. This pattern will become the New Covenant norm.

Implications for the Transition Generation

Acts 10 is not a late development in church history; it occurs within the living memory of the apostles and within the forty-year generation that Jesus said would not pass away before the end of the age. The gospel is going to the nations while the old covenant structures still exist, so that the fullness of the Gentiles can come in and the remnant of Israel can be saved before the judgment falls on Jerusalem. The inclusion of Cornelius’s household is a down payment on the worldwide harvest that will be gathered before AD 70. It is also a living demonstration that the New Creation is not delayed; it is already breaking in.

8. New Creation Reality

Acts 10 reveals the New Creation breaking into the old order. The categories that defined identity under the Law — clean and unclean, Jew and Gentile, insider and outsider — are being transcended by the blood of Christ and the gift of the Spirit. Cornelius does not become a Jew in order to become a Christian; he is accepted as a Gentile believer. Peter does not abandon his Jewishness, but he learns that it is no longer the boundary of the people of God. In the New Covenant, the Spirit creates one family from every nation. This is not a future hope delayed until some later age; it is the present reality of the Eighth Day, already being lived out in Caesarea while the Temple still stands. The same Spirit who fell on Cornelius’s household still falls on all who believe, making them full members of the one new man in Christ.

9. Reflection

Peter had to overcome deeply ingrained categories of clean and unclean before he could fully embrace the Gentiles as brothers. What categories — cultural, ethnic, social, or religious — still linger in your own heart that might hinder you from freely welcoming those whom God has already cleansed by the blood of Christ? How does the story of Cornelius challenge you to see the New Covenant people of God as already one, without the old dividing walls?
