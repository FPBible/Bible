Acts 4

1. Immediate Context

Following the miracle at the Beautiful Gate in Acts 3, the apostolic witness faces its first official state opposition. Acts 4 documents the arrest and trial of Peter and John by the Sanhedrin, specifically the Sadducees who were "greatly troubled" by the proclamation of the resurrection. This chapter establishes the legal and spiritual conflict between the dying Old Covenant hierarchy and the emerging New Covenant Kingdom. It culminates in a corporate prayer that identifies the local persecution as the direct fulfillment of Psalm 2, signaling that the "Kings of the Earth" (local rulers) had indeed gathered against the Lord’s Anointed.

2. Scripture (WEB)

Source (verbatim): https://ebible.org/web/ACT04.htm

1 As they spoke to the people, the priests and the captain of the temple and the Sadducees came to them, 2 being greatly troubled because they taught the people and proclaimed in Jesus the resurrection from the dead. 3 They laid hands on them, and put them in custody until the next day, for it was now evening. 4 But many of those who heard the word believed, and the number of the men came to be about five thousand.
5 In the morning, their rulers, elders, and scribes were gathered together in Jerusalem. 6 Annas the high priest was there, with Caiaphas, John, Alexander, and as many as were of the relatives of the high priest. 7 When they had stood them in the middle, they inquired, “By what power, or in what name, have you done this?”
8 Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, said to them, “You rulers of the people, and elders of Israel, 9 if we are examined today concerning a good deed done to a crippled man, by what means this man has been healed, 10 let it be known to you all, and to all the people of Israel, that in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead, in him this man stands here before you whole. 11 He is ‘the stone which was regarded as worthless by you, the builders, which has become the head of the corner.’ 12 There is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven that is given among men, by which we must be saved.”
13 Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and had perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marveled. They recognized that they had been with Jesus. 14 Seeing the man who was healed standing with them, they could say nothing against it. 15 But when they had commanded them to go aside out of the council, they conferred among themselves, 16 saying, “What shall we do to these men? For indeed, that a notable miracle has been done through them is plain to all who dwell in Jerusalem, and we can’t deny it. 17 But so that this spreads no further among the people, let’s threaten them, that from now on they don’t speak to anyone in this name.” 18 They called them, and commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus.
19 But Peter and John answered them, “Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, judge for yourselves, 20 for we can’t help telling the things which we saw and heard.”
21 When they had further threatened them, they let them go, finding no way to punish them, because of the people; for everyone glorified God for that which was done. 22 For the man was more than forty years old on whom this miracle of healing was performed.
23 Being let go, they came to their own company, and reported all that the chief priests and the elders had said to them. 24 When they heard it, they lifted up their voice to God with one accord, and said, “O Lord, you are God, who made the heaven, the earth, the sea, and all that is in them; 25 who by the mouth of your servant, David, said,
‘Why did the nations rage,
and the peoples plot a vain thing?
26 The kings of the earth take a stand,
and the rulers were gathered together,
against the Lord, and against his Anointed.’
27 “For truly, in this city against your holy servant, Jesus, whom you anointed, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the people of Israel, were gathered together 28 to do whatever your hand and your council foreordained to happen. 29 Now, Lord, look at their threats, and grant to your servants to speak your word with all boldness, 30 while you stretch out your hand to heal; and that signs and wonders may be done through the name of your holy servant, Jesus.”
31 When they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were gathered together. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they spoke the word of God with boldness.
32 The multitude of those who believed were of one heart and soul. Not one of them claimed that anything of the things which he possessed was his own, but they had all things in common. 33 With great power, the apostles gave their testimony of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. Great grace was on them all. 34 For neither was there among them any who lacked, for as many as were owners of lands or houses sold them, and brought the proceeds of the things that were sold, 35 and laid them at the apostles’ feet. Then distribution was made to each, according as anyone had need. 36 Joses, who by the apostles was surnamed Barnabas (which is, being interpreted, Son of Encouragement), a Levite, a man of Cyprus by race, 37 having a field, sold it, and brought the money and laid it at the apostles’ feet.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • The Builders (v. 11): Peter identifies the Sanhedrin as the "builders" of Psalm 118:22, confirming they were the generation responsible for the rejection of the cornerstone.
  • Psalm 2 Context (v. 25-26): The early church explicitly applies the "raging nations" and "kings of the earth" to the local Judean rulers and Roman authorities in Jerusalem.
  • The Shaking (v. 31): A divine indicator of covenantal upheaval (Haggai 2:6; Hebrews 12:26), signaling the passing of the Old system and the establishment of the New.
  • Five Thousand (v. 4): The rapid growth of the "remnant" during the transition period, fulfilling the promise of a harvest before the end of the age.

4. Annotations

  • Sadducees (v. 1): The priestly aristocracy who denied the resurrection; their opposition was primarily due to the threat Jesus' reign posed to their Temple-based authority.
  • Cornerstone (v. 11): The primary support stone of a building; used here to show Jesus is the foundation of the New Temple, displacing the physical one.
  • Ignorant Men (v. 13): Greek agrammatoi; meaning they were not trained in the Rabbinic schools, yet possessed a wisdom that superseded the Jewish elites.
  • In This City (v. 27): A crucial marker of audience relevance; the "kings" and "nations" of Psalm 2 were acting right there in first-century Jerusalem.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

The Sadducees and the Resurrection Conflict (v. 1-4)

The arrest of Peter and John was triggered specifically by the Sadducees, who controlled the Temple and held a theological worldview that denied any afterlife or resurrection. For them, the proclamation of Jesus' resurrection was not merely a religious disagreement; it was a direct assault on the legitimacy of the current high-priestly order. If Jesus were alive and raised, then the Sanhedrin were not only murderers but were legally deposed by the true King. This conflict highlights the "already" reality of Christ's reign—the resurrection had already begun the process of making all things new, which the old guard was desperate to suppress.

The Rejected Stone and the New Temple Foundation (v. 11)

When Peter quotes Psalm 118, he is delivering a judicial sentence against the "builders"—the religious leaders of Israel. In the Old Testament context, the builders were tasked with constructing God's house. Peter declares that they have failed their primary task by rejecting the very Cornerstone upon which the true House of God is built. By identifying Jesus as the "head of the corner," Peter is announcing that a New Temple is being erected in the midst of the old one. The physical Temple in which they stood was now legally "worthless" because it lacked the Cornerstone. This represents the core of the covenant transition: the movement from a building made of stone to a body built upon the Living Christ.

Exclusive Covenantal Authority: No Other Name (v. 12)

Verse 12 is often used as a general statement of salvation, but in its first-century context, it was an explosive claim of exclusive covenantal authority. Under the Old Covenant, the "name" of the high priest and the sacrificial system provided a temporary "salvation" or standing before God. Peter is asserting that this system is now obsolete. There is no other name "under heaven"—within the entire scope of the covenant world—that carries the power to save. This statement effectively excommunicated the Sanhedrin from the true Israel unless they submitted to the name of Jesus. It was the definitive declaration that the "End of the Law" had arrived in the Person of the Messiah.

The Kings of the Earth and the Psalm 2 Reality (v. 24-28)

The corporate prayer of the church in verses 24-28 provides a masterclass in fulfilled hermeneutics. Rather than pushing Psalm 2 into a distant global future, they saw it as being fulfilled "in this city" (Jerusalem). They identified "Herod and Pontius Pilate" as the kings and rulers, and the "Gentiles and people of Israel" as the nations and peoples. This demonstrates that the "great tribulation" and the "raging of nations" associated with the Day of the Lord was the local, first-century opposition to the Gospel. The "kings of the earth" in prophetic language often referred to the local rulers of the Land (the oikoumene or the land of Israel), and the apostles recognized that the battle of the ages was happening before their eyes.

The Shaking of the Earth: A Sign of Judgment (v. 31)

When the place was "shaken," it was more than a physical miracle; it was a prophetic sign. In the Old Testament, God’s presence shaking the earth always signified a change in the governing order or the fall of a kingdom. This shaking was a precursor to the "shaking of heaven and earth" described in Hebrews 12:26-27, which resulted in the removal of the Old Covenant system so that "the things which cannot be shaken"—the New Covenant Kingdom—might remain. The boldness given to the apostles through this shaking was the spiritual empowerment necessary to stand through the "last days" of the Jewish age.

Corporate Unity as the New Jerusalem Blueprint (v. 32-37)

The radical sharing of resources was not a mandate for modern socialism, but a practical expression of the "New Creation" reality among the first-century remnant. Knowing that the judgment of AD 70 was approaching and that the physical Jerusalem was marked for destruction, the believers detached themselves from the Old Covenant economy. Selling "lands and houses" was an act of faith that the old world was passing away. They were investing in the New Jerusalem—the corporate body of Christ. This unity was the "Great Grace" that validated their testimony of the resurrection; they lived as though the Kingdom had already arrived, because for them, it had.

8. New Creation Reality

In Acts 4, we see that the opposition of the world does not signal a delay in the Kingdom, but rather the desperate reaction of a dying system. We do not look for a future foundation to be laid; the Cornerstone is already the "head of the corner." Because there is no other name under heaven by which we are saved, we walk in an absolute spiritual authority that is not intimidated by the "shaking" of earthly institutions or the threats of temporal rulers.

9. Reflection

If the early church saw the fulfillment of the "kings of the earth" raging as a local event in Jerusalem, how does that simplify your understanding of the "battles" described in the book of Revelation?
