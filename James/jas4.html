James 4

Humility, Desire, and Friendship with God

1. Immediate Context

James traces conflict to its root: pleasures that wage war in the members. The assemblies are fighting, coveting, and asking badly because desire has replaced trust. He names that divided loyalty with the prophets’ hardest word: adultery. Friendship with the world is enmity with God. In the last days of the old order, “the world” is not a vague culture-war slogan. It is the stained system of status, violence, and self-rule that is about to be judged. James then calls them near to God — clean hands, purified hearts — and dismantles the last illusion: “we will go to this city, spend a year, trade, and make a profit.”

2. Scripture (WEB)

Source (verbatim): https://ebible.org/web/JAS04.htm

1 Where do wars and fightings among you come from? Don’t they come from your pleasures that war in your members?
2 You lust, and don’t have. You murder and covet, and can’t obtain. You fight and make war. You don’t have, because you don’t ask.
3 You ask, and don’t receive, because you ask with wrong motives, so that you may spend it on your pleasures.
4 You adulterers and adulteresses, don’t you know that friendship with the world is hostility toward God? Whoever therefore wants to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God.
5 Or do you think that the Scripture says in vain, “The Spirit who lives in us yearns jealously”?
6 But he gives more grace. Therefore it says, “God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.”
7 Be subject therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.
8 Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, you sinners. Purify your hearts, you double-minded.
9 Lament, mourn, and weep. Let your laughter be turned to mourning and your joy to gloom.
10 Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he will exalt you.
11 Don’t speak against one another, brothers. He who speaks against a brother and judges his brother, speaks against the law and judges the law. But if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law but a judge.
12 Only one is the lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy. But who are you to judge another?
13 Come now, you who say, “Today or tomorrow let’s go into this city and spend a year there, trade, and make a profit.”
14 Yet you don’t know what your life will be like tomorrow. For what is your life? For you are a vapor that appears for a little time and then vanishes away.
15 For you ought to say, “If the Lord wills, we will both live, and do this or that.”
16 But now you glory in your boasting. All such boasting is evil.
17 To him therefore who knows to do good and doesn’t do it, to him it is sin.

3. Fulfillment Notes

  • Adultery language: “You adulterers and adulteresses” (v. 4) is covenant-jealousy speech from the prophets, not a private morality lecture only.
  • The world: Friendship with the world (v. 4) is alliance with the old age’s desires and rankings as that age nears its end.
  • Near to God: “Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you” (v. 8) is present restoration, not a postponed presence.
  • You don’t know what tomorrow will bring (v. 14) — humility before the shaking of that generation; life is a vapour, plans are not lord.

4. Annotations

  • Wars and fightings — not geopolitics first; desire in the members producing communal violence.
  • He yearns jealously — God’s Spirit is not indifferent to divided hearts; jealousy here is covenant love, not insecurity.
  • Resist the devil — the accuser’s order flees where people submit to God; not a later theatrical rite.
  • One lawgiver and judge — stop playing God over a brother’s life; the old world’s judging-seat is not yours.

5. Fulfillment Commentary

Conflict begins where desire replaces trust

“Where do wars and fightings among you come from?” James does not start with Rome or with synagogue politics. He starts inside. Pleasures that war in your members. You lust and do not have. You kill and covet and cannot obtain. You fight. You ask and do not receive, because you ask so that you may spend it on your pleasures.

This is the same desire-story as chapter 1, now grown into communal wreckage. When desire becomes lord, prayer becomes a tool and the brother becomes an obstacle. New Creation life does not pretend this is “just personality.” It names it as war in the members — the old Adamic engine still trying to run the assembly.

Friendship with the world is covenant adultery

James reaches for Hosea’s vocabulary: adulterers and adulteresses. Friendship with the world is enmity with God. Whoever wants to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God. In the 50s–60s, “the world” is the honour-system, the violence-system, the self-sufficient trading-system of an age God is about to expose.

This is not a call to hatred of people. It is a call to refuse alliance with a dying order. God’s jealousy is the jealousy of a husband, not the ego of a tyrant. “The Spirit who lives in us yearns jealously.” He will not share the bride with the world that crucified the Lord of glory. Grace is greater — He gives more grace to the humble — but He does not bless a double bed.

Submission is alignment, not humiliation

“Be subject therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you.” That is present tense restoration. Cleanse hands, purify hearts, you double-minded. Lament. Let laughter be turned to mourning. Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and He will exalt you.

James is not installing a shame culture. He is calling a divided people back to a single loyalty. The devil flees where God is actually submitted to — because the accuser’s power in that generation was accusation, rivalry, and the old world’s fear. Drawing near is not climbing a ladder. It is coming home to the Father who already brought them forth by the word of truth.

Stop playing God over one another

“Don’t speak against one another, brothers.” The one who judges a brother speaks against the law and judges the law. There is one lawgiver and judge, able to save and to destroy. Who are you to judge your neighbour?

This is the old world again: the judging-seat. Assemblies under pressure love to appoint themselves as each other’s covenant court. James forbids it. Mercy was the litmus in chapter 2. Humility is the litmus here. You are not the Lawgiver. In a generation about to see the true Judge act in history, playing God over a brother is a dangerous game.

Control is the last illusion James dismantles

“Today or tomorrow we will go into this city, spend a year, trade, and make a profit.” James calls the boast evil. You do not know what tomorrow will bring. You are a vapour. You ought to say, “If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that.” In the years before the war and the fall of the city, that sentence was not a pious sticker. It was realism.

To the one who knows the good and does not do it, it is sin. James will not let knowledge remain theoretical. New Creation life holds plans with an open hand because the Lord is actually Lord. The old world sold certainty: routes, profits, calendars. The firstfruits people live by “if the Lord wills” — not as superstition, but as friendship with God instead of friendship with control.

8. Key Idioms

  • Adulterers and adulteresses — prophetic covenant language for divided loyalty, not only sexual scandal.
  • Friendship with the world — alliance with the old age’s desires, rankings, and control.
  • Draw near — present restoration of communion; God is not postponing His nearness.
  • Vapour — life’s brevity under God’s will; the death-blow to the illusion of control.

9. New Creation Reality

God’s jealousy is friendship, not threat. New Creation life draws near now, resists the accuser now, and holds tomorrow with an open hand. You do not need the world’s friendship to be safe. You already have the Father who gives more grace to the humble.

10. Reflection

Where have I still made friendship with control, status, or the old world’s certainty — and called it prudence instead of adultery?
