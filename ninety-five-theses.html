Ninety-Five Theses Against the Unfinished Reformation

The Reformation recovered much, yet it remains unfinished. These propositions examine what must still be left behind so that the body of Christ in 2026 fully aligns with the Eighth Day reality: the Old Covenant age ended in 70 AD, the dividing wall is gone, there is one new man, every believer is a priest, and Christ alone is Mediator.

Out of love for the truth and the desire to bring it into the open, the following propositions are set forth for examination. They concern the unfinished work of the Reformation and the call to complete it in light of the New Creation already established in Christ.

In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.

On the New Creation and the end of the Old Covenant order

  1. When our Lord and Master Jesus Christ said the kingdom of God is at hand, He meant the decisive transition from the Old Covenant age to the New Creation reality, not an indefinitely postponed future program.
  2. The destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple in 70 AD was the judgment on that generation and the public end of the Old Covenant administration, not a temporary setback awaiting restoration.
  3. In Christ the dividing wall of hostility has been broken down; there is one new man, not two peoples with parallel destinies.
  4. The priesthood of the Old Covenant ended; every believer is a priest. No human office re-creates a mediating class.
  5. Pastors, elders, and teachers are servants who declare what is already true in Christ; they do not occupy a higher spiritual estate than the body.
  6. Any system that treats a pastor’s interpretation, a confession, or a denominational standard as functionally binding on conscience has reintroduced a magisterium under a different name.
  7. The Reformation replaced the Pope with pastors, synods, and confessions while retaining the logic that some men stand between the people and the Word.
  8. Assemblies that still require a specially ordained person to administer the Word or the Supper operate contrary to the priesthood of all believers.
  9. The claim that “sola scriptura” is practiced while members are expected to submit to the pastor’s interpretation or the denomination’s confession is a distinction without a difference.
  10. The Eighth Day has already begun. What remains is for the body to stop living as though the Old Covenant structures still govern.

On the unfinished break with mediating authority

  1. The magisterial Reformers rejected the Pope yet retained the principle that human authority may bind the conscience of the believer on doctrine and practice.
  2. True freedom of conscience in the New Creation is the direct access every believer has through Christ alone.
  3. Any structure that inserts an obligatory human intermediary between the believer and Christ has not yet left the old order.
  4. Confessions and statements of faith may clarify teaching; when they become the boundary of fellowship more strictly than the apostles’ teaching on the one new man, they have exceeded their place.
  5. The pastoral office is described in the New Testament as service, not as a continuing mediating priesthood.
  6. Language that elevates a pastor as “God’s man,” “spiritual covering,” or untouchable authority re-creates the status once claimed by popes and bishops.
  7. The body is not an audience. Every member is a priest offering spiritual sacrifices acceptable through Christ.
  8. Systems that concentrate authority in a single personality or brand reduce the body to spectators of someone else’s ministry.
  9. Denominational hierarchies that bind conscience beyond the local assembly reintroduce the logic the Reformation claimed to leave behind.
  10. The unfinished Reformation is visible wherever ordinary believers are kept in tutelage, waiting for an official interpretation before they may walk in the New Creation.

On the persistence of Old Covenant categories

  1. Dispensationalism reintroduced a permanent distinction between Israel and the Church that the New Testament does not maintain once the wall is removed.
  2. The system treats land promises and ethnic identity as still operative after the cross and after 70 AD, contrary to the finality of what Christ accomplished.
  3. A future restoration of ethnic Israel, a rebuilt temple, a seven-year tribulation, and a pre-tribulation rapture keep the body waiting for what the New Testament declares already present or already judged.
  4. By preserving a future for ethnic Israel distinct from the one new man, the system denies that Christ is all and in all.
  5. The land was never a bloodline prize that continues after the Old Covenant ended. The inheritance is in Christ.
  6. There is no remaining command to restore an ethnic kingdom or rebuild a temple. The Temple of His body has already been raised.
  7. Prophetic charts that map current events onto Daniel or Revelation treat the text as a newspaper of the future rather than the proclamation of what has already been accomplished.
  8. The kingdom is not awaiting a future political installation. Believers already sit with Christ in the heavenly places.
  9. Any teaching that keeps the body in a holding pattern until ethnic Israel is restored or a temple is rebuilt denies the finality of the cross and the judgment of 70 AD.
  10. The finished work means there is no remaining debt to be paid by political loyalty to a particular state or by support for a future national restoration.

On the priesthood of all believers and the present reality

  1. Every believer is a priest. The Old Covenant priesthood ended when its Temple fell.
  2. No human office mediates forgiveness, access, or the benefits of the New Covenant. Christ alone is Mediator.
  3. Assemblies that require a pastor to “lead” the Supper or to pronounce the word of forgiveness in a manner that implies mediation have not yet received the full implication of the priesthood of all believers.
  4. The pastoral office, when described in terms drawn from Old Covenant priesthood or prophetic office, confuses service with mediation.
  5. Celebrity status and multi-site empires that center a single personality exceed anything the New Testament assigns to elders.
  6. When the institution protects the office rather than the body, the same dynamic long criticized in Rome continues under a different name.
  7. The treasures of the Church are not confessions, charts, or hierarchical structures; they are the present reality of union with Christ.
  8. Believers do not need a professional class to interpret Scripture before they may walk in the New Creation. The Spirit is given to all who believe.
  9. The call to repentance is the call to leave the old identity — whether papal, pastoral, ethnic, or national — and to live as the one new man.
  10. Churches that major on future tribulation, rapture timing, and Middle East prophecy while neglecting the present priesthood of every believer have inverted the apostolic emphasis.

On the Eighth Day and the unfinished work

  1. The Eighth Day is the New Creation environment that does not return to the seven-day cycle of shadows, performance, genealogy, and temporary provision.
  2. Jesus rose on the first day of the week, the eighth day of new beginning. The old order finished; the new beginning opened in the Person who is raised.
  3. The Reformation recovered justification by faith and the authority of Scripture, yet it did not fully leave the structures of mediating authority and ethnic category that the Eighth Day abolishes.
  4. What was unfinished in the sixteenth century remains unfinished wherever the body still lives under the logic of the old order.
  5. Alignment with the Eighth Day in 2026 means recognizing that the Old Covenant age has ended, the dividing wall is gone, and every believer is a priest with direct access through Christ.
  6. It means refusing to reintroduce a mediating class under the name of pastor, confession, or denomination.
  7. It means refusing to treat ethnic or territorial categories as still central to God’s plan.
  8. It means living from the finished work rather than waiting for a future kingdom, temple, or restoration.
  9. Physical mortality remains part of created human life; what was abolished is the covenantal alienation and condemnation of the old order.
  10. The kingdom has come. Christ is all and in all. Anything that requires a pope, a pastor-mediator, an ethnic restoration, or a future temple to complete it is still operating from the old order, not the Eighth Day reality.

Final propositions

  1. The Reformation was a necessary break with the medieval system, yet it retained enough of the old logic that further reformation is still required.
  2. Further reformation in 2026 is the recognition that the Old Covenant ended in 70 AD and that the New Creation stands.
  3. Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes; the Old Covenant administration, with its priesthood, temple, and ethnic markers, has been superseded.
  4. The one new man does not require a restored national Israel to complete the story; the story is complete in the risen Christ and the people joined to Him from every nation.
  5. The destruction of the Temple ended the sacrificial system; no future temple is required, and animal sacrifices will not be restored.
  6. Believers already sit with Christ in the heavenly places; the kingdom is not awaiting a future political installation.
  7. Pastors who teach that ordinary believers must wait for their interpretation or for future events before they can walk in the New Creation keep people in tutelage.
  8. The finished work means there is no remaining debt to be paid by pilgrimage, penance, confessional subscription, or political loyalty to a particular state.
  9. Dispensational charts that map current events onto Daniel or Revelation treat the text as a newspaper of the future rather than the proclamation of what has already been accomplished.
  10. The treasures of Christ are open to every believer without human gatekeepers.
  11. Let those who claim the Reformation examine whether they have merely installed a lighter version of the same mediating structures.
  12. Let those who still wait for ethnic restoration or a rebuilt temple examine whether they have received the finality of the cross and the judgment of 70 AD.
  13. The call is not to a new denomination or a new confession. The call is to live as the one new man in the present New Creation.
  14. In the Eighth Day there is no mediating priesthood, no ethnic boundary, and no future temple. There is Christ, and those joined to Him.
  15. Anything that requires a pope, a pastor-mediator, an ethnic restoration, or the sword to complete it is still operating from the old order.
  16. The kingdom has come. The old covenant age has ended. Christ is all and in all.
  17. The Reformation remains unfinished until the body fully leaves the structures the Eighth Day has already abolished.
  18. In 2026 the unfinished work is the same as it was in the first century after 70 AD: to live from the finished reality rather than from the shadows that have passed.
  19. Out of love for the truth, these propositions are set forth so that the body may examine whether it is still living under the logic of the old order or has entered the Eighth Day.
  20. The treasures are open. The wall is gone. The Mediator is one. The kingdom is present. The old age has ended. Let the Reformation finish what it began.
  21. The Eighth Day is not a future age. It is the present reality that began when the old house fell.
  22. Those who still organize the body around ethnic identity have not yet received the one new man.
  23. Those who still require an ordained mediator for access have not yet received the priesthood of all believers.
  24. Those who still wait for a future kingdom have not yet received the present reign of Christ.
  25. Those who still treat confessions as binding on conscience have not yet received the direct access of the New Covenant.
  26. The unfinished Reformation is the gap between what Christ finished and what the body continues to practice.
  27. Closing that gap does not require a new institution. It requires the body to stop living as though the Old Covenant still stands.
  28. In 2026 the call remains the same as it was after 70 AD: come out of the old order and live in the New Creation.
  29. Christ is the substance of every shadow the Reformation still partly retains.
  30. The dividing wall is gone. Any system that rebuilds it in the name of theology has not yet entered the Eighth Day.
  31. Every believer is a priest. Any system that re-creates a mediating class has not yet received the finished work.
  32. The kingdom is present. Any system that keeps the body waiting for a future installation has not yet received the judgment of 70 AD.
  33. The Mediator is one. Any system that inserts a human intermediary has not yet left the old order.
  34. The inheritance is in Christ. Any system that still ties it to land or ethnicity has not yet received the New Creation.
  35. The sacrificial system ended. Any system that looks for a restored temple has not yet received the once-for-all offering.
  36. The Reformation recovered the gospel of justification. It has not yet fully recovered the reality of the one new man and the priesthood of all believers.
  37. Further reformation is not a return to the sixteenth century. It is the completion of what the sixteenth century only began.
  38. In the Eighth Day the body does not need a lighter version of the old system. It needs to leave the old system entirely.
  39. The unfinished work is not the addition of more structure. It is the removal of what the cross and 70 AD already removed.
  40. Let the body examine itself in 2026 by this measure: is it living from the finished reality or still organizing around the shadows?
  41. The treasures are open. The wall is gone. The Mediator is one. The kingdom is present. The old age has ended.
  42. Let the Reformation finish what it began by aligning fully with the Eighth Day reality already established in Christ.
  43. The body does not need a new hierarchy. It needs to leave the old one.
  44. The body does not need a future restoration. It needs to receive the present one.
  45. The body does not need a mediating office. It needs to receive the priesthood already given.
  46. The body does not need a new confession. It needs to live from the finished work.
  47. In 2026 the measure is simple: has the body left the structures the Eighth Day already abolished?
