Ninety-Five Theses Against the Unfinished Reformation

Directed at the systems that exalt pastors, confessions, and ethnic categories in the place of Christ, and that have continued the patterns of coercion, execution, and war under Protestant banners. Revised from the fulfilled New Creation reality: the Old Covenant age ended with the judgment of 70 AD; the dividing wall is gone; one new man exists in Christ; all believers are priests.

Out of love for the truth and the desire to bring it into the open, the following propositions are set forth for examination. They concern the unfinished work of the Reformation, the exaltation of human leaders in place of the one Mediator, the historical and ongoing violence committed under Protestant names, and the specific modern system of Dispensationalism that has shaped American policy while preserving Old Covenant categories the New Creation abolished.

In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.

On the New Creation and the end of the Old Covenant order

  1. When our Lord and Master Jesus Christ said the kingdom of God is at hand, He meant the decisive transition from the Old Covenant age to the New Creation reality, not an indefinitely postponed future program.
  2. The destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple in 70 AD was the judgment on that generation and the public end of the Old Covenant administration, not a temporary setback awaiting restoration.
  3. In Christ the dividing wall of hostility has been broken down; there is one new man, not two peoples with parallel destinies.
  4. The priesthood of the Old Covenant ended; every believer is a priest. No human office re-creates a mediating class.
  5. Pastors, elders, and teachers are servants who declare what is already true in Christ; they do not occupy a higher spiritual estate than the body.
  6. Any system that treats the pastor’s word, a confession, or a denominational standard as functionally binding on conscience has reintroduced a magisterium under a different name.
  7. The Reformation replaced the Pope with pastors, synods, and confessions while retaining the logic that some men stand between the people and the Word.
  8. Protestant churches still operate as if the local assembly requires a specially ordained mediator to administer Word and sacrament, contrary to the priesthood of all believers.
  9. Celebrity pastors, multi-site empires, and hierarchical denominations function as local or national popes in practice, even when they reject the title.
  10. The claim that “sola scriptura” is practiced while members are expected to submit to the pastor’s interpretation or the denomination’s confession is a distinction without a difference.

On Protestant violence and the unfinished break with coercion

  1. Luther and the magisterial Reformers endorsed the execution of Anabaptists. Felix Manz was drowned in Zurich in 1527 with Zwingli’s approval; thousands more followed by sword, fire, and water across Protestant territories.
  2. Estimates place Anabaptist executions in the thousands during the Reformation era. Both Catholic and Protestant authorities treated re-baptism and refusal of the sword as capital offenses.
  3. John Calvin’s Geneva burned Michael Servetus in 1553. Other Reformers, including Melanchthon, approved. The principle of executing heretics was not abandoned; only the target changed.
  4. Protestant state churches in Saxony, Geneva, England, and elsewhere banished, imprisoned, or killed dissenters. The sword remained the enforcer of orthodoxy.
  5. The Wars of Religion (French Wars, Dutch Revolt, Thirty Years’ War) killed millions. Protestant princes and cities fought under the banner of the Gospel while practicing the same coercion they had condemned in Rome.
  6. The Peace of Westphalia (1648) ended open religious war by partitioning Europe under the principle cuius regio, eius religio, not by establishing the freedom of conscience the New Creation implies.
  7. Protestant nations continued to use force against religious minorities long after the Reformation. The logic that the magistrate must enforce true religion survived the break with Rome.
  8. Those who claim the Reformation freed the conscience while preserving the right of the Christian magistrate to punish heresy have not escaped the system they opposed.
  9. The Anabaptist insistence that true Christians do not wield the sword against other believers was rejected by the magisterial Reformers; that rejection reveals the incomplete nature of the break.
  10. Modern Protestantism has not publicly repudiated this history as a betrayal of the New Creation; it has largely treated it as an unfortunate chapter rather than a structural failure.

On Dispensationalism as the modern restoration of Old Covenant categories

  1. Dispensationalism, systematized by John Nelson Darby in the nineteenth century and popularized by the Scofield Reference Bible (1909), reintroduced a permanent distinction between Israel and the Church.
  2. The Scofield notes taught a future restoration of ethnic Israel, a rebuilt temple, a seven-year tribulation, and a pre-tribulation rapture. These notes shaped generations of American evangelicals.
  3. This system treats the land promises and ethnic identity as still operative in a way the New Testament does not, once the wall is removed and the New Creation has come.
  4. Dispensationalism therefore functions as a Protestant form of the old ethnic and territorial categories that the cross and 70 AD ended.
  5. Christian Zionism, heavily influenced by dispensational categories, treats modern political Israel as the prophetic fulfillment of covenant promises that were fulfilled in Christ and the remnant.
  6. Influential evangelical leaders have applied Genesis 12:3 to the modern state of Israel in a way that binds American policy to a particular political entity under threat of divine curse.
  7. This reading has contributed to consistent American evangelical support for Israeli policies, including settlements in contested territories, regardless of the human cost.
  8. Dispensational expectation of prophetic wars in the Middle East has provided theological cover for American military involvement framed as alignment with God’s end-times plan.
  9. Reports from within the U.S. military have documented commanders invoking “end times” language in connection with operations against Iran and other regional actors.
  10. Mike Huckabee, a Baptist minister and prominent Christian Zionist, was appointed U.S. ambassador to Israel; his public theology treats support for the state as a biblical mandate.
  11. The influence is not universal among Protestants, but it is concentrated and politically effective within large segments of American evangelicalism that shape Republican foreign policy.
  12. Dispensationalism keeps the Church waiting for a future kingdom while the New Testament declares the kingdom present and the old age judged.
  13. By preserving a future for ethnic Israel distinct from the one new man, it denies the finality of what Christ accomplished and what 70 AD confirmed.
  14. The system requires ongoing prophetic speculation (Gog and Magog, rebuilt temple, Antichrist) that the fulfilled reading of the prophets and Revelation renders unnecessary.
  15. American foreign policy in the region has repeatedly been shaped by a mixture of strategic interest and this theological overlay; the theological component is not incidental.

On the practical exaltation of pastors and the persistence of religion

  1. Protestant pastors regularly function as the primary interpreters whose view settles disputes; dissent is treated as rebellion against God rather than disagreement among brothers.
  2. The language of “spiritual covering,” “God’s man,” and “touch not the Lord’s anointed” re-creates the untouchable status once claimed by popes and bishops.
  3. Mega-church and multi-site models concentrate authority in a single personality or brand; the body is reduced to an audience.
  4. Denominational hierarchies (presbyteries, synods, episcopal structures retained in some Protestant bodies) exercise discipline that binds conscience beyond the local assembly.
  5. The requirement that ministers be seminary-trained and formally ordained creates a professional class that ordinary believers are expected to defer to on doctrine.
  6. Confessions and statements of faith, while useful for clarity, become functional creeds that define the boundaries of fellowship more strictly than the apostles’ teaching on the one new man.
  7. Churches that claim the priesthood of all believers still require a pastor to “lead” the Lord’s Supper or to pronounce the word of forgiveness in a manner that implies mediation.
  8. The pastoral office is often described in terms drawn from Old Covenant priesthood or prophetic office rather than the servant pattern of the New Testament.
  9. Celebrity pastors who command large followings and political access operate with an influence that exceeds anything the New Testament assigns to elders.
  10. When a pastor’s public error or moral failure is shielded by the institution, the system protects the office rather than the body—the same dynamic long criticized in Rome.
  11. Protestantism’s rejection of the Pope has not produced consistent rejection of human authority standing over the conscience of the believer.
  12. The result is a lighter version of the same hierarchical religion: fewer relics and indulgences, the same logic of mediated access and enforced conformity.
  13. True freedom in the New Creation is the direct access every believer has through Christ alone; any structure that inserts an obligatory human intermediary has not yet left the old order.

On war, coercion, and the misuse of the name of Jesus

  1. Protestant nations have participated in wars of conquest, colonial violence, and modern conflicts while invoking Christian civilization or biblical mandate.
  2. The Thirty Years’ War alone produced death on a scale that dwarfed many later conflicts; it was fought in significant part over which version of Christian orthodoxy would prevail.
  3. Later Protestant-majority powers continued to use force against indigenous peoples, religious minorities, and political opponents under various justifications that mixed theology with national interest.
  4. In the modern era, dispensational influence has aligned large numbers of American evangelicals with policies that include military aid, arms transfers, and operations whose civilian costs are treated as secondary to prophetic necessity.
  5. The claim that these actions are required by Scripture because of Israel’s role in a future tribulation is a theological judgment, not a plain reading of the New Testament’s present reality.
  6. When pastors and evangelical leaders publicly bless wars or territorial expansions as fulfillment of prophecy, they place the name of Jesus on political and military decisions.
  7. The New Testament contains no command to restore an ethnic kingdom, rebuild a temple, or align national policy with a particular Middle Eastern state as a condition of blessing.
  8. Support for policies that produce large-scale civilian death, displacement, or collective punishment cannot be sanitized by prophetic charts.
  9. The same churches that condemn Catholic crusades often remain silent when Protestant-influenced policy produces comparable suffering under different theological language.
  10. Religion that requires the sword to protect its version of truth has not yet entered the Eighth Day reality in which the wall is gone and the kingdom is present.
  11. The Anabaptist witness that the followers of Christ do not kill for the sake of the faith was suppressed by force; that suppression remains a standing indictment of the magisterial Reformation.
  12. Modern dispensational politics continues the pattern by treating geopolitical outcomes as necessary for God’s plan, thereby justifying force in the name of Jesus.

On the finished work and the present reality

  1. Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes; the Old Covenant administration, with its priesthood, temple, and ethnic markers, has been superseded.
  2. The one new man does not require a restored national Israel to complete the story; the story is complete in the risen Christ and the people joined to Him from every nation.
  3. Physical mortality remains part of created human life; what was abolished is the covenantal alienation and condemnation of the old order.
  4. Believers already sit with Christ in the heavenly places; the kingdom is not awaiting a future political installation.
  5. Any system that keeps the Church in a holding pattern until ethnic Israel is restored or a temple is rebuilt denies the finality of the cross and the judgment of 70 AD.
  6. The treasures of the Church are not indulgences, confessions, or prophetic charts; they are the present reality of union with Christ.
  7. Pastors who teach that ordinary believers must wait for their interpretation or for future events before they can walk in the New Creation keep people in tutelage.
  8. The call to repentance is the call to leave the old identity—whether papal, pastoral, ethnic, or national—and to live as the one new man.
  9. Churches that major on future tribulation, rapture timing, and Middle East prophecy while neglecting the present priesthood of every believer have inverted the apostolic emphasis.
  10. The finished work means there is no remaining debt to be paid by pilgrimage, penance, military support, or political loyalty to a particular state.
  11. Dispensational charts that map current events onto Daniel or Revelation treat the text as a newspaper of the future rather than the proclamation of what has already been accomplished.
  12. The destruction of the Temple ended the sacrificial system; no future temple is required, and animal sacrifices will not be restored.
  13. Those who labor to prepare conditions for a third temple or a restored priesthood are working against the finality of Christ’s offering.
  14. American evangelical political engagement that treats support for Israel as a test of orthodoxy has made a political alliance into a doctrinal boundary.
  15. The New Testament knows no such test; the boundary is faith in Christ, not alignment with a modern nation-state.

Final propositions

  1. Protestants executed Anabaptists by drowning and burning while claiming to restore the Gospel; the contradiction remains.
  2. Protestants burned Servetus and approved the act; the principle of capital punishment for doctrine was retained.
  3. Protestants fought wars that killed millions over which confession would rule; the sword was not laid down.
  4. Dispensationalism revived the ethnic and territorial categories the New Creation ended and exported them into American foreign policy.
  5. Evangelical leaders have invoked biblical blessing and curse language to support military and territorial policies whose human cost is measured in civilian deaths and displacement.
  6. The exaltation of pastors as the necessary interpreters and leaders recreates, in lighter form, the hierarchy the Reformers attacked.
  7. True reformation is not the replacement of one set of human authorities with another; it is the recognition that Christ alone is Head and every believer is a priest.
  8. The Eighth Day reality is the present possession of those in Christ; it is not postponed until a future millennium or a restored Israel.
  9. Religion that requires ongoing mediators, future prophetic fulfillments tied to ethnicity, and the sword to protect its version of truth has not yet left the old order.
  10. The one new man does not need a Christian nation, a restored temple, or a political Israel to complete God’s purpose; the purpose is already realized in the body of Christ.
  11. Those who continue to bind conscience to pastors, confessions, or dispensational charts keep people under tutors when the Son has made them free.
  12. The historical record of Protestant executions and religious wars demonstrates that the break with Rome was incomplete on the question of coercion.
  13. The modern record of dispensational influence on policy demonstrates that Old Covenant categories, once revived, again justify force.
  14. Reject the religion that exalts the pastor in the place of the Pope; the New Creation has no such office.
  15. Abandon the system that treats ethnic Israel as still central to God’s plan; the wall is gone.
  16. Discard the expectation that future wars and a rebuilt temple are required; the judgment has already fallen and the offering is complete.
  17. The treasures of Christ are open to every believer without human gatekeepers.
  18. Let those who claim the Reformation examine whether they have merely installed a lighter version of the same hierarchical religion.
  19. Let those who invoke the name of Jesus over modern conflicts examine whether they are proclaiming the finished New Creation or defending an unfinished system.
  20. The kingdom has come. The old covenant age has ended. Christ is all and in all. Anything that requires a pope, a pastor-mediator, an ethnic restoration, or the sword to complete it is still religion, not the New Creation reality.
